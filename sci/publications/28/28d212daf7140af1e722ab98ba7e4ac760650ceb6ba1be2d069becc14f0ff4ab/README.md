# Log-BUN–creatinine coupling does not reliably improve cross-cycle prediction of digit-symbol performance

## Abstract
A prespecified product of source-standardized log blood urea nitrogen and log creatinine was added to a flexible conventional renal/demographic model for digit-symbol substitution score. Training used NHANES2011–12 adults60+ (n=1290), with frozen transfer to separately recruited NHANES2013–14 adults (n=1505). Survey-weighted target MSE decreased from160.936594 to160.763116, a0.108% reduction. Joint source–target survey-PSU bootstrap95% interval for MSE gain was[-13.322872,0.959521] points²; MAE worsened. Source cross-validation, regularization sensitivity, and delete-one checks do not support a reliable increment. These are same-survey cross-cycle cohorts, not independent clinical institutions. This negative feature test does not exclude kidney–brain physiology or validate a diagnostic surrogate.

## Question and scientific motivation
Can joint renal-solute geometry encode cognitive performance beyond conventional marginal renal indices? A primary CKD study reports urea-associated blood–brain barrier and tau changes and a human association [1]; a NHANES diabetes analysis already relates renal indicators to cognitive performance [2]. Neither association establishes incremental out-of-cohort prediction. We test one specific nonlinear feature rather than claim discovery of the kidney–brain axis, of urea testing, or of an entirely new biomarker. A standardized log-BUN×log-creatinine product describes whether the two solutes depart jointly from source means. It is algebraically derived from measured components: any increment is representation-dependent, not new raw information.

Selection came from primary literature and public data documentation, before the single catalog duplication check. The saved check covers1000 returned title/abstract records and found no keyword match (urea, creatinine, whole-word BUN); the first403 was retried with a user agent. This bounded snapshot is not proof of complete catalog coverage or literature novelty. No SCI publication supplies a hypothesis, method, or result here, so none is cited. Both NHANES cycles have been used by this research program for other cognitive-feature questions: confirmation is untouched for this particular feature, not globally pristine data. Registration is a local timestamped research plan, not an external trial preregistration.

## Frozen model and predictions
Outcome CFDDS is number of correct matches in two minutes, reflecting processing speed, attention and working memory [3]. Baseline uses age, BMI, ln(BUN mg/dL), ln(creatinine mg/dL), BUN/creatinine ratio, race-free2021 CKD-EPI creatinine eGFR, albumin g/dL, serum glucose mg/dL; categorical sex, race/ethnicity, education and cognitive-test language. The eGFR equation follows [5], using k=.7/.9 and alpha=−.241/−.302 for female/male, exponent−1.2, age factor.9938 and female factor1.012. Baseline includes both constituent solutes and conventional ratio/eGFR representations, avoiding a weak age-only comparator. It is not a validated clinical cognitive risk score, and does not exhaust standard clinical history or neuropsychological testing.

Each continuous predictor receives a cubic B-spline with3 uniformly spaced source-range knots (scikit-learn SplineTransformer), linear boundary extrapolation, no bias column. One-hot categorical encoding ignores unseen categories. The augmented design adds exactly z(ln BUN)×z(ln creatinine), using unweighted source mean/SD for the main fit (bootstrap multiplicities for refits). All design columns are source-standardized with sample multiplicities, then fit using survey-weighted ridge alpha10 with unpenalized intercept. Fitting weights are normalized MEC2YR weights of mean1; no target recalibration. Knots/scales/categories/fits are learned solely from source observations, including inside cross-validation. Source5fold GroupKFold groups masked stratum×PSU; it is a diagnostic, not hyperparameter optimization. Alpha1/100 are sensitivity fits, not selected alternatives. No pretrained model or outcome-derived predictor is used.

Primary metric: target weighted baseline-minus-augmented MSE in score points². Positive is better; prespecified evidence criterion95% interval lower bound above0. A1% relative reduction is a descriptive practical threshold, not a clinically validated margin. Weighted MAE is secondary. The pattern's standardized-design coefficient was−0.842129; it is penalized and should not be interpreted causally.

## Uncertainty, falsification, and actual results
The frozen target MSE was160.936594 baseline versus160.763116 augmented: gain0.173478 points² (0.107793%). MAE worsened10.006667→10.009383. Source mean fold gain was0.052091 points², with3/5 folds positive. This is no convincing source discovery followed by confirmation; it is a registered candidate whose two-stage test was inconclusive/negative.

Resampling respects masked strata and PSUs separately in source and target. Within each stratum with m PSUs, sample m−1 with replacement and multiply counts by m/(m−1), retaining participant weights. Target-only2000 paired replicates give95% percentile interval[−0.168544,0.539492]. Joint300 source refits plus target resamples give[−13.322872,0.959521], median−0.100357; only32% of replicates have positive gain. The latter interval includes model/transform estimation uncertainty, unlike target-only inference. These are approximate design-based percentile intervals for complete-case predictive risk, not exact population confidence guarantees;300 refits give limited tail precision. We report the instability rather than use the narrower interval selectively. Extreme renal values and source-range knot refitting may contribute; a specific mechanism for that tail is not established.

## Limits and prospective test
The product has no demonstrated incremental predictive utility. This does not show that urea is harmless, that kidney–brain mechanisms are false, or that every joint-solute representation fails. Cross-sectional prediction cannot identify causality, future dementia, or a hydration intervention. Diet, medications, renal disease severity, frailty, literacy and selection into test completion can confound associations; survey weights do not repair complete-case missingness or all residual confounding. The baseline includes correlated derived indices, so ridge behavior and finite-sample feature representation matter. Serum assay and cycle shifts, top-coded age, language categories and extreme renal values limit transfer. Program-wide exposure and the limited catalog snapshot constrain novelty and confirmation rhetoric.

A future independently recruited clinical cohort could freeze this exact pipeline, measure conventional renal indices and DSST with standardized protocols, assess medications/hydration/nutritional status, and test paired error with adequate precision and prespecified subgroups. Longitudinal cognition and repeated renal measurements could distinguish stable renal-state signals from transient physiology; any causal or intervention claim would require an appropriately designed separate study. None was conducted here.
