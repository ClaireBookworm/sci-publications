# AST–ALT coupling adds no established CRP prediction benefit beyond nonlinear enzyme levels and their ratio

## Central result and authorship
In NHANES2007–2008 (source n=4707) and separately sampled2009–2010 (target n=5028), adding standardized log-AST×log-ALT to a nonlinear standard-marker baseline reduced target log-CRP MSE by 0.0001956 (0.017%;95% joint source-refit/target PSU bootstrap interval -0.0033731 to 0.0012007). This does not establish improvement. A post-primary algebraic self-check showed that the proposed term is already in the span of additive cubic splines of log-AST, log-ALT and log(AST/ALT): 2ab=a²+b²−(a−b)². On5027 target records within source enzyme/ratio support, unpenalized predictions agreed within2.1e−13. The tiny ridge gain reflects parameterization/penalty, not an added model dimension. This negative predictive test does not refute liver–inflammation biology or establish clinical equivalence. Transfer is between survey cross-sections, not independent external cohorts. ## Motivation, question and registration
Primary population studies establish routine aminotransferase abnormalities and links with metabolic/inflammatory factors. [Fraser et al., PMID18054554](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18054554/) studied ALT in adolescents; its reported CRP association motivates a question, not direct evidence for adults or a new biomarker. [Ioannou et al., PMID16405537](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16405537/) studied adult ALT/AST abnormalities and metabolic risk. Our narrower question is whether continuous enzyme *coupling* improves transported prediction of a measured inflammatory state after accounting for nonlinear levels, adiposity, renal/albumin markers and the conventional AST/ALT ratio. We do not assert that aminotransferase biology, interaction analysis, or ratio analyses are novel or universally unused.

## Frozen outcome, features and statistical estimand
Outcome is natural log LBXCRP (mg/dL), a concurrent inflammatory-state measure, not future disease. Public below-detection substitutions are retained; no censoring model is fitted. CRP documentation reports latex-enhanced nephelometry. BIOPRO documentation describes enzymatic-rate ALT/AST assays on Beckman Synchron instruments, and creatinine standardization notes; released values are used without further calibration. No cross-platform generality is claimed.

Baseline: cubic B-splines (3 equally spaced knots, no bias, linear extrapolation) separately for age, BMI, waist, log-AST, log-ALT, log(AST/ALT), log-creatinine and albumin; one-hot sex, race/ethnicity, ever/current smoking and exam season. AST=LBXSASSI and ALT=LBXSATSI, both U/L; creatinine mg/dL; albumin g/dL; waist cm; BMI kg/m². Including the ratio gives a more relevant standard-marker comparator than omitting an established enzyme summary. This is a transparent predictive comparator, not an established clinical CRP score. The augmented design adds just one product of source-MEC-weight-standardized log-AST and log-ALT. Source weights fit all scalers; spline bounds and categories fit only source. All columns standardized using source weights. Ridge alpha1 with unpenalized intercept fits weighted squared loss plus alpha×squared coefficient norm. Weights WTMEC2YR are normalized to mean1. Fold and bootstrap preprocessing is refitted only within the source training records. Target labels never choose model features, penalty, knots or thresholds.

Estimand: target MEC-weighted MSE(baseline)−MSE(augmented) in squared natural-log units. Positive favors augmentation. The registered prediction requires a95% interval entirely above0. Five-fold source CV keeps each masked stratum–PSU pair together. Uncertainty:300 independent source/target stratified rescaled PSU bootstrap draws. Within each stratum with m observed PSUs, sample m−1 PSUs with replacement and multiply inclusion counts by m/(m−1); refit source models then evaluate both on the same target resample. Percentile interval is approximate, conditional on the released masked design and complete cases, with finite-bootstrap Monte Carlo uncertainty. Seed181913. It is not a causal effect interval. Examination weights rather than fasting-subsample weights are appropriate because all markers here are from MEC components.

## Algebraic falsification: no new model dimension
Write a=log(AST), b=log(ALT), r=a−b=log(AST/ALT). Then

**ab = (a²+b²−r²)/2.**

The product of centered/scaled a and b differs from ab only by linear terms and a constant. Additive cubic B-splines in a, b and r span those quadratics and linear terms within their fitted bounds. Consequently the proposed column is already in the baseline span on source support; it need not add functional flexibility. This was initially overlooked, discovered during author self-check, and retained as an informative negative result rather than recast as biomarker discovery.

Numerical least-squares projection of the final standardized added column onto intercept+baseline has maximum source residual 4.97e-14. On all5028 target records residual is 0.00407974; one target record falls outside source log-enzyme/ratio bounds, where linear extrapolation breaks the exact identity. On5027 in-support target records, projection residual is 1.37e-13. Weighted unpenalized least squares using SVD cutoff1e−10 yields maximum paired prediction difference 2.07e-13, MSE gain 8.88e-16; full-target OLS gain -2.36e-07. Other covariate extrapolation can occur but the exact product representation uses enzyme/ratio columns only.

Ridge is not invariant to redundant feature augmentation: a duplicate direction changes the effective penalty, so the small ridge difference is a parameterization/regularization effect, apart from target extrapolation. This is not evidence for information absent from the baseline model's span. The theorem does **not** imply all physiological interactions are redundant; it specifically applies when a,b,a−b quadratics are already represented.

## Scope, limitations and future tests
This is a narrow negative computational result, not a new biomarker, causal mechanism, clinical recommendation or refutation of the liver–inflammation association. No disease outcomes or treatment utility were tested. Residual confounding by alcohol, viral hepatitis, medication, physical activity and unmeasured conditions remains; the models predict rather than identify causal pathways. Albumin may itself index inflammation. Assay floors, complete-case selection and shared survey methods limit inference. The small gain, wide interval, sensitivity reversals and algebraic redundancy preclude an improved-health-prediction claim. We do not claim true equivalence merely because an interval crosses zero.

A future preregistered clinical observational study could compare truly nonredundant two-dimensional enzyme surfaces against a sufficiently flexible ratio-adjusted standard-marker model in a separate health system, with repeated enzyme/CRP measures, alcohol/hepatitis/medication ascertainment and prospective outcomes. It should lock preprocessing, clinically meaningful error margins and calibration tests before external evaluation. Such a study has not been performed here. No intervention is needed to assess predictive transport, and any mechanistic claim would require a separately approved design.
