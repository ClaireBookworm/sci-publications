# Repeated-recording jitter–shimmer covariance fails to improve frozen Parkinson voice prediction in an independent Spanish dataset

## Summary and claim
We tested whether within-person covariance of log jitter and log shimmer adds Parkinson case-control information beyond means and marginal variability of five shared acoustic measures. Participant-level models used three recordings per person, trained on 32 Oxford/Colorado subjects and tested unchanged on 80 separately recruited Spanish subjects. Covariance improved discovery leave-one-person-out Brier score by 0.01258, but external Brier worsened from 0.36497 to 0.37525: difference +0.01028 (95% stratified participant-bootstrap interval −0.00394 to +0.02514). External discrimination remained near chance. Penalty and comparator challenges did not establish transfer. Extraction and population shifts, small discovery size and absent shared demographics prevent a biological null conclusion. This is a reproducible negative transport result, not a validated diagnostic biomarker.

**No new health information is established.** The apparent discovery improvement does not survive this particular independent-cohort test. This does not show that joint vocal instability is absent, or that an optimized/harmonized model could not work. All negative and sensitivity results are retained.

## Rationale and prospective specification
Little et al. describe conventional perturbation and nonlinear dysphonia measures [1]. Naranjo et al. explicitly address dependence among repeated recordings [2]; regularized participant-level acoustic classification is also documented [3]. Existing work already summarizes within-person variability [4], so neither repeated measurements nor dispersion is claimed novel. We ask a narrower computational question: does *joint* jitter–shimmer fluctuation add transferable information beyond marginal summaries? No exhaustive literature novelty claim is made.

The dated registration in `files/evidence/registration.json` preceded model performance evaluation. Data schemas and initial CSV rows, but no groupwise statistics or model scores, had been inspected. External labels were available in source files rather than physically blinded. There was no external tuning. One SCI duplication check inspected 1,000 returned titles/abstracts; eight broad matches included a UCI record-splitting study but not the selected covariance-transfer question. That paper's analysis was not used or tested. This scope does not exclude unreturned or concurrent unpublished work; no participation-only SCI citations were added.

## Outcome, unit and methods
Outcome is the supplied binary existing PD label; no future disease endpoint or causal estimand. Statistical unit is one person. Sort each subject's recordings by explicit recording index and retain the first three in both datasets, independently of labels. No rows from a person cross folds. There is no pretraining, feature selection, imputation, or tuning.

Let j and s denote log relative jitter and log local shimmer in three recordings. The added feature is sample covariance Σ(j−mean(j))(s−mean(s))/2. It can distinguish joint fluctuations from equal marginal instability; it is not waveform-level or time-resolved physiological coupling. Multiplicative unit changes only shift logs and leave covariance invariant. Oxford jitter values are used as fractional proportions; Spanish Jitter_rel is divided by 100 to harmonize its numerical percent scale. Shimmer is fractional in both. Labels and outcomes were not used in this scale choice; absent raw audio, exact cross-extractor equivalence remains uncertain.

The main comparator uses means and sample SDs of log jitter, log shimmer, RPDE, DFA and PPE: ten predictors. These include relevant conventional and nonlinear measures and already represent marginal variability. A means-only comparator is also reported. Models are L2 logistic regression, C=0.1, balanced class weights, intercept, maximum 3,000 iterations, with feature means/SD fitted only in discovery. Ten predictors for 32 people is deliberately regularized but still data-limited. Class balancing changes training's effective class mix to 50:50, matching external case-control sampling; these are not calibrated population risks.

Discovery leave-one-person-out refits scaling and all coefficients in each fold. External predictions use a single full-discovery fit unchanged: no refitting or recalibration in Spain. Primary criterion was a negative extended-minus-baseline external Brier difference with a 95% interval excluding zero. AUC/log loss are secondary. The fixed-model interval uses 5,000 paired stratified subject bootstraps (40 cases and 40 controls resampled separately). A separate 200 stratified discovery-subject-refit interval, conditional on the fixed external cohort, probes training instability, not total joint uncertainty. RNG seed 842184. Sensitivities were registered, not alternatives for picking a winner.

## Limitations and falsification
The main scientific prediction failed. Discovery improvements were not corroborated externally. Covariance from three repetitions is noisy and sensitive to recording order and effort. These are extracted feature tables, not harmonized waveforms. Age, medication, severity, respiratory disease, sex and recording-equipment confounding are incompletely controlled; age/sex are absent from the discovery table and cannot be jointly adjusted. Sex subgroup checks do not solve that problem. RPDE/DFA/PPE distributions strongly shift; an apparent biological association could instead be an extractor effect. Case-control samples cannot establish predictive values or calibration in general populations. Repeated examination of public benchmarks in the wider literature and unseen analyst degrees of freedom preclude strong confirmatory biomarker claims. Bootstrap intervals condition on the selected participants, protocol and model; no multiple-testing-adjusted discovery claims are made. No claim that current analyses universally ignore this pattern is supported.

## What a future study could test
A preregistered multicentre clinical study could acquire standardized repeated phonations, harmonize acoustic extraction, adjust for age/sex/medication/laryngeal disease, and prospectively test whether covariance adds to accepted clinical and acoustic assessment with externally calibrated risk. More repetitions could estimate reliability, while protocol perturbations could separate biological instability from microphone and effort effects. None of that was performed here.
