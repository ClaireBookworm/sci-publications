# Presenting symptoms and provoked angina: an uncertain interaction across two angiography cohorts

## Abstract
Across independently collected Cleveland and Hungarian angiography cohorts (303 source,293 target), adding the conjunction of asymptomatic presenting chest-pain category and exercise-induced angina to flexible additive predictors changed target Brier error by -0.002922 (joint bootstrap95% interval [-0.011469,0.006166]); incremental benefit was not established. Baseline versus augmented AUC was 0.860408 versus 0.859197. Modest mean Brier improvement did not meet the registered uncertainty criterion; reverse transport and female-only refitting showed deterioration. This is a negative/uncertain cross-center incremental-prediction result, not evidence of a new clinical biomarker.

## Literature-led question and interpretation
Presenting symptoms and exercise-provoked symptoms describe different contexts. Primary studies examined anginal chest pain during exercise [1] and documented painless exercise ischemia among patients with coronary disease [2]. Motivated by these studies and available UCI data [3], we test whether the conjunction `I(cp=4) * I(exang=1)` predicts angiographic disease beyond those predictors separately. No general novelty of angina, interaction models, or symptom interpretation is claimed; the contribution is a prespecified, reproducible cross-center test against flexible marginal predictors with source-sample uncertainty. We cannot show that all current analyses ignore this information. `cp=4` means the release's asymptomatic presenting category, NOT absence of all symptoms, proof of silent ischemia, or painless exercise. The conjunction actually combines an asymptomatic presentation with **pain during exercise**; it must not be called silent ischemia. Neither underlying pain thresholds nor mechanisms are measured.

All columns standardized using source means/SD. Both models use sklearn logistic L2, C=1,lbfgs,max_iter2000,tolerance1e-9,unpenalized intercept. Augmented model adds only the binary conjunction before standardization. No target preprocessing, tuning, recalibration or outcome-derived predictors. Disease-associated angiographic vessel count and thallium excluded from both models to avoid invasive/concurrent diagnostic information and severe missingness.

This baseline includes standard clinical and available exercise measures, including each component of the proposed pattern. It is not a validated clinical risk score. A linear continuous-predictor comparator challenges the spline baseline; no superiority over complete clinical workups, Duke treadmill score (duration absent), imaging or modern diagnostic pathways is claimed. ## Limitations and future test
Small source cohort, few women, coarse symptoms, unmeasured medications/workload, verification/referral selection, endpoint-era mismatch, missingness and prevalence shift preclude clinical claims. The comparison is conditional on this fixed model and two historical centers. Much of the main signal is already represented by standard separate symptoms/exercise measures. A weak interaction here cannot demonstrate hidden body-state information absent from all current practice. No claim of clinical utility, causal physiology, population calibration, equivalence or universally absent interaction. A future prospective, multicenter diagnostic study could preregister the same conjunction against a contemporary standard score, harmonize presenting and exercise symptoms, capture medications/workload and blinded disease adjudication, and assess calibration and decision utility with adequate source/target sample size. That study is proposed, not performed.
