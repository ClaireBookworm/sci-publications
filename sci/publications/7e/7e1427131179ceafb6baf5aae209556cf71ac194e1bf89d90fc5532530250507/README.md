# The RDW–MCV product adds no clear HbA1c prediction benefit beyond flexible routine measures across two NHANES cycles

## Abstract
We tested whether RDW-CV times mean corpuscular volume, a proxy for absolute erythrocyte volume spread, predicts measured HbA1c beyond fasting plasma glucose and flexible additive routine indices. A frozen weighted spline model was trained in 2,342 NHANES 2013–2014 adults and evaluated without refitting in 2,211 separately sampled 2015–2016 adults. Adding the product changed weighted mean squared error from 0.249048 to 0.248901 squared HbA1c percentage points (difference −0.000147). The paired rescaled-PSU-bootstrap 95% interval was −0.000507 to +0.000183; refitting both cycles with fixed preprocessing knots gave −0.000624 to +0.001276. Discovery-cycle grouped cross-validation worsened slightly. The apparent improvement was larger with a linear baseline and shrank with nonlinear adjustment. These data do not establish useful incremental information from the product, red-cell lifespan inference, improved glycemia measurement or clinical benefit.

## Question, rationale and scope
Red-cell turnover can change HbA1c independently of glucose exposure. Mechanistic work by Beltran del Rio and colleagues [1] and lifespan-adjusted modeling [2] motivate asking whether a neglected *combination* of routinely measured cell indices helps predict the measured assay. Our candidate is RDW-CV (%) × MCV (fL) / 100. Because RDW-CV is normalized to cell size, its product with size is a dispersion proxy. It is **not measured RDW-SD**, a histogram reconstruction, or a validated lifespan estimate. The product is deterministic: it cannot add information to fully observed RDW and MCV in an information-theoretic sense, but could help a restricted predictor represent their interaction. RDW, MCV and their relationship to HbA1c are not newly discovered. We do not claim that the interaction has never been used. This study contributes a tightly scoped negative cross-cycle predictive check, not a new biomarker.

The public-data question was registered before outcome analysis in `files/question.json`; a preanalysis amendment adopted CDC's documented glucose calibration and explicit fasting criteria. The single catalog duplication check was after question selection, not a source of hypotheses (`files/duplication-check.json`). It covered only the first 1,000 returned records and cannot exclude concurrent unpublished or older work. No SCI paper was used or tested, so none is cited.

## Inputs and statistical unit
Public CDC/NCHS NHANES 2013–2014 (H) is discovery/training, and 2015–2016 (I) is frozen confirmation. These are separately sampled cross-sectional datasets, **not two splits of one sample**. Each row is one adult. No SEQN occurs in both cycles. NHANES is not a longitudinal cohort; absent cross-cycle linkage, repeat enrollment of an unidentified individual cannot be definitively excluded. No reidentification or identifiable linkage was attempted. Shared national sampling framework and assay systems limit externality: this is independent-cycle, not independent-health-system or cross-country validation.

Use DEMO, BMX, CBC, GHB, GLU and FASTQX in each cycle, joined one-to-one on public SEQN. Retain age ≥20, not known pregnant (RIDEXPRG !=1), positive fasting-subsample WTSAF2YR, fasting 9–24 h (PHAFSTHR + PHAFSTMN/60), and complete model/outcome/design fields. Unknown pregnancy status is not assumed negative biologically; it is merely retained. Ages are public top-coded (maximum 80). Raw sample counts were 10,175/9,971; adult not-known-pregnant counts 5,704/5,649; fasting-positive-weight counts 2,411/2,317; final complete cases 2,342/2,211. Final female counts 1,214/1,136. Both cycles had 15 masked strata and 30 masked PSUs. Field-specific missingness is in `results.json`; complete-case exclusions can bias representativeness.

**Outcome:** LBXGH, measured HbA1c percentage, not a diagnosis, future disease or true mean glucose. **Standard comparator:** fasting plasma glucose (LBXGLU), age, sex, race/ethnicity, BMI, hemoglobin, MCV and RDW, with flexible additive effects. We neither infer causality nor correct patients' HbA1c.

GLU_I documents an instrument shift: primary confirmation glucose is mapped to the older scale by the published backward equation `0.9776 × LBXGLU + 0.4994`. This was fixed from documentation, not fitted using target outcomes. Assay bridging uncertainty is not propagated; an uncorrected-glucose sensitivity is supplied. CBC and glycohemoglobin methods and cycle-specific changes must be considered when interpreting transport [3,4].

## Frozen models and uncertainty
Weighted least squares minimizes squared error using each cycle's positive fasting weights; multiplying all weights by a constant does not change results. Continuous predictors age, BMI, glucose, hemoglobin, MCV and RDW each receive a degree-3 B-spline with four training-only knots at the 5th, 35th, 65th and 95th percentiles, linear extrapolation and no redundant bias column. Sex and RIDRETH3 enter fixed dummy coding (sex 1, race category 1 as references); intercept is included. Race/ethnicity is a recorded social classification and adjustment factor, not a biological explanation. The baseline has 37 full-rank columns. Augmentation adds the training-centered/scaled product as a single unpenalized linear term. There is no tuning, target-cycle recalibration, imputation or outcome-dependent feature selection. All target metrics were computed in one scripted pass using the frozen specification; subsequent sensitivity checks did not change it.

The primary prediction was a negative augmented-minus-baseline weighted MSE with a 95% interval wholly below zero. MSE has units of squared HbA1c percentage points; RMSE/MAE are percentage points. The paired conditional interval uses 2,000 Rao–Wu-style rescaled bootstrap draws of masked PSUs within masked strata: for m=2 PSUs, draw m−1 and multiply counts by m/(m−1). All records in a PSU move together; survey weights are multiplied by these factors. A 400-draw two-cycle bootstrap also refits coefficients using resampled training weights, with preprocessing knots and product standardization fixed to original training. Thus it captures coefficient-fitting but not knot-estimation uncertainty. Both intervals are approximate percentile intervals with only 15 masked strata per cycle, not calibrated clinical or exact design intervals; finite-population corrections and calibration/nonresponse weight reconstruction are unavailable. ## Falsification and sensitivities (all author checks)
- Five-fold discovery-only grouped CV, holding entire masked PSUs out and fitting preprocessing within folds: baseline MSE 0.231555, augmented 0.231719, change **+0.000164**. This was diagnostic, not model selection; it does not support a stable gain.
- Linear additive comparator: change −0.002149; quadratic additive comparator: −0.000066689. The larger gain against a rigid linear baseline shrinks with flexible adjustment, and the product coefficient reverses sign between linear and spline fits. This is consistent with a representational artifact rather than a robust physiological law; it does not prove the mechanism of that artifact.
- Frozen-model no-anemia target subgroup (hemoglobin ≥12 g/dL for female coding, ≥13 for male; n=2,002): change −0.000273676. This crude filter does not exclude iron deficiency or altered turnover.
- Frozen-model target HbA1c <6.5 subgroup (n=1,902): change −0.000166891. Outcome-based subgrouping is descriptive, not a deployable rule; no subgroup inferential claims are made.
- Without glucose bridging: change −0.000141630. Small gain remains, without evidence that it is meaningful.
- Project product onto baseline training features, shuffle its training residual, refit with this shuffled residual while retaining the real target residual product. **26/200** controls had MSE change at least as favorable as observed. This is a stress test, not a valid causal permutation p-value or exchangeability-based null test.
- A second numerical implementation (scikit-learn weighted linear regression) matched predictions to 1.78×10⁻¹⁴; raw versus standardized product parameterization agreed to 4.09×10⁻¹⁴. Separate weighted-SSE arithmetic reproduced the central delta within 10⁻¹⁰. Rank, nonoverlapping IDs and m−1 bootstrap weight factors were checked.
- An initial author run used unrescaled m-PSU replacement; the author check identified its inadequate survey variance scale and replaced it with the stated m−1 rescaled bootstrap. Initial exploratory outputs/code are preserved in the worker archive, with the correction documented here. No initial interval underlies a claim in this paper.

## Limitations and what could test the hypothesis clinically
One fasting glucose measurement is not time-averaged glycemia. Residual HbA1c prediction differences could reflect postprandial exposure, diabetes treatment, kidney disease, iron status, unmeasured hemoglobin variants, inflammation, assay differences or social confounding. Those were not comprehensively measured or adjusted. CBC variables and outcome are contemporaneous; this is not prospective disease prediction. A flexible additive model is stronger than a simple linear baseline but not the full space of possible predictors; the result only applies to this prespecified augmentation/comparator. Full CBC histograms, reticulocytes, direct lifespan measures and continuous glucose data are absent. Complete-case weighting, public masked design variables and possible training-knot uncertainty limit inference. No claim of clinically useful hidden physiological information, lifespan measurement, biological causality, diabetes diagnosis, or generalizable clinical benefit is supported.

A future **proposed**, not performed, clinical validation could collect repeated continuous glucose exposure, directly measured RBC survival or validated turnover markers, iron/renal/hemoglobin-variant information, and paired analyzer histograms in an independently recruited cohort. It could prospectively test whether absolute cell-volume spread explains HbA1c-minus-average-glucose discordance beyond routine indices, with frozen models, between-analyzer calibration and clinical decision thresholds. This analysis does not justify changing treatment or replacing HbA1c.
