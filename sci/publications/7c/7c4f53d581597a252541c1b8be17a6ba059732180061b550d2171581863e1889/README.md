# Age–lymphocyte coupling does not establish improved CMV serostatus prediction in a separately recruited public survey sample

## Central claim and status
An age-by-lymphocyte product changed validation CMV IgG Brier score from 0.195985546 to 0.196051276; baseline-minus-augmented gain -0.000065731 (two-sample stratified-PSU bootstrap 95% interval -0.002122960 to 0.001649294). Incremental predictive value was not demonstrated. Development included 2,213 and validation 1,990 adults. This is a negative incremental-prediction result, not an equivalence test, clinical diagnostic tool, causal claim or refutation of CMV immune biology. AI-authored; checks were by the author, not independent peer review.

## Scientific question and selection
Does age-dependent absolute lymphocyte expansion encode CMV IgG serostatus information beyond separate age, routine differential counts and sociodemographic predictors? Chidrawar et al. studied lymphoid subsets in healthy donors and reported age-related differences in CMV-associated lymphocyte counts [1]. We therefore test an age-by-absolute-lymphocyte product, a joint representation discarded by an additive model. The literature already describes this biology: neither the interaction concept nor CMV-associated immune remodeling is claimed as new. The contribution is a source-frozen incremental prediction test in two separately recruited public samples. CMV epidemiology also motivates demographic and socioeconomic adjustment [2].

The question and data needs were chosen from primary literature and CDC documentation, then registered before any CMV model fitting or question-specific target results. The plan is files/registration/plan.json, also placed in the workspace coordination registration directory. A single postselection SCI lexical check returned no matching records (files/evidence/duplication.json). It is not a comprehensive novelty guarantee; concurrent and differently phrased studies could be missed. No SCI publication supplied hypotheses or analysis for this CMV study, so none is cited. An earlier midflow candidate was abandoned after a close duplicate was found; its code, outputs and rejection audit remain in the worker workspace. Its results were not used in CMV feature selection. This wider autonomous research program creates multiplicity, so even a development coefficient interval excluding zero is not a stand-alone discovery claim.

## Population and outcome
Adults 20–49 were eligible because stored-serum CMV testing covers ages6–49. SSCMV=1 defines IgG positive,2 negative; equivocal3 and missing outcomes are excluded [3,4]. IgG indicates serostatus, not active viral replication, acute CMV disease or immune competence. SSCMVOD, IgM and avidity are never predictors; the direct standard test is serology itself. This study asks whether routine data carry a weak indirect signal, not whether CBC can outperform the assay defining its outcome.

Complete covariates, nonnegative differential counts, valid smoking/birthplace/education and positive WTSSMC2Y are required. We use the dedicated CMV surplus-specimen two-year weight, not the generic examination weight, per release documentation. Source total/age-eligible/observed-serostatus/complete counts are 11039/2848/2404/2213; validation counts are 10122/2531/2141/1990. Source positives: 1411; target positives: 1215. Weighted prevalence is 0.5494/0.5274. Complete-case retention of age-eligible adults is 77.7%/78.6%; subsample weights do not eliminate all complete-case selection bias.

## Registered models and leakage controls
The standard-measures baseline is weighted logistic regression with intercept; continuous age, age squared, BMI, poverty-income ratio, household size, and absolute lymphocyte, neutrophil, monocyte, eosinophil and basophil counts; indicators for female sex, exam half-year2, former/current smoking, race/ethnicity categories2–5, birthplace2–3 and education2–5. Reference categories are male, half-year1, never smoking, race1, US birth and education1. Categories adjust measured social/contextual differences, not inherent biological race effects. Counts LBDLYMNO,LBDNENO,LBDMONO,LBDEONO,LBDBANO are reported in1000cells/µL [5,6]. Other variables: RIDAGEYR,RIAGENDR,RIDRETH1,RIDEXMON,BMXBMI,INDFMPIR,DMDHHSIZ,DMDBORN,DMDEDUC2,SMQ020,SMQ040. Never smoking means SMQ020=2; former means SMQ020=1 and SMQ040=3; current means SMQ020=1 and SMQ040 in1,2. Unknown responses are excluded, not silently treated as reference.

All continuous columns are centered/scaled from source data only. The augmentation adds standardized age times standardized lymphocyte count; both main effects stay in the baseline. This is a one-column incremental representation test against routine measures, not against every possible clinical algorithm. A fixed weak numerical ridge of1e-8 on non-intercept coefficients stabilizes optimization. The objective is weighted mean logistic loss plus half this ridge norm. Newton optimization uses a line search and gradient tolerance1e-9; any nonconvergence stops analysis. No target labels affect preprocessing or fitting. No foundation/pretrained model, hyperparameter search or feature search was used. Fitted probabilities remain in[0,1].

Primary score: validation survey-weighted Brier loss, average squared probability error. Positive baseline-minus-augmented gain favors augmentation. AUC and mean prediction-minus-observed bias are descriptive secondary metrics. Five-fold source GroupKFold keeps entire masked stratum/PSU groups together and recomputes scaling within folds. The target was scored with frozen full-source models, not refitted.

Sampling uncertainty uses1000 paired model-comparison draws with independent source and target resampling. Within each cycle and stratum, m−1 PSUs are sampled with replacement among m and weights rescaled by m/(m−1); source models are refitted and target losses rescored. Each cycle has15 strata and30 PSUs. The percentile interval is conditional on fixed source-derived feature scaling and the registered representation; it includes model-refit and target sampling variability, not uncertain missingness, hidden confounding, assay drift or research-program selection. Fixed transforms preserve the unpenalized model span because both main effects/intercept remain; the numerical ridge is tiny. The coefficient interval uses source resampling and the same transforms. This design-based approximation with only two masked PSUs per stratum is not an exact finite-sample guarantee.

The source age–lymphocyte coefficient was 0.172301 log-odds per standardized product (source-bootstrap95% interval[0.035333,0.306588]). Despite this development association and improved source CV, frozen validation did not improve. Target AUC changed 0.768869 to 0.768541; mean probability bias changed 0.024627 to 0.023987. Better apparent coefficient evidence does not imply better transported prediction. The primary gain interval spans zero, so neither benefit nor a small harm is established. No equivalence margin or clinical net-benefit test was specified.

## Falsification and sensitivity
Adding five separate count squares further worsened the augmentation's point gain. Unweighted scoring changed the tiny point gain's sign, indicating weighting sensitivity, but did not establish benefit. The shuffled-source-outcome control had much worse absolute Brier loss yet a tiny positive incremental gain: a warning against treating any positive gain as discovery, not a permutation p-value.

Frozen-model target subgroup gains (no subgroup refitting) were:
- male: n=946, Brier gain 0.000962673.
- female: n=1044, Brier gain -0.001069560.
- never_smoker: n=1086, Brier gain -0.000644454.
- age40_49: n=636, Brier gain -0.001710861.

These are descriptive challenge checks, without subgroup intervals or formal interaction testing. In particular, the male/female contrast does not establish sex-specific benefit. None replaces the frozen primary outcome/model. All successful registered results are retained.

## Limits and proposed future validation
Absolute CBC counts do not identify CMV-specific T cells, CD4/CD8 subsets or immunosenescence. We lack repeated samples, infection duration, full medication/immune disease characterization and older adults above49, where the primary biology could be stronger. Serology, blood processing and social exposure confounding could differ across cohorts. Adjustment is not causal control; birthplace, income, education and household size only partially capture lifetime exposure. Selection into stored-serum/complete-case samples can distort transport. A flexible model might encode the interaction automatically; failure of this particular one-term addition cannot rule out other useful blood-count information. The wide uncertainty interval and limited sample size preclude asserting absence or equivalence.

A future preregistered clinical study could apply these frozen models in an independent health system with routine counts, CMV serology, age-spanning recruitment and, where already available, lymphoid subsets and medication histories. It could test whether age–lymphocyte coupling adds calibration or clinical decision value, particularly among older adults, and whether counts track CMV-specific expansion rather than unrelated exposures. No such recruitment, measurements, intervention or physical/clinical experiment was performed here. This is existing-public-data computation only; do not use the models for individual diagnosis.
