# Chloride–bicarbonate geometry does not establish incremental digit-symbol prediction across two NHANES samples

## Question and scope
Can a joint serum chloride–bicarbonate pattern predict processing-speed performance better than routinely interpreted chemistry and demographic measures? Prior primary studies connect serum bicarbonate with cognition in hypertensive adults [1] and show that sodium and anion gap complicate the chloride–bicarbonate relation [2]. These motivate a joint-feature test, not a causal claim or a claim that acid–base interactions are previously unknown. A recent NHANES bicarbonate/BUN study [3] further limits novelty: the contribution here is a registered cross-sample incremental-prediction test of one explicit product. No claim of priority or exhaustive literature coverage is made.

**Central result:** adding the source-standardized chloride-by-bicarbonate product to a fixed flexible baseline did not establish improved digit-symbol prediction. The independent-cycle weighted MSE gain was **−0.066461 score²**, with a two-sample, full-pipeline PSU-bootstrap 95% percentile interval **[−1.153023, 0.680793]**. This is a negative model comparison, not an equivalence test or a rejection of acid–base effects on cognition.

## Estimator and prediction
The candidate is z(chloride) × z(bicarbonate), using unweighted G-only mean and population SD, then standardizing the resulting design column with other predictors. Baseline: separate cubic B-splines (four uniformly spaced knots, five columns each, linear extrapolation) for age, BMI, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, albumin, log creatinine, BUN, glucose, and albumin-corrected anion gap Na−Cl−HCO3+2.5(4−albumin). Sex, race/ethnicity, education and test language enter as one-hot indicators; sex interacts with age and BMI spline blocks. This controls standard individual chemistry values and a standard composite, not a validated clinical DSST risk instrument. Both designs use G-only unweighted preprocessing and weighted ridge regression (alpha10, unpenalized intercept). MEC weights are normalized to mean1 within each sample. There is no outcome imputation, target fitting, penalty tuning, or target-derived preprocessing.

The predeclared success criterion was positive H weighted MSE gain (baseline minus augmented), full-pipeline95% interval wholly above zero, and no worse MAE. Uncertainty: 2000 stratified PSU resamples of H conditional on the fitted models, and300 independent G/H stratified PSU resamples with G preprocessing and model refitting. Each stratum resamples its observed PSUs with replacement; source rows are duplicated by multiplicity. These are approximate survey-aware percentile intervals, not official design-based confidence statements for the full older US population; weights are not adjusted for complete-case selection. G has31 observed PSUs in14 strata; H has30 in15. Random seeds are fixed in code. Fivefold G group CV keeps each masked PSU together and refits preprocessing within folds; it is descriptive, not a tuning loop.

## Limitations and next test
No positive transferable new health marker was established. The interval admits small benefits and harms; no minimum clinically important difference was registered. One product and one outcome do not exhaust nonlinear physiological information. Cross-sectional chemistry and cognition cannot identify causation, acidemia, brain injury or prospective decline. No blood pH, PCO2 or medication adjustment was available in this model; diuretics, kidney function, illness, socioeconomic differences, education, language and complete-case selection remain explanations. Log creatinine/BUN and demographics mitigate but cannot remove confounding. Same-survey cycle transfer offers weaker generalization than another cohort, country or assay platform. Reuse of these survey cycles across many earlier questions creates programme-level multiplicity; this paper makes no discovery significance claim.

A future independent observational cohort could lock this feature and both linear/flexible baselines before enrollment or outcome access, measure blood-gas pH/PCO2 and medication use, and test future cognitive change and calibration. A randomized clinical intervention would need separate clinical justification; this existing-data analysis neither performs nor supports bicarbonate treatment.
