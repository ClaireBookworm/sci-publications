# Frequency-wise better-ear switching advantage and perceived hearing trouble: an exploratory cross-cohort incremental-prediction test

## Abstract
We tested whether crossed left/right audiometric deficits contain useful information beyond standard ear-level hearing summaries. The proposed switching advantage is the better-ear four-frequency pure-tone average minus the average of the frequency-wise minimum thresholds. It is nonnegative and vanishes when one ear is consistently better. We fitted weighted logistic models in 3816 NHANES2011–2012 adults and evaluated frozen predictions in 4263 separately recruited2015–2016 adults. The outcome was concurrent self-reported hearing trouble. Adding frequency-wise better-ear switching advantage changed target Brier score from 0.125382 to 0.125969, difference +0.000587 (joint source/target PSU-bootstrap95% interval -0.000106 to +0.002596). The registered improvement criterion was not met. This is exploratory cross-cohort validation, not untouched confirmation or independent-study-program validation, and does not establish a binaural mechanism or clinical utility.

## Measurement and algebraic interpretation
For frequency set F={0.5,1,2,4kHz}, let L_f and R_f be left/right dB HL thresholds. Define S=min(mean_F L, mean_F R)−mean_F min(L,R). S is the gap between a conventional better-ear PTA and an idealized frequency-wise better-ear summary, in dB. Algebraically,

**S = [mean_F |L−R| − |mean_F(L−R)|]/2.**

The triangle inequality makes S≥0. If the same ear is no worse at every frequency, S=0. S is invariant to relabeling ears and additive threshold shifts. Thus it is a crossed-deficit compression, not new raw information or proof that the auditory system actually combines ears optimally. Positive S could reduce difficulty if complementary audibility matters, or mark complex impairment. The model did not constrain its direction. This exact algebra also means that S is redundant once frequency-specific absolute asymmetries and better/worse PTA are supplied; the richer-baseline sensitivity must not be treated as evidence of an additional independent biomarker.

## Limitations and next study
Complete-case and testability selection may exclude severe impairment; self-report includes cognition, expectations, hearing-device use and environment. Residual confounding by noise, middle-ear disease, medications, occupation, language and socioeconomic factors remains. Threshold quantization/calibration and differing test protocols can affect S. We did not measure binaural integration, speech recognition or cochlear synaptopathy. Model improvement, if present, is not a causal estimate. Negative results constrain this specification, not every representation of bilateral audiometry. Shared-program validation and prior cohort exposure make this exploratory and narrow. A richer full-audiogram model is a necessary future benchmark; this study's richer sensitivity is only linear in frequency means/asymmetry.

A future independent study could preregister S, repeat bilateral thresholds to quantify reliability, record aided/unaided status and hearing-in-noise performance, then test the frozen model and clinically useful improvement margin prospectively. None of those clinical measurements were performed. An existing independent dataset with bilateral audiometry and comparable self-report would provide the next computational confirmation.
