# Frequency-varying interaural asymmetry did not improve digit-symbol prediction across two NHANES samples

## Question and motivation
[Lin (2011), Hearing loss and cognition among older adults](https://doi.org/10.1093/gerona/glr115) studied average hearing loss and DSST in NHANES. We ask a different, representational question: does frequency-varying left/right imbalance retain cognitive-score information lost by ordinary pure-tone averages (PTAs)? Unequal peripheral injury or variable response reliability could plausibly yield such dispersion, but these data cannot distinguish either mechanism. We do not claim this feature has never been studied or introduces new raw measurements.

The question was selected from literature/codebooks, then a single documented catalog duplication search found 12 adjacent records (hearing trouble and tinnitus, not DSST). No SCI analysis, numerical result or model is used here; those publications are therefore not scientific dependencies. Index omissions and concurrent work remain possible. An earlier albumin/globulin–CRP candidate was abandoned as a substantive duplicate and its code, outputs and evidence retained outside this package. This unsuccessful history is not hidden. The current target was not used to select features or penalties; all target subgroup analyses below are exploratory. Initial registration used an erroneous DSST mnemonic; CFQ documentation identified CFDRIGHT, implemented throughout. Education was implemented categorically; these metadata corrections are recorded, not passed off as prospectively pristine preregistration.

## Definitions, standard comparator and leakage prevention
For seven frequencies (0.5,1,2,3,4,6,8kHz), take the first recorded unmasked air-conduction thresholds `AUXU500`, `AUXU1K1`, `AUXU2K`, `AUXU3K`, `AUXU4K`, `AUXU6K`, `AUXU8K`, for each ear. Values outside −20..120dB HL (including 666/888 sentinel values) are excluded, not treated as severe loss. Retest and second1kHz readings are not substituted. Outcome is CFDRIGHT, number of correct symbols (0..133), not dementia diagnosis. [Audiometry documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/1999/DataFiles/AUX1.htm) and [CFQ documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/1999/DataFiles/CFQ.htm) define these measurements; [2001 audiometry](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2001/DataFiles/AUX_B.htm) defines the independent release.

Let d_f=right_f−left_f. The candidate is sqrt(mean_f((d_f−mean(d))²)), a population SD across seven fixed frequencies, invariant to ear swapping. Ordinary better/worse ear PTA is the within-ear mean at0.5,1,2,4kHz, then min/max across ears. The baseline includes both PTAs, signed mean(d), seven bilateral frequency means, age, poverty-income ratio and BMI, each with separate cubic B-splines (3 uniformly spaced source knots, no spline bias; linear extrapolation), and one-hot sex, race/ethnicity and five-category education. This richer baseline prevents a simple unmodelled hearing-frequency mean from masquerading as an asymmetry discovery. It is a transparent statistical comparator, not an accepted clinical DSST prediction score. Adding one standardized dispersion column tests an incremental representation, not new raw information. There are no target-fit transformations, tuning or recalibration.

Both designs are standardized on source data and fitted by weighted ridge regression with intercept and fixed alpha10 (sum normalized weighted residual squares plus alpha times coefficient-square sum). Weights are WTSAU2YR, the audiometry subsample MEC weights, normalized to mean1. Categories use unknown-ignore encoding. All transform fits, models and bootstrap refits use source participants only; identifiers, survey labels and outcomes are not predictors. Source CV has five PSU-grouped folds with transforms refitted inside each fold; it is descriptive and not used to tune alpha. Five-fold mean gain averages fold gains rather than asserting a pooled design-based estimate. Target is loaded in the same script but no fitting API receives target outcomes.

Primary gain = weighted target baseline MSE minus augmented MSE, in correct-symbol²; positive is better. The intended success criterion requires positive source grouped-CV average gain and target full-pipeline95% lower bound above0. This is one primary feature/test, not a many-feature search with a best winner reported.

## Results
Source n245; target n255. Target baseline MSE237.038967, augmented238.307320; gain−1.268353 correct-symbol² (−0.5351% improvement). MAE11.934695 vs11.984993. The source five-fold average gain was−1.334078; all five folds were negative. The criterion failed.

For uncertainty, independently within each cycle/stratum resample m−1 of m PSUs with replacement and multiply multiplicities by m/(m−1), retaining survey weights. There are13 source strata (one with3PSUs, others2) and15 target strata (2 each). No singleton stratum occurred. Uniform-knot spline support and scalers/models are refitted using nonzero source bootstrap observations; fractional multiplicities enter fitting/scaling, and normalized combined weights preserve the ridge objective's effective source sample size. The bootstrap is an approximate design-based uncertainty procedure on the selected domain, not official NHANES replicate-weight inference. It cannot undo complete-case selection.

The 2,000-replicate target-only interval conditional on the source fit was[−4.454491,1.557102]. The more relevant300-replicate source-refit plus target PSU interval was[−17.016879,3.279132]; only30% of these gains were positive. These percentile intervals are broad and allow benefit as well as harm. A failure to demonstrate benefit is not equivalence, biological absence or evidence against the general hearing–cognition association. Point gains are small relative to residual MSE; no clinically useful threshold was established.

## Limitations and future test
The sample is small, age-restricted, complete-case and drawn from a half-sample audiometry examination; some high/no-response thresholds are excluded. Sampling weights do not fix missingness, measurement error or participation bias. Cognitive capacity may itself affect audiometric response reliability. Education/income/age adjustment does not eliminate confounding by vascular disease, noise exposure, vision, motor speed, hearing aids or health literacy. This is contemporaneous prediction, not causal inference, prospective risk or screening validation. Identical survey infrastructure limits transport; a distinct external cohort remains untested. The broad refit interval precludes ruling out useful gains. A richer regularized model, another representation or repeat-measure aggregation could behave differently; none was opportunistically optimized here.

A future clinical observational study could preregister the exact feature and locked comparator in a distinct older-adult cohort, record repeated audiometry to separate reliable asymmetry from response variability, measure visual/motor and vascular confounders, and test longitudinal cognitive change and calibration. No such recruitment, measurement, intervention or experiment was performed or is implied. The present contribution is a reproducible negative test of one proposed representation.
