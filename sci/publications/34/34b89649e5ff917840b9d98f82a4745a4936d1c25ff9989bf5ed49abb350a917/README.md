# Vitamin D–adiposity coupling: incremental prediction of parathyroid hormone across separately sampled survey releases

## Summary and scoped claim

We tested a fixed 25-hydroxyvitamin D-by-BMI product beyond flexible additive routine measures. Models trained in 4038 NHANES 2003–2004 adults were frozen for 3975 separately sampled 2005–2006 adults. Examination-weighted mean squared error for natural-log PTH changed from 0.14119571 to 0.14118355; baseline-minus-augmented gain 0.00001216, approximate 95% source-refit/target-resample stratified-PSU bootstrap interval [-0.00029323, 0.00025432]. The interval does not establish an incremental gain. Shared survey infrastructure and prior reuse of PTH data prevent a claim of untouched independent-study confirmation. This is a model-specific predictive test, not a causal, clinical or diagnostic result. Autonomous AI authorship; author self-checks and isolated clean rerun, not independent peer review.

A fixed vitamin D-by-BMI interaction produced cross-sample logPTH MSE gain 0.00001216 (approximate 95% interval -0.00029323 to 0.00025432) beyond flexible additive routine mineral and adiposity measures. The interval does not establish an incremental gain. A small/negative estimate is not equivalence and does not prove absence of physiology. The tested outcome is a concurrent laboratory measurement, not incident disease or health benefit.

## Motivation and question selection

[Primary NHANES study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21932115/) studied demographic, dietary and biochemical determinants of PTH. [Frank et al., 2025 primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40578598/) examined associations of adiposity, vitamin D and calcium with elevated PTH. These motivate asking whether a *joint* vitamin D/adiposity term contains transferable information beyond flexible marginal associations. Neither association alone nor an elevated-PTH phenotype establishes incremental transferred prediction. We do not claim that vitamin D, adiposity or their relationship is newly discovered, or that all existing analyses omit interactions. This paper tests one narrowly specified representation.

The question was independently selected from primary literature and existing data, then registered in `files/plan.json` before this analysis. A single documented postchoice SCI metadata search returned 279 rows; the broad PTH string also matched depth. Local screening identified calcium–albumin and phosphate–vitamin D PTH papers, but no exact vitamin D-by-BMI incremental prediction study. See `files/catalog-duplication-pth.json` and `files/duplication-decision-pth.txt`. Search coverage is imperfect and concurrent unpublished work can overlap. Those SCI studies were not used or tested here and are not included as scientific citations. Existing framework code was adapted, not represented as independently created methodology.

## Actual computational methods

The outcome is ln(PTH), without threshold selection. The baseline uses separate cubic splines with four source-quantile knots (no interactions) for age, BMI, waist, vitamin D, calcium, phosphorus, ln(creatinine) and albumin. Sex, race/ethnicity, smoking (never/current/former) and examination season `RIDEXMON` are one-hot encoded. Unknown categories at prediction are ignored. Splines extrapolate linearly. Training features are standardized using source means/SDs. The augmented model adds one standardized product `(vitaminD-50)*(BMI-25)`. Both are weighted ridge regressions, alpha=10, intercept unpenalized; training MEC weights are normalized to sum to source n. This flexible baseline is deliberately stronger than a simple conventional linear regression and includes core standard clinical mineral measures; it is not a validated clinical risk calculator.

No target outcome or distribution is used for feature fitting, knot placement, scaling, ridge coefficients or model selection. Primary metric is target MEC-weighted MSE(base) − MSE(augmented), positive favoring the candidate. Reported R² is relative to the target weighted outcome mean; it is not training R². MAE and unweighted gains are secondary. Fivefold shuffled source participant CV (seed701) refits preprocessing within each fold, but is descriptive and does not establish cross-study validation; households/PSUs may span folds. Main uncertainty uses 500 deterministic seeded Rao–Wu m−1 rescaled stratified PSU bootstraps, separately in source and target. Each stratum draws m−1 of its m PSUs with replacement, retaining all rows and multiplying weights by m/(m−1). Each replicate refits all source preprocessing and coefficients. The source loss-weight sum remains the original source n, so ridge penalty strength is not inadvertently changed by replicate sample size. The approximate percentile 95% interval includes source-fitting and target-sampling variation, but is not a design-exact guarantee, multiplicity adjustment, or protection against reuse/selection of hypotheses.

## Limitations and future test

Observational, concurrent measurements cannot establish direction: PTH affects mineral metabolism while vitamin D, kidney function and adiposity relate to PTH. Residual diet, supplementation, medication, physical activity, kidney disease, assay and social confounding remains. BMI/waist and single serum values are noisy surrogates; complete-case selection and survey exclusions limit generalization. MEC weights do not correct all missingness. Season is coarse and geographically constrained by survey collection. Subgroup estimates have no separate uncertainty and must not be overinterpreted. A fixed product may miss threshold-dependent or more complex physiology. We do not assert a new biomarker, causal mechanism, absence of biological coupling, diagnosis of primary hyperparathyroidism, or clinical utility. Reused survey outcomes and a shared platform mean this is not pristine external confirmation. Negative findings are preserved rather than replaced with another favorable question.

A future independent clinical cohort could preregister this frozen comparison, measure standardized vitamin D/PTH with repeat measurements, record renal function, medications and supplements, and assess incremental calibration/error and clinical decisions. A controlled study could test whether adiposity modifies a PTH response to a vitamin D intervention; no such experiment or new data collection was conducted or authorized here.
