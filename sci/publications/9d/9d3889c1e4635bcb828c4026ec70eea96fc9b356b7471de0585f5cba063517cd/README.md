# Upper-arm length did not improve cross-cycle FVC prediction beyond flexible standard anthropometry

## Abstract
Upper-arm length could encode body proportions not captured by standing height, but whether that information improves held-out lung-volume prediction is uncertain. We fixed a one-term extension before testing its association: add measured upper-arm length to an empirical FVC model using age, sex, height, BMI, waist, smoking and self-reported race/ethnicity. We fitted NHANES 2007–2008 adults (n=3,749) and applied the frozen models to the separately recruited 2009–2010 dataset (n=4,171). Target RMSE was 518.992 mL without and 519.032 mL with the arm term. Baseline-minus-augmented RMSE was −0.040 mL (target stratified-PSU bootstrap 95% interval −0.566 to +0.457; source-and-target refit interval −0.965 to +0.470). Source five-fold out-of-fold gain was +0.137 mL. Survey weighting, stronger anthropometric flexibility and other sensitivity tests did not show a consistent benefit. This is a scoped negative predictive result, not proof that body proportions contain no physiological information or an equivalence study. Independent-programme validation was not performed.

## Question and literature basis
We chose this question from primary physiological literature and the availability of NHANES limb-segment measurements, not the SCI catalog. Body-frame analyses in CARDIA ([Jacobs et al., 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1519841/)) motivate asking whether standard height loses anatomical information. [Golshan et al., 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17539839/) evaluated arm-span equations, including a separate validation group; arm span is not upper-arm length, so this is only motivation, not evidence that our proxy works. [Quanjer et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25254426/) studied cohort changes in proportions, while [Burton and Burton](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32530561/) compared sitting and total height scaling. These studies do not establish the incremental utility of a single upper-arm term. We do not claim proportionality, limb-based prediction, or anthropometric lung-function modelling is new. Our contribution is a reproducible, explicitly conditional transfer test of this particular weak proxy with a strong comparator and negative result.

The registered representation is raw BMXARML (cm) added linearly. By partial regression, this is equivalent to using its residual after projection on baseline covariates. We did not select a best segment, tune a ratio exponent, search outcomes, or choose a model on target scores. `files/plan.json` records the locally frozen plan, not an externally preregistered trial. One documented post-selection SCI keyword duplication check returned 55 records; none matched this study. Endpoint 503 retries belong to that same check. The search cannot exclude differently indexed or concurrent work. No SCI paper was used or tested, so no SCI citations are added.

Cache-access dates are not claimed as original cache-download dates.

The statistical unit is one participant. We inner-joined files one-to-one on SEQN, selected age 20–79, FVC quality A/B (meets or exceeds ATS collection standards), positive MEC weight and complete variables. Smoking: never if SMQ020=2; former if SMQ020=1 and SMQ040=3; current if SMQ020=1 and SMQ040=1/2. Refused, unknown or invalid answers are missing; no imputation. Source demographics n=10,149, merged n=6,528, age-eligible n=5,318, quality-eligible n=3,823, complete n=3,749. Corresponding target counts: 10,537; 6,973; 5,663; 4,291; 4,171. This selection is not all US adults: serious illness and spirometry contraindications and complete-case selection limit transport.

Source and target are distinct cross-sectional recruitment cycles, not random splits of one dataset; person IDs do not overlap. Both use the NHANES programme and largely shared protocols, not different clinical sites/programmes with independent measurement systems. Public IDs do not provide a cross-cycle linkage audit capable of excluding every concealed re-enrolment. These cached cycles were used in unrelated previous work; confirmation here means no target arm–FVC analysis or target-guided model selection in this project, not a globally never-examined dataset. This qualifies a cross-cycle predictive test only. An independent-programme test remains missing.

## Fixed methods and comparator
Outcome: baseline/pre-bronchodilator SPXNFVC, mL, not disease diagnosis, future risk, or FEV1/FVC. No other spirometric result is used as a predictor. The comparator is an empirically refitted model of standard measures, not a deployed GLI clinical reference equation and not a diagnostic reference standard. Continuous age, height, BMI and waist are centered at 50 years,170 cm,25 kg/m²,90 cm and scaled by 20,10,5,15 respectively, then entered linearly and quadratically. Add female indicator, female interactions with linear and quadratic age and height, former/current smoking indicators and four race/ethnicity indicators (reference RIDRETH1=1). Intercept plus 19 covariates gives a full-rank20-column design; arm adds one column (rank21). Source StandardScaler parameters are frozen for target use. Source-only unweighted ordinary least squares is primary. Race/ethnicity is a measured social classification used as a confounding benchmark, not a biological explanation or recommendation for race-based reference equations; a no-race sensitivity is reported.

Two frozen models are applied to all target participants without refitting. Prespecified success required positive RMSE gain with target-cluster95% interval entirely above zero and no worse MAE. This failed. Uncertainty: seed20261118,2,000 paired target resamples of PSUs within strata with replacement, retaining all persons in a sampled PSU; percentile95% interval conditional on source fits. Also200 independent paired source/target PSU resamples with source model and preprocessing refitted; these intervals are Monte Carlo approximations with only16 source and15 target strata, not a full official NHANES replicate-weight variance procedure. Target includes31 PSUs, source32. Source5fold OOF (shuffle seed914) is descriptive, no tuning; folds are participant-level and not independent-dataset evidence. Cluster uncertainty addresses within-PSU dependence but cannot remove confounding or selection bias.

## Results
Target baseline versus augmented MAE:405.600 vs405.525 mL; R²:0.758781 vs0.758744. A tiny MAE decrease did not accompany RMSE benefit. Source partial arm coefficient was +12.082 mL/cm, numerically checked via residual regression. Coefficient magnitude is not evidence of clinical utility or causality. Source residual arm SD was1.404 cm. Target mean arm length was37.069 versus37.501 cm in source despite similar mean heights167.897 and167.834 cm; target source-adjusted arm residual mean was−0.430 cm. Cohort anatomy, adiposity, measurement or residual confounding could explain this difference; we did not diagnose its cause or recalibrate on target.

Weighted analysis uses normalized source WTMEC2YR for fitting and target MEC weights for evaluation; it is a descriptive population-weight sensitivity, not a missingness adjustment. BMI18.5–30 probes the CDC warning that limb-length measurements are affected by extreme adiposity. QualityA restricts to the highest FVC quality. Leg control adds BMXLEG with complete-case restriction. Spline sensitivity adds cubic B-splines (four source knots per continuous variable, include_bias=False) to the polynomial baseline and fits standardized Ridge(alpha=1) in both models to stabilize redundant basis functions. It still does not support incremental arm benefit.

In50 null checks, we permuted source arm residuals after projection on baseline features, reconstructed arm length and refitted; target labels remained unchanged. Null gain range was−0.738 to+0.093 mL. This is a falsification diagnostic, not a permutation p-value or evidence of equivalence. The no-race coefficient changed sign to−8.260 mL/cm; this model dependence further warns against anatomical interpretation. Small positive restricted-sample gains are not rescue endpoints.

## Scope and future test
This negative result addresses one linear segment proxy and an empirical comparator. It does not establish zero information, equivalence, lack of effects in disease groups, failure of arm span or sitting height, or failure of all nonlinear proportionality representations. The interval is local to this model and selected cohorts; it is not a bound on all possible utility. Cross-sectional confounding, unknown occupational exposures, smoking dose, prior illness, posture, skeletal proportions and measurement error remain. Anthropometry may encode social, developmental and illness influences. FVC is effort dependent and cannot distinguish restriction without total lung capacity. Complete-case/quality restriction and only two cycles limit generality. Neither causal mechanism nor clinical benefit has been demonstrated.

A future independent clinical measurement study could collect upper-arm length, arm span, sitting height and reproducible spirometry simultaneously, assess segment measurement reliability and evaluate frozen models and clinically relevant error margins across disease and demographic groups. It could test whether direct trunk measures work where this proxy failed. We performed no such study and no new data collection or intervention.
