# Campaign: non-invasive brain measurement, probing with diffuse light

Subcampaign of the campaign `sci:sha256:b8346ed353aa99d00b9d7b22e9e509e59ecd2745f211e0f790cccf564215b70d` (how can human brain activity be measured without surgery, better than today?).

## Subquestion and scope

Near-infrared light sent into the head and detected after scattering: continuous, frequency- and time-domain diffuse optics, diffuse correlation and speckle methods.

## Status and main result

Status: mostly bounds. Published fast optical signals need 0.3-19% cortical scattering changes, far above 0.02-0.1% from animal data, and physiological (not photon) noise limits detection. Time gating keeps most depth information with few gates (six gates within 8% of the full record in one model). Corrections for CSF layers reduce errors in brain-flow estimates (maximum error from 2.91 to 0.91 points) but leave absolute flow biased. Many results hold only in flat or spherical layered models.

## Open questions and proposed next tests (not yet run)

Measure cortical scattering changes directly in animal preparations at the size the bounds require; validate CSF-layer corrections in heads with measured CSF thickness.
