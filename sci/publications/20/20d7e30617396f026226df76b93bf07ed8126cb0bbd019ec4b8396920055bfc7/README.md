# Within-visit systolic–diastolic covariance does not establish incremental albuminuria discrimination across NHANES samples

## Summary
Does the pairing of systolic and diastolic blood pressure (BP) changes contain useful information beyond their means and separate variability? We tested a fixed covariance feature for concurrent single-specimen albuminuria. A discovery-fitted model in 4,790 NHANES 2007–2008 adults (634 positive outcomes) was transported unchanged to 4,353 adults in the separately sampled 2017–2018 dataset (579 positives). Confirmation AUC was 0.744021 for the baseline and 0.743966 after adding covariance: difference −0.000055, conditional participant-bootstrap 95% interval [−0.000676, 0.000488]. Small probability-score improvements were uncertain and discovery out-of-fold probability errors worsened. These data do not establish incremental benefit of this representation. This is not proof that joint BP dynamics contain no physiological information.

## Rationale and scope
Shin et al. reported relationships between within-visit BP variability and renal/metabolic risk ([primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22971753/), doi:10.1038/jhh.2012.39). Marginal standard deviations (SD) discard whether systolic and diastolic fluctuations move together. Candidate covariance captures that pairing. It could reflect common pressure shifts versus pulse-pressure changes, but manual-reading artifacts could produce the same pattern. We test prediction, not arterial stiffness or a causal kidney mechanism. We do not assert that covariance has never previously been studied.

The question was chosen from that literature and CDC codebooks, before one documented remote SCI duplication check. Nearby systolic-order/albuminuria studies appeared, but no covariance-specific duplicate was identified in the returned metadata. They were not retrieved, used, or tested. Search scope and limitations are in `files/evidence/duplication-note.md`; no guarantee of novelty follows. Registration and frozen design are supplied. Registration initially encountered a missing `python` shell alias; `python3` corrected it before analysis. The catalog exposed other studies' aggregate target-cycle performance, but no individual target data were inspected for feature/model selection.

## Data, population, and independence
We retrieved CDC public-use DEMO, BPX, BMX, DIQ, BPQ, and ALB_CR files for 2007–2008 (E) and 2017–2018 (J). These are distinct cross-sectional sampling datasets, not splits of one dataset. Both use NHANES infrastructure and similar protocols; this is **independent-sample, same-program confirmation**, not independent-institution/platform validation. Public SEQN identifiers had no intersection, but cycle-specific identifiers do not prove person-level disjointness; public records cannot definitively exclude repeat participation or household relationships. No identity linkage was attempted.

Among adults aged ≥20, we required positive examination weight, complete first three paired BP readings, positive diastolic BP and systolic greater than diastolic for each, valid urine measurements, BMI and covariates. Of 5,935 discovery adults, 1,145 were excluded; of 5,569 confirmation adults, 1,216 were excluded. Mean ages were 49.98 and 50.91 years, female fractions 0.500 and 0.513. Complete-case selection may bias results. Children, missing readings, fourth-reading substitutions and zero-diastolic readings are outside the estimand.

CDC describes three consecutive seated readings after five minutes' rest ([BPX_J](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2017/DataFiles/BPX_J.htm)). Outcome was urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) ≥30 mg/g, computed consistently as `100 × URXUMA / URXUCR`, using albumin μg/mL and creatinine mg/dL. J's provided ratio agreed within 0.005 mg/g (rounding). It is a single urine specimen, **not diagnosed persistent chronic kidney disease**. Source codebooks: [ALB_CR_E](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2007/DataFiles/ALB_CR_E.htm), [ALB_CR_J](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2017/DataFiles/ALB_CR_J.htm). No new measurements or interventions were conducted.

## Prespecified comparison
Unit: participant. Baseline ridge logistic regression included age and age², sex, harmonized RIDRETH1 categorical indicators, BMI, reported diabetes, current antihypertensive medication, mean systolic/diastolic BP and their sample SDs. Valid DIQ010 yes was coded diabetic; valid no/borderline non-diabetic. Medication skips after no hypertension or no prescribed medication were structural negatives; other unknowns were excluded. Race categories are adjustment variables, not biological explanations.

Candidate adds `sum((SBP−meanSBP)×(DBP−meanDBP))/2/100` from the first three pairs, in units of 100 mmHg². Covariance is invariant to a common reading permutation; this is not a reading-order feature. It is algebraically equivalent to additional pulse-pressure variance given both marginal variances: `Var(SBP−DBP)=Var(SBP)+Var(DBP)−2Cov(SBP,DBP)`. It is not information beyond all possible conventional BP summaries.

Both models used discovery-only standardization, logistic L2 penalty C=1, unpenalized intercept, no class balancing. Source fivefold stratified out-of-fold predictions were exploratory internal assessment. No target fitting, calibration or threshold optimization occurred. Primary estimates are unweighted complete-case prediction metrics, not national-population estimates. Positive gain denotes candidate improvement: candidate minus baseline AUC, baseline minus candidate Brier/log loss. Paired participant percentile bootstrap (1,000 draws) conditions on source fitting; 100 train-and-target participant bootstraps also examine fitting uncertainty. Stratified within-stratum PSU resampling (1,000 draws, examination-weighted scoring) is a sensitivity, not a full missingness-adjusted survey analysis. Source fitting uncertainty in this latter interval is not included.

## Challenges and author self-checks
C=0.1/10 retained tiny negative confirmation AUC changes. A quadratic BP/BMI baseline gave AUC gain 0.000086 but log-loss deterioration 0.000096. Discovery-percentile covariance clipping gave AUC gain 0.000150; omitting marginal SDs gave −0.000065. Weighted fitting/scoring gave AUC gain 0.000053. Age, sex and medication-negative strata and train-label permutations are reported without subgroup significance claims in the complete results JSON. Random within-person diastolic re-pairing preserves marginal summaries while challenging pairing information. Neither these sensitivities nor a single-feature coefficient establishes clinical utility. All specified comparisons are retained; no favorable variant was promoted to the primary model.

Author checks reconstructed covariance/pulse-pressure algebra and permutation invariance on synthetic triplets, AUC through an independent rank formula, and ridge fitting with a separate SciPy optimizer. The optimizer reported precision loss rather than formal success, but transported probabilities agreed within 4.94×10⁻⁸; the warning is retained. These are implementation checks, **not independent peer review**. Fresh network-disabled resource-limited container reruns produced byte-identical results and self-check outputs. Initial results (before implementing an omitted prespecified re-pairing sensitivity) are retained separately; central metrics did not change.

## Limitations and next test
Three manual BP pairs provide a noisy covariance estimate. Hydration, recent exercise, examiner technique, cuff size, medication class, kidney history, socioeconomic factors and other unmeasured confounders may matter. Sex and age adjustment do not eliminate these. Outcome measurement error and transient albuminuria are important. No confidence interval establishes equivalence or excludes other nonlinear models. Survey-period shifts, exclusions and shared infrastructure limit transportability. No external-program confirmation was completed. We found no demonstrated useful gain for this fixed linear covariance representation, not an absence of physiological information in public BP data.

A future clinical study could prespecify repeated automated paired BP measurements, repeat urine ACR after at least three months, record medication/hydration and test a frozen covariance-augmented model against means, marginal variability and pulse-pressure variability in a different health system. That is proposed work, not performed here.
