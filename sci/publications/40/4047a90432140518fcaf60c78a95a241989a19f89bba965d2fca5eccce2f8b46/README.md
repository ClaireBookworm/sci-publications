# Replicate voice variability in Parkinson screening: source-cohort improvement fails external transfer

## Claim and scope
Between-phonation dispersion of log jitter and log shimmer improved source-only leave-one-identifier-out prediction but did not demonstrate transfer to an independent 80-person voice dataset: Brier loss changed by 0.02162 (joint bootstrap 95% interval -0.03293 to 0.07897).

This is a negative test of a specific frozen feature/model pipeline, not evidence that repeat-phonation variability is biologically absent, nor a clinical diagnostic validation. Both models perform worse than a constant probability 0.5 on the balanced external cohort (Brier 0.25). The interval permits benefit as well as harm. The apparent source-cohort gain is insufficient evidence of generalizable physiology. We do not claim that dispersion is novel or unused throughout the literature: replicated voice analyses already exist. The contribution is an explicit, reproducible between-dataset test of this simple summary.

## Motivation, sources, and registration
Little and colleagues motivate moving beyond standard acoustic measures toward nonlinear voice dynamics [3,5]. Naranjo and colleagues explicitly address dependence among repeated phonations [4]; the UCI documentation attributes their variation to both technical imperfections and biology [2]. Our question was whether this variation itself provides portable subject-level information after controlling for mean acoustic measurements. Literature and raw feature definitions were consulted before the single bounded SCI duplication check. The registration and check are under `files/evidence/`; the latter searched 1000 catalog titles/abstracts once for four fixed phrases with no matches. It cannot exclude alternate wording or concurrent unpublished work. SCI papers did not supply the question or enter this analysis and none are cited merely for participation.

The initial registration described 31 Oxford people and 23 cases, following UCI. The actual raw file has 32 distinct `phon_R01_Sxx` prefixes and 24 case prefixes, each with a stable label. We preserve all 32 identifier groups rather than silently discard or merge one. Participant identity cannot be independently reconstructed from this anonymized table. Source leave-one-out means leave-one-identifier-out, not guaranteed leave-one-person-out if identifiers alias. The discrepancy was discovered after the first computation, logged, and prompted a clearly post-result deletion sensitivity, not a change of primary endpoint.

## Frozen methods
The statistical unit is the source identifier group or target participant, never an individual recording. The binary outcome is the released Parkinson case label. First-three selection gives equal replicate counts and uses recording number, not outcome or acoustic values. No group has fewer than three recordings; required values are positive and complete. Natural logarithms are applied separately to absolute jitter (seconds) and local shimmer; using absolute jitter avoids relative-jitter percent/fraction mismatch. Dispersion of log values is invariant to multiplicative units, but not to rounding or algorithm differences.

Baseline: participant means of log absolute jitter, log shimmer, RPDE, DFA and PPE. This includes conventional perturbation measures and three established nonlinear measures; it is not every available acoustic feature or a clinical examination. Added features: sample SD (ddof 1) of the three log-jitter and three log-shimmer values. Small replicate counts make these noisy. A source-fitted StandardScaler feeds L2 logistic regression, C1, balanced class weights, intercept unpenalized, fixed solver and no hyperparameter search. Class weighting reduces source prevalence effects but does not correct ascertainment or protocol shift. Target data do not fit the scaler, select features or set C. Identifiers, class labels, gender, and recording counts are not model predictors.

Primary endpoint: target Brier loss difference (added minus baseline; negative is improvement). A successful registered test required the upper 95% bound below zero. The 1000-draw joint stratified bootstrap independently resamples source and target groups with replacement and refits on the source only; each dataset's observed class counts are fixed, so the interval is conditional on case-control composition. AUROC is secondary. Source leave-one-identifier-out refits scaling and model in every fold. Frozen sensitivities include reverse transfer, C0.1/10, perturbation-only baseline, all source recordings, target gender strata,100 source-label permutations and 200 target joint-dispersion permutations. These are challenges, not multiple alternative success criteria. The target is an independent cohort test under this registration, not a large untouched prospective confirmation. No external clinical claim is supported.

## Results
| Evaluation | Baseline Brier | Added Brier | Baseline AUROC | Added AUROC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Source identifier-LOO |0.17957|0.13769|0.77083|0.85938|
| Independent target 80 |0.37722|0.39884|0.58188|0.57313|

Target Brier difference is+0.02162, joint bootstrap 95% interval[-0.03293,0.07897]. AUROC difference is-0.00875, interval[-0.08252,0.04314]. Thus the registered transfer criterion fails; significant harm is also not established. No full deployment model or clinical efficacy is claimed.

Sensitivity target Brier differences: C0.1 +0.01118; C10 +0.05864; perturbation-only baseline +0.05443; all available source repetitions +0.01965. Reverse transfer gives+0.00104 with severe absolute loss (baseline 0.53612, added 0.53716). Target men (48,26 cases) have Brier 0.39288 to 0.38808; women (32,14 cases)0.35374 to 0.41499. These small, unadjusted strata are not reliable interaction estimates. Gender is only available in the target. The corrected fractions of permutation differences at least as favorable as the observed Brier difference were 0.79208 for 100 source-label permutations and 0.56716 for 200 target-dispersion permutations. These are descriptive falsification checks, not clinical significance tests.

The post-result single-source-ID-deletion challenge gives external differences from+0.00560 to+0.03508 across 32 refits: no single deletion reverses the point estimate. This does not solve unknown identifier aliasing. Complete values, all deletion results, model predictions and bootstrap draws are in `files/outputs/`.

## Domain shift and limitations
Compared with the source range,40/80 target RPDE means,47/80 DFA means and 39/80 PPE means lie outside support. Ten jitter-dispersion and 12 shimmer-dispersion summaries also lie outside source support. This is a protocol/population transfer failure, not an identified biological mechanism. Different extraction algorithms, microphones, recording conditions, language, disease severity, treatment and source rounding can alter both means and dispersions. Extracted features rather than raw audio prevent common-pipeline reprocessing. Source age/sex and detailed medication variables are unavailable here; target age and disease-stage adjustment was not performed. Case-control ascertainment and label error can inflate apparent source accuracy. Three repeats are insufficient to separate technical from physiological variance. Small samples, only eight source control identifiers, uncertain source identity documentation, fixed class proportions, selected baselines and one external cohort limit inference. A null transfer result does not rule out other summaries or larger studies.

## Future test, not performed
A prospective multi-site study could collect at least 10 standardized sustained phonations per person, record age, sex, medication, disease stage and microphone metadata, repeat on another day, and estimate technical versus biological variance with a common raw-audio extraction pipeline. A frozen subject-level model should then be evaluated in a new institution against perturbation means, nonlinear measures and clinical assessment, with prespecified calibration and discrimination endpoints. This would test whether reliable repeat-phonation instability carries physiological information rather than protocol signatures. No such study was performed or authorized here.
