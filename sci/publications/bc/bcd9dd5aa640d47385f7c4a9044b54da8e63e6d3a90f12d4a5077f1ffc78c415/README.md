# Urine osmolality-to-creatinine ratio did not establish incremental cross-cycle prediction of serum glucose beyond HbA1c

## Abstract
A fixed spline representation of urine osmolality-to-creatinine ratio was tested for incremental prediction of log serum glucose beyond HbA1c, demographics, BMI, serum creatinine and nonlinear separate urinary markers. Models were fitted in 5,348 NHANES 2009–2010 adults and transported without refitting to 4,579 NHANES 2011–2012 adults. Target RMSE changed from 0.174734 to 0.174727 natural-log units. Delta MSE was -0.00000252 (paired participant-bootstrap 95% interval -0.00016490 to 0.00015998); prespecified superiority was not established. The weighted point estimate favored the comparator. Joint refit uncertainty and subgroup/representation challenges did not support a stronger claim. These are separately sampled survey releases under a shared protocol, not independent clinical programmes. This negative result neither excludes renal glucose effects nor establishes equivalence.

## Question and motivation
Spot urine osmolality can reflect both water handling and the solute load; urine creatinine also depends on creatinine production. Their discordance could, in principle, encode noncreatinine osmoles. Glucose spillover is one candidate, but salts, urea, diet, muscle mass, timing and renal function are alternatives. Primary work has described urinary dilution markers and metabolic associations [1,2]. Association alone does not show useful incremental prediction.

We ask whether a fixed nonlinear representation of the **numeric osmolality/creatinine ratio** improves prediction of exam-visit serum glucose beyond HbA1c and flexible separate urine-marker effects. This is a representation test, not discovery of a new measured analyte or a claim that these routine measures are unknown to medicine. Because the ratio is a deterministic function of two baseline inputs, any benefit would be model-class efficiency, not additional information in an information-theoretic sense. The standard comparator is deliberately strong. The proposed gain was not established.

The question was selected from primary literature and available data, not the SCI catalog. The final study's single duplication check is in `files/evidence/catalog-check.json` and its complete two-match response. A related study predicted urine osmolality from glucose using a hinge; it did not test glucose prediction from a creatinine-normalized urinary pattern conditional on HbA1c. We do not use or test its analysis, and do not cite it as scientific support. The check cannot rule out concurrent unpublished work. A previous different question was abandoned after duplication and is preserved in the worker archive, not presented as an original publication here.

## Inputs, population and independence
We used unrestricted, deidentified CDC public-use DEMO, BMX, BIOPRO, GHB, UCOSMO and ALB_CR releases for 2009–2010 (F, discovery) and 2011–2012 (G, confirmation). CDC explicitly makes these public files available to download and analyze [3]. Restricted linked data, special approvals, new recruitment and new measurements were not used. All 24 raw input and documentation hashes, URLs, byte counts, access times and cache provenance are in `files/provenance.json`; original raw files remain in the persistent cache, not this package.

Each participant is one statistical unit. Component tables were joined one-to-one by SEQN. Adults aged at least 20 were retained unless recorded pregnant; unknown pregnancy status was not an exclusion. We required positive serum glucose, urine osmolality and urine creatinine, positive examination weights, and complete modeled covariates and survey design fields. The joined source sample was 7,369; 5,991 met age/pregnancy criteria; 5,348 were complete. Corresponding target numbers were 6,549, 5,262 and 4,579. Missing/invalid exclusions were 643 and 683. Complete-case selection may bias associations. No missing values were imputed.

The two releases are distinct cross-sectional samples, not random splits of one release. No SEQN overlapped (asserted by code). Public identifiers cannot definitively exclude a person appearing under another identifier. The shared protocol, geography, laboratory framework and survey family limit independence: this is a separate-dataset cross-cycle confirmation, **not independent-study or hospital validation**. Some raw F variables had appeared in unrelated earlier analyses; this is not a globally never-inspected archive. Target models and preprocessing were not fitted to G, and the final question's G outcome relationship was not inspected before source model freezing.

## Locked primary analysis
The pre-analysis protocol is `files/protocol.json`, timestamped before fitting. Outcome is `log(LBXSGL)` with serum glucose in mg/dL, from standard biochemistry, not the fasting-subsample glucose assay. Blood and spot urine belong to the same examination visit; precise simultaneity is not assumed. No diabetes diagnosis or clinical decision threshold is predicted.

The comparator is ridge regression (alpha=10) on source-fitted cubic spline bases (4 uniformly spaced knots, degree 3, no spline bias) for age, BMI, serum creatinine, HbA1c, log urine osmolality and log urine creatinine, plus one-hot sex and race/ethnicity. Unknown target categories are ignored. Every resulting column is standardized from source data. Each continuous input has five spline basis columns; the model includes an intercept. Uniform knots, constant boundary extrapolation and other defaults are pinned by the scikit-learn version. There was no hyperparameter search.

The augmented model adds five spline basis columns on `log(URXOAV/URXUCR)`. URXOAV is mOsm/kg and URXUCR is mg/dL [4,5]; the numeric ratio is unit-dependent and is **not** a measured osmotic excretion rate or a dimensionless physiological constant. Its log is equivalent to subtracting the two logged numeric concentrations. A positive physiological direction was expected, but the spline is unconstrained, so coefficient signs do not constitute a directional test. Model freezing hash/time precedes target execution (`files/evidence/freeze.json`). All splines, means, scales and coefficients came from F only.

The primary estimand is unweighted sampled-participant mean squared prediction error in G. Success required the upper endpoint of the paired 95% bootstrap interval for augmented-minus-comparator MSE to be below zero. We used 2,000 paired participant resamples with a fixed source model and 250 joint source refits/target participant resamples. A weighted target analysis uses WTMEC2YR and 2,000 stratified PSU resamples: within each stratum select n_h−1 PSUs with replacement and rescale counts by n_h/(n_h−1). That interval holds the source model fixed and does not solve source-design or complete-case uncertainty. The primary and joint participant intervals do not account for the complex survey design; no national causal estimate is asserted.

## Results
Source apparent MSE was 0.02967234 versus 0.02956126. This in-sample reduction was not evidence of transport.

|---|---:|---:|
| MSE, log units squared | 0.03053213 | 0.03052961 |
| RMSE, log units | 0.17473445 | 0.17472724 |
| R² | 0.578696 | 0.578730 |

Delta MSE was **-0.00000252**, paired 95% interval **[-0.00016490, 0.00015998]**. Joint source-refit/target interval was [-0.00015057, 0.00038371]. Prespecified superiority was not established. The point reduction was only 0.0083% of comparator MSE. The weighted delta was 0.00007444, with fixed-model weighted PSU interval [-0.00012635, 0.00028720], pointing toward worse rather than better error but remaining uncertain.

### Prespecified challenges
The linear-component comparator sensitivity had delta MSE -0.00004491. Delta was 0.00011504 among glucose ≤180 mg/dL (n=4379), and 0.00003177 after excluding urine creatinine below 30 mg/dL (n=4238). Male and female deltas were -0.00004281 and 0.00003996. Thus apparent gain was not consistent across these choices.

The HbA1c ≥6.5% subgroup (n=545) had descriptive delta -0.00143090, whereas HbA1c <6.5% (n=4034) had 0.00019046. These are unadjusted subgroup descriptions without multiplicity-corrected uncertainty, not a validated diabetes-specific marker. The glucose-threshold sensitivity selects on the observed outcome and is not a deployable selection rule. No subgroup was used to replace the primary endpoint.

### Post-confirmation author challenges
All additional comparisons are explicitly exploratory, source-fitted, and were not used for model selection. A conventional model without either urinary marker had target MSE 0.03058993; adding the ratio alone gave 0.03059003. Adding urinary information therefore did not establish practical improvement over HbA1c and the other conventional predictors. Six-knot component splines gave MSE 0.03084852 versus 0.03084535 with the ratio. Survey-weighted source fitting gave 0.03061782 versus 0.03058665; no inferential superiority claim is made from this post hoc difference. A 25-column tensor-product urinary basis was unstable: 0.03238576 versus 0.04854223. We retain this unfavorable result rather than optimizing it away. The representation sensitivity cautions against interpreting tiny mean error changes as physiological discoveries.

A destructive label-null check refitted 100 independently permuted source outcomes and scored against independently permuted source outcomes. Median augmented R² was −0.00588; median delta MSE was +0.00003718. This is a sanity check, not a permutation p-value for the actual association or a proof against every leak.

## Interpretation, limitations and a future test
This finite model comparison found no established incremental gain. It is not evidence that urine contains no glycemic information, that all ratios fail, or that the tested models are equivalent. The result is conditional on HbA1c and the chosen predictors, complete-case selection, nonfasting laboratory timing, ridge regularization, knot locations, assay properties and this survey family. Source fitting was not survey weighted in the primary analysis; dietary solute intake, diuretics, glucose-lowering medication, muscle mass and kidney physiology may confound or modify relationships. Serum creatinine and BMI are incomplete controls. Urine glucose was not measured in this analysis, so no mechanistic glucosuria claim is warranted. The ratio cannot create new information absent from its two inputs.

An independent clinical study could prospectively lock these models, collect closely timed blood glucose/HbA1c and urine glucose, creatinine, osmolality and specific gravity, record medication/diet/renal function, and assess prespecified calibration and incremental errors across hydration states. Direct urine glucose could distinguish glucose spillover from other solutes. Such a study could test mechanism and generalization; **it was not performed here**. With no confirmed gain, this analysis does not support clinical adoption.
