# ST-depression–angina coupling did not improve cross-site coronary-disease prediction

## Abstract
A literature-motivated continuous ST-depression by exercise-angina interaction was tested beyond flexible additive clinical measurements. Models trained in the UCI Cleveland database (303 patients) were transferred without target fitting to the Hungarian database (261 complete cases). Target Brier score increased from 0.157613 to 0.159604: augmented-minus-baseline change +0.001992, source-and-target stratified refit-bootstrap 95% interval [-0.002243, 0.014796]. Source cross-validation likewise showed no improvement. These historically reused, institutionally distinct databases support a narrow negative incremental-prediction result, not equivalence, a clinical recommendation, or lifetime-untouched confirmation.

## Scientific question and motivation
Does the joint expression of exercise-induced ST depression and angina contain useful coronary-disease information beyond their separate nonlinear associations and conventional predictors? A primary study in selected unstable-angina patients reported useful diagnostic specificity for combined ST depression and exercise angina [1]. The Duke treadmill work also combines ST deviation and angina additively with exercise duration [2]. We therefore test one continuous interaction, not a search over target-selected features. The contribution is a reproducible incremental-prediction and cross-site test; we do not claim to discover symptom–ECG concordance, that all current analyses ignore interactions, or to reproduce those papers' populations or endpoints. A potentially useful pattern can fail to improve transport prediction. We retain that failure.

The question and analysis plan were recorded at the actual timestamp in `files/evidence/question-registration.json`, before this candidate's outcome scoring. One SCI metadata duplication check queried a bounded response of 1000 publications for angina with ST-depression terms and found no matches. Its exact query, UTC time and limitations are in `catalog-check.json`. This is not an exhaustive full-text search and cannot exclude concurrent unpublished studies. No SCI publication supplied the question or was used/tested; none is cited.

## Population, outcome and exclusions
The unit is a patient row in these historical clinically selected, angiography-linked databases, not the general population. Outcome is released `num>0` versus zero, reflecting angiographic disease rather than future cardiovascular events; the dictionary describes the endpoint by coronary diameter narrowing around a 50% boundary. We do not reinterpret it as ischemia mechanism, stenosis severity or treatment need.

There are 303/303 included Cleveland patients (139 disease-positive) and 261/294 Hungarian patients (98 disease-positive), after requiring complete age, recorded sex, chest-pain category, resting blood pressure, cholesterol, fasting-blood-sugar indicator, resting ECG category, maximum exercise heart rate, exercise-induced angina and exercise-induced ST depression relative to rest. Positive blood pressure, cholesterol and heart rate are also required. Hungarian missing counts are age0, BP1, cholesterol23, heart rate1, ST0, sex0, chest pain0, fasting-blood-sugar8, ECG1, angina1; exclusions overlap, so these counts do not sum to33. No primary predictor has a nonpositive BP/cholesterol/heart-rate exclusion in the Hungarian file. All missing values are parsed from `?`; no target imputation occurs.

Coronary-vessel counts and thallium results are excluded because they are outcome-adjacent or poorly shared modalities. ST slope is a clinically relevant standard measure with extensive target missingness; an adjusted complete-case sensitivity addresses it. Exercise duration and exercise-limiting angina index are not available in the harmonized 14-column processed files. Thus we cannot calculate or claim to outperform the Duke treadmill score. We compare relevant available standard measurements, not all clinical standard-of-care information.

## Frozen model and leakage prevention
The additive baseline contains cubic splines for age, BP, cholesterol, maximum heart rate and ST depression, each with four uniform knots, degree3, no spline bias and linear extrapolation. Source-fitted spline columns are standardized. Categorical sex, chest pain, fasting-blood-sugar status, resting ECG and exercise angina use fixed valid coding levels, dropping the first level. There are 33 baseline columns. Logistic regression minimizes summed log loss plus an L2 penalty, `C=1`, with an unpenalized intercept; tolerance1e-10, maximum3000 iterations.

The augmented model adds exactly one column:

`z = standardize_source[((ST - mean_source(ST))/sd_source(ST)) * (angina - mean_source(angina))]`.

Scaling and all preprocessing are refitted only within each training fold or bootstrap source draw. Both models use exactly the same baseline columns and the same penalty. Interactions may change all fitted coefficients; they are not causal effect modifiers. Source-only five-fold stratified CV uses shuffle seed817. Target prediction uses the full source fit without target labels, calibration, hyperparameter tuning or target-fitted normalization. The primary endpoint is target Brier difference (augmented minus baseline; negative favors the interaction); AUC and log loss are secondary. No winning sensitivity model replaces the primary model.

Uncertainty uses 2000 paired target bootstrap draws, stratified by disease class, holding trained models fixed, plus 300 independent source-and-target stratified bootstrap draws with complete source preprocessing/refitting. Seed251125 fixes the analysis RNG. Percentile95% intervals are reported. These bootstrap intervals condition on observed class counts; they do not propagate uncertain prevalence, historical selection, missing-data mechanisms, institution effects or researcher dataset reuse. A single source/target pair cannot estimate between-institution heterogeneity. Small-sample bootstrap coverage is not guaranteed; no multiplicity-adjusted significance claim is made.

## Challenges and author self-checks
All main sensitivity Brier changes remained positive: stronger regularization C=.1, +0.001600; weaker C=10, +0.000773; linear baseline, +0.002664; women (n69,12 cases), +0.004018; men (n192,86 cases), +0.001263; ST-slope-adjusted complete cases (source303,target95), +0.001120; and restricting ST to released values0–4 (source298,target260), +0.001636. The slope sensitivity has a severely selected target and does not repair missing-data bias. Some secondary AUC/log-loss sensitivities change direction; they do not override the frozen Brier endpoint.

The linear baseline actually transferred better than the spline baseline (Brier0.135490 versus0.157613). This is a warning that flexible fitting can hurt transport in small datasets, not evidence that the source-selected spline model is optimal. Nevertheless the added interaction failed against both. A single shuffled-source-label diagnostic produced target AUC0.4021/0.4030, eliminating strong positive discrimination; it is not a calibrated permutation p-value or proof against all leakage.

Author checks recomputed Brier, AUC and log loss from saved patient predictions; recomputed both bootstrap percentile intervals; checked unique row indices, binary labels, identical baseline design columns, one added column, finite predictions, input hashes, and manual rank-based AUC. ## Limitations and interpretation
This result is about incremental prediction from a small historical clinical database pair. Referral and verification selection, medication effects (including beta-blockade), treatment access, exercise protocol/workload differences, laboratory differences and missingness could obscure or create interactions. Medication and exercise-duration adjustments are unavailable in this analysis. Complete-case selection excludes33 target patients; missingness is not modeled. Source and target have different disease prevalence and predictor ranges. We did not recalibrate to target prevalence, which would require a separate calibration cohort. Angina is subjective and recorded sex is binary; no broader sex/gender inference is supported. Distinct institutions are not independent measurement technologies. There is no contemporary cohort or longitudinal outcome validation.

The evidence does not establish a new biomarker or justify clinical decisions. It narrows the conjecture: this one linear-product interaction does not yield an observed gain beyond these additive measurements under frozen cross-site fitting. Other nonlinear concordance representations, standardized workloads and explicit symptom timing remain untested; target-driven exploration of those features here would require a new independent dataset for confirmation.

## Future clinical test (not performed)
A future observational clinical study could preregister the model, record exercise duration, medication, workload, symptom timing and ST trajectories under standardized protocols, and test externally calibrated incremental Brier prediction against the actual Duke score and a full contemporary clinical model, using an independently recruited validation site. Investigators could assess missingness, overlap, subgroup calibration and prespecified clinical decision benefit. Such a study is proposed only; we collected no data and conducted no clinical experiment.
