# Bilirubin–cotinine coupling does not improve conditional airflow-ratio prediction across two NHANES samples

## Abstract and scoped claim
A literature-motivated product of centered log serum bilirubin and log(1+cotinine) was tested for information about contemporaneous airflow beyond flexible separate biomarkers and conventional predictors. In 3,546 NHANES 2007–08 adults, adding the product increased fivefold mean squared error (MSE) by 0.07247 percentage-point². A frozen model tested in 3,957 separately sampled 2009–10 adults increased MSE by 0.02589 percentage-point²; a joint source-refit/target stratified-PSU bootstrap 95% interval was [−0.02916,+0.19549]. The specified stable-improvement criterion failed. We found no reproducible predictive benefit for this particular representation. This is not an equivalence test, a causal result, a refutation of bilirubin biology, or independent clinical-cohort validation.

## Question, rationale, and relationship to literature
Bilirubin has been associated with respiratory outcomes in an observational health-record cohort ([Horsfall et al., JAMA 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21325185/)), lung function in SAPALDIA ([Curjuric et al., ERJ 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24177000/)), and genetic variation with smoking-stratified respiratory health ([Horsfall et al., ERJ 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25086287/)). These motivate, but do not establish, a protective bilirubin signal conditional on tobacco exposure. We ask whether an objective serum exposure interaction contributes beyond nonlinear additive bilirubin, cotinine, smoking status and demographic/liver measurements. This is a representation test using existing analytes, not discovery of a new biomarker or a claim that the interaction has never been considered.

Question and dataset needs were locally registered before the single catalog search (protocol.json and duplication-check.json). That check found a related bilirubin–albumin/FEV1 paper, not this exposure interaction/airflow-ratio comparison. We did not use or test that publication. The search cannot exclude unpublished concurrent work. This question was selected from primary literature and data, not catalog ranking.

## Methods and prior decision rule
Outcome:100×baseline(pre-bronchodilator)FEV1/FVC, in percentage points. This is not COPD diagnosis or longitudinal decline. CDC's [SPX documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2007/DataFiles/SPX_E.htm) describes measurements and quality. FEV1/FVC itself is a standard measure; its components are NOT entered as predictors, which would leak the answer.

The baseline predicts this measure from age,height,BMI, log bilirubin(mg/dL),log(1+cotinine ng/mL),albumin(g/dL),ALT(U/L),sex,recorded race/ethnicity and smoking status. Each continuous variable has a four-knot cubic spline, knots at source quantiles, no bias column, followed by standardization. Categories use one-hot encoding; unknown target categories are ignored. Standardized male×centered-age and male×centered-height products are also included. Both models use Ridge(alpha=10), with unpenalized intercept. The candidate adds one standardized product of source-centered log bilirubin and log(1+cotinine). Transformations and centering are learned exclusively in the source, separately inside each CV fold. Race/ethnicity is a contextual adjustment, not a biological mechanism.

Positive interaction was anticipated: a more positive bilirubin slope with higher tobacco exposure. Success required lower source fivefold CV MSE, lower target MSE with joint bootstrap upper95% bound<0, and agreement with a six-knot baseline. Fivefold participant CV used shuffle seed481. No target model selection was performed. Fit and primary evaluation are unweighted predictive analyses of eligible participants, not national population estimates. Survey-weighted target evaluation is a sensitivity analysis, not a full weighted-training analysis.

Uncertainty:500 independent joint source/target stratified-PSU rescaled bootstrap draws(seed20261118). Within each stratum sample n−1 PSUs with replacement and scale by n/(n−1). Source strata have exactly two PSUs (checked), permitting exact integer expansion; all source preprocessing and model coefficients are refitted. Target losses receive independently drawn PSU weights. Intervals are empirical2.5/97.5 percentiles, not multiplicity-adjusted. Subgroup results are descriptive, not separately powered hypotheses.

## Author checks, limitations, and future test
The author checked one-to-one merges, outcome plausibility, source-only transformations, no identifier overlap, bootstrap structure, independent paired-loss arithmetic and ridge normal equations(coefficient agreement<1e−8). Fresh offline containers reran both stages and all500 bootstrap replicates; central output bytes matched. See author-self-check.json, author-check-results.json and clean-rerun.json. These are same-author checks, not independent peer review.

The interval permits small improvements as well as deterioration. No claim of equivalence, clinical utility, extra information in a deterministic product beyond its inputs, or absence of nonlinear biological effects follows. Unmeasured smoking history, nicotine products, genetics, liver disease, medications, socioeconomic factors and occupational exposures can confound; recent cotinine does not capture lifetime smoke burden. Bilirubin may be consequence rather than cause of health status. A one-term product and ridge penalty may miss a true interaction. Quality/complete-case selection and prior study familiarity limit generalization. The cross-cycle result is not independent-protocol confirmation. We have no evidence here that this pattern predicts physiology better than the baseline.

A future independently operated clinical cohort could preregister this exact frozen interaction, collect repeated bilirubin/cotinine and pack-years alongside quality-assured pre/post-bronchodilator spirometry, and evaluate prospective lung-function decline beyond standard clinical predictors. That could distinguish transient exposure correlation from stable predictive information. No recruitment, measurements, intervention or clinical experiment was performed here.
