# Ferritin–CRP coupling adds no reliable cross-cycle hemoglobin prediction beyond conventional iron and CBC measures

## Abstract
A registered product of standardized log ferritin and log CRP was added to flexible conventional iron, CBC and demographic predictors of hemoglobin in nonpregnant women20–49. Training used NHANES2003–04(n=922); frozen transfer used separately recruited NHANES2005–06(n=998). Weighted MSE decreased0.677043 to0.675608(g/dL)², a0.212% gain; joint source–target survey-PSU bootstrap95% interval for gain was[−0.007054,0.007185]. MAE worsened and source cross-validation mean gain was negative. This feature test does not establish improved physiological inference or clinical utility. Validation is across cycles within one survey, not independent institutions.

## Motivation, question and registration
Inflammation complicates iron-marker interpretation. BRINDA examined inflammation adjustment for soluble transferrin receptor [1]. A primary NHANES analysis estimated ferritin thresholds from hemoglobin and sTfR relationships [2]. These motivate testing whether ferritin's association with contemporaneous hemoglobin varies with CRP after accounting for standard marginal markers. Our candidate is exactly one product, not a newly discovered biological mechanism or a claim that inflammatory interpretation of ferritin is novel. We do not assert that clinical practice ignores inflammation. The narrow question is incremental out-of-cycle prediction from this representation.

## Model, baseline, leakage control and endpoints
The outcome is contemporaneous hemoglobin, not incident anemia or true tissue iron. Baseline continuous covariates: age, BMI, ln ferritin, ln CRP, ln sTfR, log10(1000×sTfR/ferritin), MCV(fL), RDW(%); categorical race/ethnicity RIDRETH1. The log ratio is a conventional body-iron-type index, not the distinct sTfR/log-ferritin index; no assay-specific body iron calibration is claimed. It adds a conventional derived iron representation alongside its component margins. CRP is already in the baseline, so this is not simply a test of adding inflammation to ferritin. Marginal splines accommodate nonlinear marker effects and conventional log-linear adjustments, but this is not a complete BRINDA model: AGP is unavailable. We do not claim comparison against every clinical diagnostic algorithm. Hct, MCH, MCHC and RBC count are excluded to avoid algebraically reconstructing hemoglobin; MCV/RDW capture standard red-cell morphology without directly reconstructing it.

Each continuous covariate uses source-fitted cubic B-splines with3 uniformly spaced knots, no bias, linear extrapolation; categorical encoding ignores unseen levels. The augmented design adds z(ln ferritin)×z(ln CRP), centered/scaled using the unweighted source sample (bootstrap multiplicities in refits). Both designs are source-standardized and ridge-fit at fixed alpha10, using WTMEC2YR normalized to mean1, with an unpenalized intercept. Spline knots/scales/categories are never learned from target data or source held-out folds. There is no target refitting, threshold tuning, pretrained model or participant duplication. The ridge penalty and feature definition were not selected on target results. Source5fold GroupKFold groups stratum×PSU, used diagnostically rather than for selection. Baseline is a flexible statistical comparator made from standard measures, not a validated clinical risk score.

Primary endpoint: survey-weighted target baseline MSE minus augmented MSE, in(g/dL)²; positive favors the new feature. Registered success requires joint source–target95% interval lower bound>0;1% relative MSE gain is a descriptive threshold, not a validated clinical margin. MAE is secondary. All models and checks are retained; negative source CV did not prompt a new candidate search.

Survey-PSU resampling is performed separately within masked strata for each cycle. With m PSUs in a stratum, draw m−1 with replacement and multiply selection counts by m/(m−1), retaining survey weights. There are no singleton complete-case strata.2000 paired target-only replicates give95% percentile gain interval[−0.002887588,0.005191731].300 source full-pipeline refits combined with target resamples give[−0.007054399,0.007184592]; median0.000343862,59% positive. ## Limits and prospective test
This test does not demonstrate hidden physiological information useful beyond standard measures. It does not refute inflammation-dependent ferritin physiology, exclude more suitable interaction models, establish equivalence, or validate replacing a hemoglobin measurement with prediction. Cross-sectional simultaneous blood measurements cannot establish temporal prediction, causality, treatment response, or true storage iron. Menstrual blood loss, iron supplementation, diet, smoking, renal function, inflammation subtype, hemoglobinopathies, other deficiencies, and socioeconomic factors remain possible confounders. Survey weights do not repair complete-case selection; race categories are social/measurement proxies, not biological causes. Shared assay/survey infrastructure, released harmonization and absent AGP constrain claims.

A future independently recruited clinical cohort could freeze this exact baseline and augmentation, measure ferritin, CRP, AGP, sTfR and CBC under standardized assays, document bleeding/supplements/inflammatory diagnoses, and estimate paired prediction error with prespecified precision. Repeated measurements and reference iron-status assessment could test whether apparent ferritin–inflammation coupling reflects stable iron restriction or transient inflammation. Such a study, particularly longitudinal or interventional work needed for causal claims, is only proposed here; none was conducted.
