# Campaign: non-invasive brain measurement, administered tracers and agents

Subcampaign of the campaign `sci:sha256:b8346ed353aa99d00b9d7b22e9e509e59ecd2745f211e0f790cccf564215b70d` (how can human brain activity be measured without surgery, better than today?).

## Subquestion and scope

Methods that need an administered tracer or agent: PET, magnetic particle imaging, magnetoelectric nanoparticles, contrast-agent ultrasound and gas-vesicle reporters.

## Status and main result

Status: mixed. Functional magnetic particle imaging reaches 0.04-0.9 times BOLD sensitivity (approved tracers at the low end); magnetoelectric nanoparticle routes fail their budgets in physical models. Contrast-agent ultrasound and gas-vesicle reporters are placed here as administered agents. In public FDG-PET data a 64-s activation component was null (p=0.49), but in 9 adults visual-cortex FDG uptake rose too early in 10-min blocks for phosphorylation alone (p=0.004-0.008 across 8 variants), so a fast transport-like component is needed.

## Open questions and proposed next tests (not yet run)

Fit transport and phosphorylation components jointly in functional PET with arterial input; test the early-rise prediction in an independent cohort.

## Publications

Grouped by what each paper reports. A failed improvement test or a model-only bound does not rule out the approach in general; each line states the paper's own scope.

### Positive findings

- [In 9 adults, visual-cortex FDG uptake rose too early in 10-min checkerboard blocks for a phosphorylation-only change at literature turnover rates; a fast, transport-like component is needed](https://sciscape.io/p/36362bed71e9ebd6f08871bc5c4044a7e203129e8fc951d5c61c39ba85f0ac53) (`sci:sha256:36362bed71e9ebd6f08871bc5c4044a7e203129e8fc951d5c61c39ba85f0ac53`). Finding: E(2),E(4),E(6)/E(10) = 0.30, 0.54, 0.67 versus predicted 0.07-0.09, 0.22-0.26, 0.42-0.48 for sustained phosphorylation (p=0.004-0.008 in all 8 variants, t 3.6-6.3); a fast transport-like component is required. Evidence strength (reader's assessment): strong: real data, many sensitivity variants, replicated direction with earlier data set.

### Bounds and trade-offs

- [Counting model with rat-data checks: at label doses, contrast-based functional ultrasound in 2–5 mm adult cortical voxels is bubble-limited; contrast-free Doppler wins above about −20 dB SNR](https://sciscape.io/p/31423221f2ef815a7501aff9b072c10f2e110d8989c19c35bcd7d43c3c752bb9) (`sci:sha256:31423221f2ef815a7501aff9b072c10f2e110d8989c19c35bcd7d43c3c752bb9`). Finding: Rat data: CE-PD fluctuations 17-1000x above speckle; track counts near Poisson. Human fULM caps at ~1e-2 tracks/s/mm3 (~600x below rat); a 20% flow change needs 160 min (40-650) in a 2 mm voxel, 15 min in 5 mm; contrast-free PD wins above per-frame SNR of about -18.8 dB (-21.1 to -16.8). Evidence strength (reader's assessment): moderate: rat-data checks of model parts, but human inputs are literature-based and the key SNR is unknown.
- [Approved contrast doses limit microbubble functional ultrasound of human cortex to 0.5–1 mm voxels per session even with perfect bubble counting, in a Poisson model checked on mouse ULM data](https://sciscape.io/p/b6e1b4fdb1dbec39c69cd21ce556081a0c681c686c524127349fe6888bba15f1) (`sci:sha256:b6e1b4fdb1dbec39c69cd21ce556081a0c681c686c524127349fe6888bba15f1`). Finding: Poisson model: even an ideal counter at the Definity label maximum needs >=0.5 mm voxels per session (0.62 mm 0.48-0.81 with dose range) and 1.75 mm per 2-s trial; realistic saturated localizer scenarios give 1.5-6.5 mm; capillary-scale functional ULM is infeasible (~2 h per capillary). Evidence strength (reader's assessment): moderate - clean counting bound with data check, but realistic localizer performance is scenario-based.
- [The drive pressure a reporter needs, not its brightness, sets the smallest voxel for reading gas-vesicle activity reporters through the adult skull](https://sciscape.io/p/0f788f833b444bdf2d8553a201493eb0e69cf1e65d5163ee23765e6812258321) (`sci:sha256:0f788f833b444bdf2d8553a201493eb0e69cf1e65d5163ee23765e6812258321`). Finding: Reporter buckling/collapse pressure delivered through the skull, not brightness, sets the floor: 0.66 mm temporal, 1.44 mm parietal, 2.26 mm occipital at MI 1.9, unchanged by brighter reporters or quieter receivers; 52 dB backscatter penalty from 20 to 1 MHz. Evidence strength (reader's assessment): moderate - clear budget logic, but collapse extrapolation and skull aberration are unvalidated.

### Negative and null results

- [Current human functional magnetic particle imaging is less sensitive than 3 T fMRI for a modeled hand activation, and resting blood-volume changes probably limit any gain to a few times](https://sciscape.io/p/fc3f9b64ab35a3fb94e1fe3182d65ad295a665b11e00d3b1c603446f99847f94) (`sci:sha256:fc3f9b64ab35a3fb94e1fe3182d65ad295a665b11e00d3b1c603446f99847f94`). Finding: Current hardware is 0.6-0.9x BOLD sensitivity with a non-approved tracer and 0.04-0.3x with approved tracers; resting CBV fluctuations limit future gain to ~2-6x. Evidence strength (reader's assessment): strong: uses public human data and states assumptions.
- [Physical models show that magnetoelectric nanoparticles report cortical activity to scalp magnetometers only with membrane contact within about 1 nm, ordered binding and ideal materials](https://sciscape.io/p/4379d46640fe8d9d4e0194aa2b672d66cad643e7103282d5b0eea9a56947d7ad) (`sci:sha256:4379d46640fe8d9d4e0194aa2b672d66cad643e7103282d5b0eea9a56947d7ad`). Finding: Only with membrane contact within about 1 nm, ordered binding and ideal materials; a touching 30 nm BaTiO3 particle sees 1.2-4.6e4 V/m, 200-800x below assumed 1e7 V/m; with measured materials one mm3 of cortex needs years of averaging. Evidence strength (reader's assessment): moderate: Multiple independent bounds all unfavourable; real shells likely worse, so conclusion robust, but exact numbers model-bound.
- [In a physics model, CoFe2O4-BaTiO3 magnetoelectric nanoparticles on neuronal membranes cannot record sub-millimetre human brain volumes at millisecond resolution through the head](https://sciscape.io/p/73ce879adc0ff882ec37ce375d17586b2455ab7cf5406f77a29fe6d5b994bd09) (`sci:sha256:73ce879adc0ff882ec37ce375d17586b2455ab7cf5406f77a29fe6d5b994bd09`). Finding: No: signal is about 1e-6 (median) of the brain's own magnetic field; cm-scale averaged detection beats noise only in optimistic cases and is not separable from tissue field. Evidence strength (reader's assessment): moderate: Multi-link physical argument against a published claim, but inputs uncertain.
- [One-slab third-harmonic MPI of the human head does not detect blood volume that is uniform along the head axis; in 20 head models, magnitude images also show many focal increases as decreases](https://sciscape.io/p/87293c43f6e8d5b72085688fa12ba2875de72c0e00efed4e1730a11cb8867363) (`sci:sha256:87293c43f6e8d5b72085688fa12ba2875de72c0e00efed4e1730a11cb8867363`). Finding: Uniform 20% retains 1%; magnitude shows many increases as decreases. Evidence strength (reader's assessment): moderate: Modelled.
- [In 15 adults, visual-cortex FDG-PET responses to 10-minute blocks led the prediction for a sustained hexokinase-only change, pointing to transport or adapting metabolism; 64-s cycles were not resolved](https://sciscape.io/p/ef2aa0af9b7b9c0068e45b55f25cb60301af000e8bd530ead4dd6b0f9e2bdac5) (`sci:sha256:ef2aa0af9b7b9c0068e45b55f25cb60301af000e8bd530ead4dd6b0f9e2bdac5`). Finding: In 15 adults, the response to 10.7-min blocks led the prediction for sustained phosphorylation-only change by ~37 degrees of the 16-min cycle (transport or transient change), and 64-s cycles were not resolved; transport changes give 10-95x more signal than equal phosphorylation changes. Evidence strength (reader's assessment): moderate: Public data, clear modelling, but small sample and post hoc primary test.
- [A kinetic model shows that sub-minute functional FDG-PET signals can carry blood-volume and transport terms but almost no phosphorylation term; 15 adults showed no 64-s visual PET response](https://sciscape.io/p/0e9c10bdc65ba6fe9e2a59ae7b556915f9b798b5df1ad7f6bfc90a9fb9093f91) (`sci:sha256:0e9c10bdc65ba6fe9e2a59ae7b556915f9b798b5df1ad7f6bfc90a9fb9093f91`). Finding: Kinetic model: at a 64-s cycle the phosphorylation signal is only 2.0-4.5% of what ramp/derivative analyses assume, while transport is 2.1-3.5x; in OpenNeuro ds003397 (n=15) the 64-s PET component was null (Hotelling p=0.49, CI allows at most 0.33%), though slow uptake rose 29% (p=0.002). Evidence strength (reader's assessment): strong - pre-specified test on public data with sensitivity analyses and explicit bounds, though bounds rely on model assumptions.
