# ST-depression–angina coupling does not reliably improve cross-center coronary disease prediction

## Abstract
A fixed ST-depression × exercise-angina interaction was tested beyond additive clinical/exercise measures in public angiography cohorts. Cleveland-trained models (303 participants) were evaluated without target fitting in VA Long Beach (200 participants). The interaction improved discovery out-of-fold log loss by 0.00442 but worsened external log loss by 0.01636 (paired fixed-model improvement interval −0.03072 to −0.00087). Joint training/test bootstrap improvement interval was −0.07152 to 0.00521. Spline, penalty, complete-case, zero-value and deduplication checks did not establish benefit. This negative result applies to one representation and historical selected cohorts; it does not prove absence of physiological symptom–electrical coupling or universal harm.

## Question and prior evidence
Can symptom–electrical coupling carry incremental information about angiographic coronary disease beyond the separate symptom and ECG measurements? The CASS study of exercise-related silent ischemia [1] and Miranda et al.'s comparison of symptomatic and silent responses [2] motivate a joint representation: ST depression multiplied by exercise angina. They do not establish its incremental transfer performance. Neither the physiological idea nor the use of these datasets for international prediction is new: Detrano et al. previously studied international probability-model transport [3]. We test a deliberately simple interaction omitted by an additive conventional-measure comparator. We do **not** claim current clinical analyses universally ignore coupling or that these variables are new biomarkers.

Selection was literature/data-first. An initial, different chronotropic-interaction question was found duplicated in SCI and abandoned; its calculations remain in the local named archive. The replacement was chosen from primary silent-ischemia literature, then one catalog metadata duplication query was performed (`silent ischemia angina interaction`, no matches). This limited conjunctive search is not proof of novelty or absence of concurrent work. No SCI publication is used as evidence or tested here. Budapest had been examined in the abandoned attempt and was excluded from this study, rather than described as untouched confirmation. See files/selection/.

## Data, population and independence
UCI Heart Disease [4] supplies separately collected institutional databases, not random splits of one participant sample. We trained on all 303 Cleveland rows (139 cases) and confirmed on all 200 VA Long Beach rows (149 cases). Both are historical angiography-referred patients, not population screening cohorts. Outcome is released `num>0`, representing angiographic disease under the repository narrowing definition, not myocardial infarction, future events, functional ischemia, or symptom severity. Unit is one released participant row; no repeated test modeling is performed.

Separate institutions support cross-cohort validation, although the datasets share a repository, era, and study collaboration. Names/identifiers are unavailable; no exact full predictor-vector match was found across sites. This does not rule out overlapping individuals. One identical full VA row is present; a removal sensitivity uses 199 rows. Prior literature [2] is also from Long Beach and may overlap this historical population, so this is not an independent empirical replication of that article; it is cross-cohort validation of a model trained at Cleveland.

## Locked methods and prediction
Before inspecting VA outcomes, we locked a positive interaction-coefficient prediction and improved external log loss. Baseline: ridge logistic regression (C=1, intercept, no class reweighting) using age, sex, chest-pain category, resting BP, cholesterol, fasting glucose indicator, resting ECG category, maximum exercise heart rate, exercise angina and ST depression (`oldpeak`). Categorical terms are fixed one-hot levels, including explicit missing category; continuous terms use training medians and training standardization. Augmentation adds only `oldpeak*exang`, computed before imputation then median-imputed and scaled with the continuous terms. Thus a missing component produces a missing interaction; we do not infer absence of angina. Training did not involve VA labels, preprocessing, thresholds or parameter selection. Angiographic vessel count and thallium were excluded to avoid near-outcome information and extreme target missingness. Exercise ST slope is included in a sensitivity; the comparator is conventional clinical measurements, not a validated deployed guideline score. The Duke treadmill score cannot be computed from these processed files because exercise duration is absent.

Primary statistic is average log loss baseline minus augmented (positive favors augmentation). We use 2,000 paired participant bootstrap resamples of the target predictions with percentile 95% intervals conditional on the fitted models. A separately locked 500-replicate joint bootstrap resamples Cleveland and VA independently, refitting both models each draw. Both use fixed random seeds; no multiplicity-adjusted exploratory inference is claimed. Secondary metrics are AUROC, Brier score, observed prevalence and mean predicted risk. Cleveland stratified five-fold OOF predictions are discovery only, not independent validation. Success required positive interaction and a primary improvement interval excluding zero above it.

In the locked VA comparison, AUROC changed from 0.727464 to 0.727991 (a small increase despite worse loss); Brier score worsened from 0.185782 to 0.190944. Observed case prevalence was 0.745, versus mean predicted risks 0.681266 and 0.669839. Thus fixed-fit worsening should not be promoted to a general claim that fitting this interaction always harms performance. No prespecified sensitivity established incremental log-loss benefit.

## Missingness, challenges and author checks
Cleveland has no missing values among primary predictors. VA has 56 missing ST-depression values, 53 missing exercise-angina and peak-HR values, 56 missing resting BP values, and seven missing cholesterol and fasting-glucose values each. The interaction is missing for 56 rows. Complete-case evaluation uses 130 participants; it also does not establish benefit. VA contains 49 zero cholesterol values and one zero resting BP; treating these as missing shifts absolute calibration markedly but leaves the improvement negative. ST slope is missing in 102 VA rows, which limits that sensitivity. Sex transport is poor: 206/303 Cleveland and 194/200 VA participants are male; no useful female-specific inference is possible.

Cubic splines (four knots, degree three) for age, peak HR and ST depression challenge an overly linear comparator. Penalties C=0.1 and 10 challenge a single ridge choice. Male-only and complete-case analyses challenge composition and imputation. A 1,000-iteration target-label permutation had an upper-tail statistic of 0.000999 relative to its own null, but this destroys both models' associations and is **not** a conditional interaction-significance test; it cannot override the negative primary comparison. Zero BP handling and duplicate removal were added after the primary result and are labeled exploratory. No sensitivity was promoted into a new primary result.

The author personally checked source documentation, outcome direction, forbidden near-outcome exclusions, train-only preprocessing, formula identity, finite predictions, cohort row counts, exact-vector overlap, missingness and ranges. A separate implementation of log loss and pairwise AUROC agreed with sklearn to 1e-12. Final computations were run twice in fresh restricted containers; all result JSON and prediction CSVs were byte-identical. These are **author self-checks**, not independent peer review. Full numeric results, code, plan/addendum, checks and rerun evidence are supplied.

## Interpretation and limits
This is a negative incremental-prediction result for one explicit interaction, not evidence that the body has no symptom–electrical coupling. A fitted positive coefficient in discovery was insufficient to ensure probability-forecast benefit under cohort shift. Low power, correlated predictors, ridge parameterization, age/sex/referral differences, antianginal medication, workload, protocols and measurement missingness could explain instability. Medication and exercise-duration information are unavailable in the processed inputs used here. Complete-case selection may be nonrandom; raw zero encodings materially affect calibration. Exact duplicate-vector checks are not identity assurance. Bootstrap intervals assume exchangeable participant rows, have only 51 target noncases and do not capture study-site sampling uncertainty; two sites cannot establish broad transportability. We did not compare all flexible learners, perform clinical decision-curve analysis or demonstrate clinical utility. The fixed representation itself is not a novel physiological mechanism.

A future prospective multicenter clinical study could preregister a symptom–electrical interaction, record exercise protocol, workload, medication and repeated symptom/ECG timing, compare against established treadmill scores and assess calibration and decision utility in an untouched center. Such a study could distinguish severity, sensory response and treatment-related confounding. It has **not** been performed here. No diagnostic or treatment recommendation follows.
