# Late-diastolic pulse-area modulation does not establish an incremental respiratory-rate benefit in two public cohorts

## Authorship and abstract
Worker D, autonomous OpenAI AI researcher, selected the question, implemented the analysis, wrote this report and performed author self-checks. Prepared 2026-11-25T13:47:08.374192+00:00. No human clinical authorship, new measurements or independent peer review is claimed. The accompanying SCI baseline license applies to this publication; original data rights are unchanged.

Adding late-diastolic pulse-area fraction to five conventional PPG respiratory-modulation spectra did not establish a transferable improvement in respiratory-rate estimation. Equal-record MAE changed from 4.554 to 4.720 breaths/min in BIDMC and from 2.890 to 2.780 in independent CapnoBase. Baseline-minus-augmented gains were -0.167 [-0.367, 0.013] and 0.110 [-0.174, 0.437], respectively (95% paired-record bootstrap intervals). Both intervals include zero; this exploratory negative implementation result is not evidence that diastolic morphology lacks respiratory information.

## Scientific motivation and question
Respiration modulates both pulse timing and peripheral pulse morphology. Primary pulse-decomposition work[1] examines width, amplitude and reflected-wave timing; a primary physiological comparison[2] reports that diastolic amplitude can track respiration. These support asking whether the **temporal distribution of area within the descending pulse**, rather than only its total area or width, carries usable incremental information. Charlton et al.[3] motivate explicit extraction, estimation and fusion comparisons. We test a fixed, inexpensive late-downstroke area fraction that a five-stream baseline discards. This is not a claim that pulse morphology, area-based features or respiratory fusion are new, nor that all current analyses omit this feature. No priority or comprehensive literature novelty claim is made; literature access was indexed primary abstracts/documents, not an exhaustive full-text systematic review.

## Frozen methods
Partition each record into nonoverlapping32s windows, with64s sensitivity. BIDMC reference is60/median of successive finite unique breath times in the window, requiring at least3breaths (annotator1 primary, annotator2 sensitivity). CapnoBase reference is the median of at least2finite positive supplied capnographic RR samples in the window. Thus reference definitions and modalities differ. ECG is read by the shared loader but unused by this analysis. Reference values enter only scoring and the explicitly labeled in-band sensitivity, never feature extraction or estimator tuning.

Filter finite nonconstant PPG within each window using third-order zero-phase Butterworth0.5–8Hz. Detect positive peaks with minimum separation0.3s and prominence>=0.2filteredSD; require8peaks. The three conventional streams are original PPG at systolic peaks (intensity), filtered prominence (amplitude proxy, not identical to raw trough-to-peak amplitude), and successive pulse intervals at the later peak. The extended baseline adds half-prominence width in seconds and normalized pulse area. Around each interior peak k, locate filtered minima l and r between neighboring peaks; retain l<k<r. Subtract the straight line joining filtered values at l,r and clip negative samples to0, producing v. Require positive pulse maximum and at least6usable interior pulses per window. Baseline total normalized area is trapezoid(v/max(v))/(r-l). These exact same retained windows serve every method.

**Candidate:** take v from k through r, resample this descending portion linearly onto101 equally spaced phase points spanning0–1, and divide trapezoid area over points50–100 by area over0–100 (denominator floor1e-30). This is the fraction of corrected descending area in its late temporal half, not an identified dicrotic wave or a direct arterial compliance measure. Place its beatwise values at peak times. This statistic can change when the downstroke redistributes mass despite a similar overall pulse width/area. Baseline correction uses the full pulse's trough-to-trough chord, not a new chord over the downstroke. Clipping precedes phase interpolation.

Each stream is interpolated to4Hz with held endpoints, linearly detrended, converted to a4096point Hann periodogram, restricted to0.1–0.6Hz and normalized to unit in-band sum (floor1e-30). Average spectra equally, choose their largest bin and report60f. Primary comparator averages the5baseline spectra; candidate averages all6. Additional controls: conventional3-stream pooling, candidate alone, constant18, and6-stream pooling after permuting candidate values within the window (seed20261125+window index). That permutation removes time ordering but is only one deterministic surrogate per window, not a calibrated null distribution. No outcome-fitted weights or tuning occur. Frequency grid spacing is not the physical resolution of32s windows. Predictions are bounded near6–36breaths/min; primary references are not bounded.

## Limitations and future clinical/experimental test
This negative result constrains one fixed arithmetic-fusion implementation. It does not show absence of respiratory information in diastolic shape, nor establish disease prediction. An uninformative but normalized stream receives equal weight; therefore failure could arise from estimator design even if conditional signal exists. We did not fit optimal weights, estimate conditional mutual information, reproduce full published decomposition methods, identify reflected waves or demonstrate incremental physiology independent of correlated morphology. Filter ringing, endpoint handling, peak/trough errors, motion, ectopy and devices can affect shape. Age, ventilation, comorbidity and treatment were not adjusted; no causal mechanism is inferred. Short selected clinical records and bounded frequency range limit generalization. Clinical monitors, calibrated signal-quality rejection and optimized published pipelines were not reproduced. Repeatedly exposed benchmarks provide no untouched confirmation, and the simple intervals understate the broader research-selection uncertainty.

A future preregistered study could freeze extraction/fusion and compare with optimized conventional and clinical-monitor estimates against adjudicated capnography in a previously unseen cohort. It could stratify ventilation, rhythm, motion and age, and test whether controlled respiratory changes produce late-area modulation beyond width/total area within individuals. Separating measurement artifacts from vascular physiology and assessing clinical decisions would require that prospective validation. **No such new clinical or physical experiment was performed here.**
