# Eosinophil-to-neutrophil balance did not improve cross-cycle FEV1/FVC prediction beyond nonlinear separate counts

## Abstract
A prespecified representation test fitted models in 3,560 NHANES 2007–2008 adults and transferred them without refitting to 3,996 NHANES 2009–2010 adults. Adding a spline of the log eosinophil-to-neutrophil ratio to demographics, smoking, anthropometry and separate count splines changed FEV1/FVC RMSE from 0.067206 to 0.067269. Delta MSE was 0.00000837 (target-participant bootstrap 95% interval 0.00000086 to 0.00001810). Source-refit plus target bootstrap uncertainty spanned zero. Weighted, quality and representation challenges did not support incremental benefit. These separately sampled survey releases share a protocol and are not independent clinical programmes. This negative result tests a deterministic feature representation, not the presence or absence of eosinophilic airway biology.

## Scientific motivation and prediction
Nerpin et al. (2018), *Respiratory Medicine* 142:94–100, [doi:10.1016/j.rmed.2018.07.011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30170809/), studied separate inflammatory markers and lung function in pooled NHANES data. This motivated a distinct predictive question: could a nonlinear balance between the two granulocyte counts improve prediction in a separately sampled release beyond flexible separate-count functions? This is not a replication of their association estimates, nor independent validation of their published claim: their underlying cycles overlap ours. We did not derive an effect size or train a model from that paper. Novelty is not claimed for granulocyte ratios generally. We test an incremental cross-cycle representation rarely evaluated by association analyses; we do not assert nobody has used it.

The chosen feature is r = log((eosinophils + 0.05)/(segmented neutrophils + 0.05)), with both counts in 1000 cells/µL. The 0.05 offset is half the counts' reported 0.1-unit resolution, not an optimized biological threshold. A ratio is fully determined by the separate counts and cannot add information in an information-theoretic sense. It may nevertheless improve finite-model approximation through nonlinear joint structure. Thus a fair comparator includes nonlinear separate counts, rather than only one count or a linear sum. The prespecified prediction was lower held-out MSE, with superiority requiring the entire paired bootstrap interval below zero; that prediction failed. There is no equivalence margin or proof of no biological effect.

## Frozen plan and methods
`files/plan.json` defines outcome, population, estimator, predictions, sensitivities, uncertainty and budget. Source fits and code/plan hashes were frozen before target computation (`files/evidence/freeze.sha256`, `frozen-at.txt`). Both raw cycles were already cached, but only source inputs were mounted during fitting; target preprocessing and prediction first ran afterward. This is study-local confirmation, not a globally pristine dataset: earlier projects and the motivating literature used these releases. All secondary variants were fixed before target evaluation; none replaced the primary model after observing results.

Unit: one participant, age 20–79, complete covariates, positive FVC and FEV1, nonnegative counts and positive MEC exam weight. The primary analysis requires A/B quality for both baseline FEV1 and FVC according to the [CDC spirometry codebook](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2007/DataFiles/SPX_E.htm). Outcome is pre-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC fraction, not COPD diagnosis, future decline or bronchodilator response. We excluded no record based on its residual or outcome threshold. Missing observations were removed, not imputed. Flow: source {'demographic_rows': 10149, 'age_eligible': 5496, 'complete_before_quality': 4042, 'abc': 3897, 'ab': 3560}; target {'demographic_rows': 10537, 'age_eligible': 5793, 'complete_before_quality': 4570, 'abc': 4398, 'ab': 3996}. Both models use the same complete cases. Quality C inclusion is secondary.

The conventional comparator uses age, height, BMI, sex, race/ethnicity and smoking status (never: SMQ020=2; former: SMQ020=1 and SMQ040=3; current: SMQ020=1 and SMQ040=1/2). Race categories are social descriptors, not biological mechanisms. Age, height, BMI and log(count+0.05) for each granulocyte have separate cubic spline expansions (4 uniformly placed source-range knots, no bias, linear extrapolation). The augmented model adds a 4-knot cubic spline of r. Source-only standardization precedes ridge regression with fixed alpha=1 and an intercept. There are 32 versus 37 coefficients excluding intercept; no target centering, tuning, recalibration, feature selection or target fitting occurs. Spline knots, scaling, coefficients and all exploratory variants are learned only on source data. Ridge resolves redundancy of the log ratio's linear component with separate count bases. Exact implementation is `files/code/analyze.py`.

Primary risk is unweighted participant mean squared error; this is not a national population estimate. MEC-weighted evaluation and separately weighted source fitting are reported as sensitivities, weights normalized within source. Uncertainty: 2,000 paired participant target bootstraps conditional on source fit; 300 paired PSU-within-stratum target bootstraps for weighted risk, retaining both PSUs with replacement; 200 joint source-participant refit and target-participant resamples. These percentile intervals target different variation. The joint analysis is not fully survey-design corrected. Source knots and scaling are refitted within each joint bootstrap. No p-value is used. Secondary subgroup and challenge results have no multiplicity-controlled confirmatory status.

## Limitations and future test
This study does not discover a new body-state signal. It provides evidence against a specific ratio representation improving a specified model, while leaving many representations and clinical endpoints untested. Absolute granulocyte counts are rounded derived variables ([CBC codebook](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2007/DataFiles/CBC_E.htm)); zero-count offset choice was not varied, so this negative result does not apply to every pseudocount or ratio definition. There is no longitudinal outcome, tissue inflammation assay, pack-year adjustment, treatment history adjustment, asthma-stratified analysis or independent prospective cohort. Complete-case and spirometry-eligibility selection can induce bias; severe disease, poor effort and unmeasured medication use may be underrepresented. Smoking categories are coarse; residual exposure, occupational and socioeconomic confounding remains. Cross-sectional blood counts do not establish pulmonary inflammation or a causal pathway. Spline/penalty choices, finite source samples, shared assay limitations and shared survey infrastructure restrict transfer. Survey-weight sensitivity is not a complete design-based national estimation exercise, and the weighted bootstrap omits source-design uncertainty.

A future independent clinical study could preregister this frozen comparator and ratio feature, obtain contemporaneous blood differential and high-quality pre/post-bronchodilator spirometry, record smoking dose and corticosteroid use, and assess incremental prediction and calibration in a separately recruited cohort before considering longitudinal decline. That is a proposed study, not work performed or authorized here. A definitive negative conclusion would require a prespecified clinical equivalence margin and adequate precision; this analysis supplies neither.
