# Urine–serum concentration interaction did not establish added albuminuria prediction across NHANES waves

## Summary
A prespecified interaction between measured urine osmolality and calculated serum osmolarity was tested for added single-spot albuminuria prediction. Models trained in 4,593 NHANES2009–10 adults were frozen and applied to3,960 NHANES2011–12 adults. Beyond flexible marginal laboratory and conventional risk-factor terms, the interaction changed weighted target Brier score by -0.00015182; a target-only stratified PSU bootstrap interval was [-0.00033436,0.00003256] and a two-wave full-refit interval was [-0.00074946,0.00077299]. Source grouped cross-validation worsened. Thus the prespecified criterion failed despite a small favorable target point estimate. Separate survey waves are not an independent clinical program. Findings do not establish a biomarker, renal concentrating defect, or absence of all useful interaction information.

## Question and prior evidence
Renal concentrating function is context dependent: a urine concentration potentially means something different at higher circulating osmotic load. Kitiwan et al. examined the marginal association of urine osmolality with kidney function/albuminuria using pooled NHANES2009–12, finding no fully adjusted marginal association ([primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34507548/)). Older controlled concentrating-capacity work did not find advantage for the urine/plasma ratio ([Rapoport et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5362306/)). Population studies have also compared urine-concentration surrogates with measured urine osmolality ([primary validation study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28586769/)). Those studies motivate, but do not establish, the present hypothesis: a simple urine–serum pairing might improve prediction after nonlinear marginal effects are already represented. This is not a claim that osmolality or its comparison with serum is newly discovered.

The candidate is a **representation of existing measurements**, not additional information in the information-theoretic sense: both component variables are already present in the baseline. The question is strictly whether one specific interaction helps a fixed additive prediction model. Arbitrary nonlinear models could represent it without an explicitly named feature.

The question was chosen from literature and public-data availability. A single documented title-OR SCI duplication check found no title matching this interaction study; four unrelated matches are retained in `files/catalog-duplication.json`. Title-only search and concurrent unpublished work cannot guarantee novelty. An earlier early-expiratory-fraction proposal was an exact duplicate and was abandoned without numerical analysis; its registration/check are preserved in the local archive. No SCI work supplies an analysis or claim here, so none is added as a decorative citation.

## Data, population, outcome and independence
Declared input files are public NHANES F(2009–10) and G(2011–12): DEMO, BMX, BIOPRO, GHB, UCOSMO, ALB_CR, BPX and BPQ. These are independently released cross-sectional survey-wave datasets, not random partitions of the same records. There are no common public SEQN values in the analytic samples. NHANES is not an intended repeat-participant panel, but individual linkage across waves is unavailable and was not attempted. Both waves share the same institution, protocols and potentially survey-design dependencies. **This is a cross-wave test, not completed validation in an independent non-NHANES clinical dataset.** Generalization beyond NHANES remains untested and the claim is limited accordingly.

Eligible participants were aged20–79, excluding known pregnancy (`RIDEXPRG==1`). Unknown/not-applicable pregnancy status was retained, not silently treated as confirmed nonpregnancy. Positive MEC examination weights, serum creatinine, glucose, urine osmolality and urine creatinine were required, with complete model covariates and outcome. Flow: source10,537 total →5,793 adults →5,725 not known pregnant →4,593 complete positive records; target9,756 →5,197 →5,140 →3,960. Source395 and target434 participants had albuminuria; weighted prevalences were 6.3876% and 7.9879%. Complete-case selection and safety/nonresponse exclusions can bias transport; no claim of nationally representative prevalence is made for this selected sample. Survey weighting alone does not correct those exclusions.

The statistical unit is one person. The binary outcome is first spot urine albumin/creatinine ratio `URDACT>=30mg/g`; no follow-up urine outcome was used even where available. This is **single-spot albuminuria, not chronic kidney disease diagnosis**. Urine concentration may affect the ACR denominator; log urine-creatinine adjustment challenges this concern but cannot remove all mathematical coupling. The [ALB_CR documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/ALB_CR_G.htm) and full provenance identify units and assay releases.

## Prespecified methods
`files/protocol.json` records the preanalysis question. `files/protocol-amendment.json` adds conventional BP controls before any main outcome/model inspection. Raw values are immutable; retrieval and computation are separated.

Calculated serum osmolarity is `2*Na + glucose/18 + BUN/2.8`, with sodium mmol/L and glucose/BUN mg/dL. It is not a measured plasma osmolality, and no osmolar gap is available. Measured random urine osmolality is `URXOAV`, mOsm/kg ([source](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2009/DataFiles/UCOSMO_F.htm), [target](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/UCOSMO_G.htm)). The interaction is the product of urine-osmolality and serum-osmolarity deviations from source means, divided by their source SDs; the product is then itself standardized on source. This is not a standardized concentrating challenge, water-clearance measurement, or direct vasopressin marker.

Baseline continuous variables: age, BMI, serum creatinine, HbA1c, sodium, serum glucose, BUN, urine osmolality, calculated serum osmolarity and mean available first3 systolic BP readings. Categorical variables: sex, race/ethnicity and current prescribed BP medication. `BPQ020==2` was coded untreated; otherwise `BPQ050A` supplied current status, with unrecognized codes missing. Race/ethnicity is a survey adjustment variable, not a biological mechanism. Each continuous predictor uses a cubic spline with5 source-quantile knots, no bias column, linear extrapolation; categorical dummy levels omit the first source category. Each design column is source-standardized. Redundancy of calculated osmolarity with its constituents is deliberate: the ridge baseline represents both component marginal effects and their summed osmotic load.

Both models are L2 logistic regression, C=1, lbfgs, tolerance1e-8, maximum1,500 iterations, weights normalized to mean1. Added model differs by only the interaction. All fitting, knots, scaling and categories use source only. Hyperparameters were not selected on target. Fivefold shuffled stratified-group cross-validation groups entire masked survey PSUs; no PSU appears on both sides of a fold. After source OOF prediction, final source models are frozen for target prediction. No target calibration or target coefficient fitting occurs. Primary loss is MEC-weighted Brier, added-minus-base (negative favors interaction); AUC is descriptive secondary.

Prediction before analysis: a negative interaction coefficient, source OOF improvement, and target Brier improvement with both target-only and two-wave-refit95% intervals wholly below0. Uncertainty uses seed88219:1,000 paired target Rao–Wu rescaled PSU bootstrap replicates within masked strata, then200 independently source/target-resampled replicates with preprocessing and coefficients refitted on source. For each stratum with n PSUs, n−1 are sampled with replacement and weights rescaled n/(n−1). Both waves have31 retained PSUs, with some three-PSU strata; intervals reflect limited survey cluster information and ignore inaccessible geographic design details. Percentile intervals are approximate, especially the200-refit tails; they are not proof of equivalence.

## Falsification, sensitivity and author checks
Prespecified sensitivities refit source then score the corresponding untouched target subset. They are not separately confirmed discoveries. Target added-minus-base Brier differences:
- `quality_no_diabetes`: -0.00008627 (target n=3503).
- `egfr60plus`: -0.00003364 (target n=3723).
- `linear`: -0.00000692 (target n=3960).
- `urine_creatinine_adjusted`: -0.00017787 (target n=3960).
- `unweighted`: -0.00021971 (target n=3960).

The internal name `quality_no_diabetes` means only HbA1c<6.5, not a laboratory quality filter or exclusion of all diagnosed diabetes. The linear baseline had better absolute target Brier than the primary spline baseline and almost no incremental interaction gain. This model dependence is evidence against a robust biomarker claim. eGFR>=60 restriction used the2021 race-free creatinine equation ([NKF equation](https://www.kidney.org/professionals/ckd-epi-creatinine-equation-2021)); primary models already included age, sex and serum creatinine separately.

Whole-source-label permutations (100) had median added-minus-base Brier0.00000524 and one of100 was at least as favorable as the observed target delta. This destroys baseline predictive structure too: it is a broad pipeline falsification, **not a conditional significance test**, and does not override the prespecified intervals. A feature interaction can have favorable target prediction without reliable source generalization.

## Interpretation and limits
These data do not establish an added albuminuria prediction benefit from this one interaction. They also do not rule out a modest benefit or other concentrating-response features. The evidence cannot identify renal tubular dysfunction, vasopressin dysregulation or causality. Random urine and serum chemistry are not a controlled, exactly synchronized osmotic challenge; timing, hydration, dietary osmoles, drugs, glycosuria, smoking and unmeasured illness may alter their relation. We did not measure urine flow, sodium excretion, copeptin or measured plasma osmolality. Serum osmolarity incorporates glucose and urea, themselves markers of disease; marginal adjustment reduces but cannot eliminate confounding. Rare extreme chemistry values can influence extrapolated spline predictions. Same-program cross-wave validation is materially weaker than an independent clinical cohort and does not meet the strongest interpretation of independent-dataset transfer. The original marginal NHANES paper uses these survey years too; it is background, not independent validation data for this study.

A future clinical study could externally preregister this frozen interaction and comparator, use simultaneously timed plasma/urine measurements, repeat ACR to establish persistence, and test whether a standardized, ethically supervised concentrating-response assessment predicts prospective renal outcomes beyond established risk markers. This is a proposal only; no such recruitment, measurement or experiment occurred here.
