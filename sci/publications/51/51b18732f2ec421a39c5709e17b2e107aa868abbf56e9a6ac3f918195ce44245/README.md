# Interear audiometric notch-depth disagreement does not improve cross-cohort tinnitus prediction in a prespecified model

## Abstract
We tested whether the absolute interear difference of 4-kHz notch depth adds predictive information for concurrent past-year tinnitus beyond conventional hearing summaries and notch indicators. NHANES2011–2012 supplied 3815 adults aged20–69 for model fitting; separately recruited2015–2016 supplied 4262 for frozen evaluation. Adding interear 4-kHz notch-depth disagreement changed cross-cohort Brier score from 0.124650 to 0.124877 (difference +0.000227; joint PSU-bootstrap95% interval -0.000439 to +0.001557). Source five-fold out-of-fold Brier improved slightly, but the registered cross-cohort criterion was not met. This is a specification-specific negative result, not equivalence or absence of physiological information. Both cycles were previously explored for other outcomes; no untouched or independent-study-program confirmation is claimed.

## Question and literature origin
Do two ears with similar conventional hearing loss but different 4-kHz notch depths encode information about tinnitus that summary audiometry misses? [Wilson2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23516088/) examined unilateral/bilateral notch patterns in a large veteran sample. [Carroll et al.2017](https://doi.org/10.15585/mmwr.mm6605e3) used conventional high-frequency notch criteria in NHANES. These motivate a **continuous interear disagreement** descriptor, not a claim to invent notching or establish that prior literature never used asymmetry. The contribution is the exact incremental prediction/transfer test, including its negative result. No SCI paper supplied the hypothesis or scientific findings. ## Frozen comparison and uncertainty
For ear h, PTA_h=mean(T0.5,T1,T2,T4); notch depth N_h=T4−(T2+T8)/2. Added feature D=abs(N_R−N_L), in dB. Baseline contains better and worse PTA (thus controlling overall interear PTA asymmetry), bilateral mean T6/T8, mean N, age, sex, education and race. Conventional CDC notch in an ear requires max(T3,T4,T6)−mean(T0.5,T1)≥15dB and max(T3,T4,T6)−T8≥5dB. Unilateral/bilateral indicators are included. The comparator thus already contains standard hearing summaries, asymmetry and notch presence rather than a deliberately weak age-only model. It is a research prediction model, not an established clinical tinnitus score.

Age, better/worse PTA, high-frequency mean and mean N receive separate cubic splines with four unweighted source-quantile knots, no bias basis. Sex and education/race indicators and notch flags enter linearly. Added D enters linearly. Both models use source-fitted scaling and L2 logistic regression C=1, tolerance1e−9, max5000 iterations and source MEC weights normalized to mean1. Target outcomes are never used for knots, scaling, coefficients or hyperparameter selection. Baseline and feature were frozen before computing target results. No pretrained models or participant/session overlap through splitting are involved.

Primary estimand is target MEC-weighted Brier difference (added−baseline, negative better); AUC and logloss are secondary. Registered success requires upper95% joint-bootstrap Brier endpoint<0, not a clinical utility threshold. Rescaled stratified PSU bootstrap samples n_h−1 PSUs with replacement and multiplier n_h/(n_h−1), retaining any singleton stratum.1000 target-only and200 independent source-refit/target resamples produce percentile intervals. Source replicates remove zero-weight rows before fitting knots and scaling; penalty normalization uses mean replicate weights over the original source rows. Intervals omit model-selection/history uncertainty, between-program heterogeneity and nonresponse-model uncertainty. Few PSUs and200 joint draws limit precision. Five-fold source stratified out-of-fold results are exploratory, not independent validation.

## Limitations and future falsification
This tests one linear disagreement descriptor conditional on one baseline, not all unused audiogram information. Complete-case selection excludes less testable ears and may bias associations; demographic adjustment does not remove residual noise, medication, middle-ear, neurologic, socioeconomic or reporting confounding. Notch depth depends on calibration and threshold noise, especially at8kHz; it is not a direct measure of cochlear synaptopathy. No tinnitus side/location or objective physiology was modeled. Shared NHANES protocol, historical cohort reuse and many previous questions weaken discovery claims. There is no evidence here for diagnosis, treatment, causality or prospective prediction.

A future independently recruited study could preregister the exact descriptor/comparison, repeat audiometry to test reliability, measure tinnitus laterality and validated severity, account for noise/medications and evaluate frozen longitudinal predictions. That clinical study was **not performed**. An independent existing-data study with harmonized bilateral audiometry and tinnitus would be the next computational confirmation.
