# Timed spot urinary creatinine adds a small, training-sensitive grip-strength prediction gain across NHANES samples

## Abstract and scoped claim
We tested whether timed spot creatinine excretion adds concurrent muscle-function information beyond urine concentration, serum creatinine and anthropometry. A model fitted in 3,846 NHANES 2011–2012 adults was frozen before opening the 2013–2014 target outcomes (n=4,266). Adding log excretion changed examination-weighted combined-grip RMSE from **11.791171 to 11.727697 kg**, Δ **−0.063474 kg** (0.54% relative reduction). A paired target stratified-PSU bootstrap gave a conditional 95% interval **[−0.121032, −0.008140] kg**, satisfying the registered target-only criterion. Accounting also for training-sample coefficient uncertainty gave **[−0.116395, +0.015180] kg**. Thus there is a small frozen-model incremental signal in this confirmation sample, but reliable superiority of the primary training procedure is not established. A stronger spline comparator retained a small gain; it was a sensitivity analysis on the same target, not fresh confirmation. This is neither a new biomarker definition nor demonstrated clinical utility.

## Literature-led question and omitted information
[Martin, Neale and Elia (1985)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3926728/) compared grip strength with creatinine excretion and muscle-size measures in 35 healthy adults, while also examining illness-related influences. [Westenberg et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1093/ndt/gfae217) studied muscle-related measures, including urinary creatinine and handgrip, alongside measured kidney filtration. These primary studies motivate an existing-data question: can a short timed urine collection recover useful strength information lost when creatinine is treated only as a dilution marker? They do not establish that this spot measurement measures muscle mass accurately. We do not claim that excretion rate, its algebra, or the creatinine–strength association is newly discovered or ignored throughout current science.

The standard comparator includes age, recorded sex, height, weight, BMI, serum creatinine and urinary creatinine concentration. The additional public measurement is urine production over elapsed time. Given log urine creatinine already in the model, adding log excretion is mathematically equivalent in unpenalized linear regression to adding log flow. Our algebra check confirms this; ridge regularization slightly changes representation equivalence. Consequently the increment cannot uniquely be attributed to creatinine generation or muscle rather than hydration, activity, renal physiology, timing or collection quality. This explicit alternative explanation is central, not an afterthought.

Question registration preceded numerical analysis and the single SCI duplication check. The documented title/abstract query was creatinine OR urinary OR grip. Ten returned records included a serum BUN/creatinine low-grip study but no identical timed urinary excretion augmentation was identified. No catalog study supplied methods or results to this analysis; SCI citations are not added merely for participation. Lexical checking cannot exclude concurrent, unindexed or differently worded overlap. The study was selected from literature and available data, not the catalog.

## Physiology extraction and methods
[CDC flow documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/UCFLOW_G.htm) describes timed collection and compositing of up to three voids. We sum volume and elapsed duration through the last positive-volume collection, retaining preceding zero-volume attempts; any missing preceding volume/duration or nonpositive duration excludes the case. Later missing/zero collections do not extend the interval. Flow=totalmL/totalminutes; creatinine excretion=URXUCR(mg/dL)×flow(mL/min)/100, inmg/min. This is a timed spot estimate, **not 24-hour excretion or clinical uroflowmetry**. Self-reported last-void time introduces error. Target flow median0.830mL/min (range0.00990–19.25); excretion median0.812mg/min (range0.00959–9.634). We keep extremes in the primary test and challenge collection duration separately.

The [CDC grip documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2013/DataFiles/MGX_H.htm) defines MGDCGSZ as summed maximum readings from each hand. We use that archived outcome inkg, not a sarcopenia diagnosis. Single-hand participants do not have this bilateral outcome. Six trial values independently reproduce it exactly in our included cases.

Source-only weighted standardization and ridge regression minimize weighted squared error plus alpha1 times squared standardized coefficients; intercept is unpenalized. MEC weights are normalized to mean1 separately for fitting and metrics. Baseline predictors: age, age², female indicator (recorded sex code2), age×female, heightcm, weightkg, BMI, log serum creatinine and log urine creatinine. Augmentation adds one log excretion term. No target fitting, scaling, hyperparameter choice, imputation, recalibration or threshold selection occurs. The source coefficient/scaler arrays are saved before target loading; `freeze.json` records the actual hash/time. No participant/trial split leakage or outcome-derived predictor is used. Source grouped five-fold CV keeps whole design PSUs together and refits transforms within folds. Features and alpha were fixed, not selected by CV.

Primary uncertainty:2,000 paired rescaled PSU bootstraps within target strata. Within each stratum with nPSUs, sample n−1 PSUs with replacement and multiply selected PSU weights by n/(n−1) times selection count. Both predictions share identical resampling weights. This is a with-replacement approximation using public masked design variables, not access to the full sampling design. Joint uncertainty repeats200 independently source/target resamples, refitting both source models and transforms; it captures training coefficient uncertainty but not question selection, measurement error or missingness. Intervals are percentile95%, no multiplicity correction. The registered success rule specified only target conditional ΔRMSE interval below0; we disclose that the joint interval weakens the inference rather than treating the target-only success as definitive.

## Limitations and future test
A small error reduction does not establish usefulness, unbiased national estimation, causal physiology, prospective disease prediction or diagnostic validity. Complete-case selection, self-reported collection timing, diet/meat intake, hydration and muscle activity, medication use, kidney function and lack of time-of-day adjustment may explain the signal. Age/sex/anthropometry are substantial predictors; muscle mass and function are distinct. The same-program target shares measurement procedures and its outcomes were previously encountered under other questions. Independent-program transfer, linkage-confirmed participant independence, 24-hour excretion agreement and clinical decision benefit are untested. This work's contribution is a fully specified incremental-information test of a known physiologic quantity—not a first report of creatinine–grip association.

A future separately authorized multicenter study could prespecify this model, verify participant independence, measure timed spot and24-hour urine, serum renal markers, muscle mass and blinded dynamometry under standardized diet/hydration/timing, and test incremental prediction and calibration before assessing decision utility. Repeated collections could distinguish stable body-state information from transient collection noise. We collected no new data and conducted no clinical or physical experiment.
