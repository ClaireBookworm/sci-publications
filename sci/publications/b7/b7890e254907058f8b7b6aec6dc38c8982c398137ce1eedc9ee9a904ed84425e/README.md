# Paired office blood-pressure covariance does not improve cross-cohort grip-strength prediction beyond means and marginal variability

## Abstract
We tested whether covariance of paired systolic and diastolic office blood-pressure readings adds information about concurrent grip strength beyond conventional pressure means, separate variability, demographics and anthropometry. NHANES2011–2012 supplied 3601 participants for fitting and separately recruited2013–2014 supplied 4008 for frozen evaluation. Adding paired systolic–diastolic covariance changed cross-cohort grip-strength MSE from 127.488748 to 127.488753 kg² (difference +0.000005; joint PSU-bootstrap95% interval -0.480825 to +2.524937). The registered improvement criterion failed. This is a model-specific negative result, not equivalence or evidence against all physiological information in repeated pressures. Both cycles were previously explored; no untouched confirmation or independent-study-program validation is claimed.

## Literature-origin question and scope
[Ji et al.2018](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12944-018-0734-4) related handgrip strength to conventional blood-pressure measures in NHANES. [Hemodynamic Determinants of the Short-Term Blood Pressure Variability](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28096150/) examined different vascular contributors to systolic and diastolic variability in ambulatory recordings. These motivate asking whether the **joint fluctuation**, rather than only each marginal variability, carries additional information about physical function. ## Features and locked model
For paired readings S_j,D_j (j=1,2,3), C = Σ_j(S_j−mean S)(D_j−mean D)/2 in mmHg². Zero variability naturally gives C=0. Conditional on separate sample variances, C also encodes pulse-pressure variability: C=(Var(S)+Var(D)−Var(S−D))/2. Thus this is a descriptor of existing paired data, not a new physiological measurement; no superiority to a model already containing pulse-pressure variance is claimed.

Baseline continuous variables: age, height, BMI, waist, serum creatinine, mean SBP, mean DBP, pulse, SBP sample SD, DBP sample SD and upper-arm circumference. Every variable receives cubic splines with four source quantile knots, bias excluded and constant extrapolation. Each spline basis interacts with female sex; sex and race indicators2–5 (reference1) are included. Added model appends **linear C and C×female**, not tuned nonlinear terms. All feature construction, knot fitting, scaling, and coefficients use source data only; the target outcome is never used for selection or calibration. StandardScaler is unweighted; Ridge alpha1 with unpenalized intercept and weights normalized to mean1. Penalization is therefore scale dependent and its sensitivity is reported.

Mean SBP/DBP are standard pressure summaries; each marginal SD is a variability comparator. BMI/waist/height/creatinine address body-size and muscle correlates. Arm circumference challenges cuff-tissue confounding. This rich comparator makes it harder for covariance merely to proxy marginal variability or body size. It is not a validated clinical risk score, and predicting grip does not replace measuring grip directly.

## Limitations and future test
This falsifies useful improvement for one linear covariance descriptor under one rich baseline, not every information source in repeated BP. Three auscultatory readings have limited repeatability; examiner effects, cuff placement, respiration, rounding to even mmHg, alerting response and arm tissue can drive covariance without cardiovascular mechanism. Medication, smoking, socioeconomic conditions, comorbidity, missingness and complete-case selection remain possible confounders. Current grip strength and pressure were collected at the same examination; there is no causal inference or prospective disease prediction. No independent-program dataset was obtained, so transfer claims are restricted to these two NHANES cycles. Historical reuse and multiple previous hypotheses preclude pristine confirmation claims.

A future independently recruited clinical study could preregister the descriptor, measure repeatability across visits and observers, include medication and cuff controls, add direct arterial stiffness measures, and evaluate frozen predictions of independently measured later physical function. Such a study was **not performed**. Existing-data validation with a harmonized independent study program is the next computational test.
