# Grip-trial curvature fails to improve transferred walking-difficulty prediction in older NHANES adults

## Abstract and scoped claim
Grip-trial curvature did not improve transferred walking-difficulty prediction over maximum strength plus endpoint slope: weighted Brier improvement -0.0002909, joint source/target PSU-bootstrap 95% interval [-0.0052406, 0.0016547]. The source sample was NHANES 2011–12 (n=1062); frozen predictions were evaluated in independently sampled NHANES 2013–14 (n=1158). A conventional model using combined maximum strength without endpoint slope performed better than either trial-pattern model. This is an informative negative test, not evidence of equivalence, a new diagnostic, or absence of all information in repeated trials.

## Motivation and prediction
[Primary research on maximum grip and hand limitations](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35871136/) motivates assessing trial-level information beyond peak force; the walking endpoint here differs from that paper's endpoint. [Population grip estimates](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7197498/) summarize combined maxima. The specific candidate is bilateral mean second difference: C = mean_hand(T3 − 2T2 + T1), in kg. A middle-trial dip produces positive curvature. This loses potentially useful hand-specific information but is a fixed, low-dimensional temporal pattern. It is not a physiological fatigue measurement: alternating hands, recovery, learning, motivation and random measurement error all contribute. The protocol anticipated that adding curvature might improve prediction; its small negative fitted coefficient and failed transfer did not support a useful signal. We make no global claim that previous analyses never used curvature. Literature/data preceded a single scoped SCI duplication check; files/catalog-check.json records coverage and failed filter attempts, not proof of novelty. No SCI publications were analytical inputs.

## Data, population, outcome and independence
Public-use NHANES [2011–12 MGX](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/MGX_G.htm) and [2013–14 MGX](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2013/DataFiles/MGX_H.htm), DEMO, BMX and PFQ files were joined one-to-one by SEQN. Statistical unit: participant, not trial. Adults >=60, positive WTMEC2YR, all six readings present and all technician effort codes 1 (maximal), valid PFQ061B and complete covariates were included. [PFQ codebook](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/PFQ_G.htm): walking a quarter mile, outcome 2/3/4 (some/much difficulty/unable) versus 1 (none). Code 5, unknown, refused and missing are excluded, never treated as normal. Age >=60 avoids the younger-adult screening skip; respondents needing walking equipment are not asked this item and remain excluded. This is therefore a selected population able to complete maximal grip testing and answer the walking question, not all older Americans.

Flow: source 1687 merged older adults → 1335 six maximal efforts → 1066 valid outcome → 1062 complete. Target: 1785 → 1418 → 1162 → 1158. Source cases 193, weighted prevalence 16.878%; target cases 209, weighted prevalence 17.608%. Source 14 strata/31 PSUs; target 15/30.

Cycles are separate repeated cross-sectional survey datasets, not splits of the same dataset. They share instrument, recruitment system and national population; this is temporal sample transfer, not validation across health systems or countries. Public participant IDs are disjoint (asserted). Direct cross-cycle person linkage is unavailable, so disjoint IDs alone cannot definitively exclude repeat individuals; no intentional longitudinal participant overlap is known. G/H files had been cached for earlier unrelated work; this question was specified before this analysis, but these cycles are not globally pristine to the researcher. Target outcomes did not select the candidate or tune its model.

## Models and uncertainty
Best = max(T1,T2,T3) hand1 + max(T1,T2,T3) hand2. Endpoint slope S = mean_hand((T3−T1)/2), kg per trial. Conventional standard model: age, BMI, best (each cubic B-spline, 4 source-derived uniform knots, degree3, no bias, then standardized); sex and RIDRETH1 categorical. Enhanced base adds the same spline representation of S. Curve model adds standardized linear C; curveflex instead adds spline C (secondary sensitivity). Scikit-learn logistic regression: C=1, L2 regularization, lbfgs, max_iter1500, tol1e−8; mean-normalized MEC weights in fitting and evaluation. No target-derived knots/scales, recalibration, tuning or feature selection. Source exploration uses five GroupKFold folds keeping PSUs together, all preprocessing refit in each fold. No hyperparameter search. Point fits use all source participants before target is mounted.

Primary estimand is weighted target Brier(base) − Brier(curve), positive better. AUC and log loss are secondary. Within each stratum with m observed PSUs, bootstrap draws m−1 PSUs with replacement, scaling counts by m/(m−1); source and target resampled independently. Report 200 joint source-refit/target draws (percentile interval, finite Monte Carlo precision), plus 1000 target-only draws conditional on the original source fits. This rescaled PSU bootstrap respects clustering better than a participant bootstrap but is conditional on complete-case selection and observed PSUs, not a full official NHANES variance analysis.

## Limitations and future test
The signal is noisy and missingness strongly selective. Complete cases and maximal-effort restrictions exclude many of the most impaired, and weights alone cannot repair that. Age, body size, sex and race adjustment does not eliminate confounding by arthritis, neurologic disease, pain, medication, education, activity or effort. Outcome is contemporaneous self-report, not prospective disability; reverse causation is plausible. The endpoint slope spline may overfit (the standard model was better), and linear curvature is a restricted candidate. Confidence intervals allow small gains and harms; absence of detected gain is not equivalence. Two waves of one survey provide only a modest independence challenge. No optimal trial summary, causal mechanism, clinical threshold or broad absence of physiological information is claimed.

A future observational clinical study could prespecify curvature and peak-strength models, recruit a broader spectrum including impaired/noncompleters, measure test–retest reliability and objective gait, and assess incremental prospective disability prediction with blinded endpoints. A controlled experimental study could distinguish learning from fatigability using standardized timing and effort. Neither was conducted here; all work used existing public data.
