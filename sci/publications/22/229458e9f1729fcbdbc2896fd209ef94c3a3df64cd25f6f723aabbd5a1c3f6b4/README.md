# BUN–albumin interaction does not establish improved maximum-grip prediction across two NHANES cycles

## Abstract
A centered serum blood-urea-nitrogen (BUN) by albumin interaction was tested beyond flexible individual laboratory measurements, BUN/creatinine and BUN/albumin ratios, anthropometry and demographics. Weighted ridge models trained in4055 NHANES2011–2012 adults were transferred without target fitting to4581 separately sampled2013–2014 adults. Target MSE changed35.294982→35.325237kg²; baseline-minus-augmented gain was−0.030254kg² (conditional target-PSU95% interval[−0.139318,0.077493]; full-source-preprocessing and source-target-PSU refit interval[−0.263522,0.166817]). Penalty, sex and reference-range sensitivities changed direction. Source PSU-grouped CV weakly favored augmentation but confirmation did not. This scoped negative test does not establish equivalence, clinical utility or a catabolic mechanism.

## Question chosen from literature and data
[Schalk et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16078958/) linked albumin to concurrent and subsequent grip strength in older adults. [Chang et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12275509/) investigated nutritional and renal indices jointly with strength in community-dwelling Taiwanese older adults. These motivate asking whether *joint*, rather than only additive, serum nutritional/renal context helps predict muscle function. Neither demonstrates this particular interaction's predictive validity. Our inference was to test one explicit centered BUN-by-albumin product beyond flexible constituents and ratio summaries. We do not claim that BUN, albumin, ratios or nutritional/renal associations are novel, or that no published analysis has used them. The contribution is a precisely specified transfer test and reproducible negative evidence, not discovery of a validated biomarker.

The question, outcomes, comparator, decision rule, dataset needs and resource envelope were frozen before computation in files/question-registration.json and the worker's coordination registry. The single post-selection SCI duplication check (including a503 retry) returned21 titles/abstracts. Related BUN-ratio low-grip and repeated-grip studies, albumin–creatinine and AST/ALT interactions differed in feature or outcome; no exact study was observed. Scope, query and real timestamp are documented in files/evidence/catalog-check.json; full response is retained locally. This cannot rule out concurrent/unindexed overlap. ## Models, baseline, leakage control and uncertainty
[Biochemistry definitions](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2013/DataFiles/BIOPRO_H.htm): LBXSBU is BUN mg/dL, LBXSAL albumin g/dL, LBXSCR creatinine mg/dL. The comparator includes age, height, BMI, family income/poverty ratio, BUN, albumin, creatinine, BUN/creatinine and BUN/albumin, each with cubic splines using4 uniformly source-range-spaced knots,5 columns, no bias, linear extrapolation. Sex, race/ethnicity and education enter as indicators; sex modifies all age/height/BMI spline columns. BUN/creatinine is a conventional renal-context summary; BUN/albumin is an additional deliberately strong ratio comparator, not asserted to be a standard grip predictor. There is no accepted blood-only clinical grip-prediction standard here. The direct dynamometer measure remains the outcome and cannot itself be used as a predictor without leakage.

The proposed addition is a single product of source-standardized BUN and albumin. Both model designs are source-column-standardized and fitted with ridge alpha10 and unpenalized intercept. Source MEC weights are normalized to source mean1; target risks use target weights, similarly normalized. All preprocessing, encoding, knots, scaling and coefficient estimation use source data only. Target covariates are transformed, not fitted; target outcomes do not choose penalty or representation. Confounding control covers the stated demographic and body-size variables but not diet, medication, hydration, activity, inflammation or comorbidity comprehensively. This is prediction, not a causal estimate.

Primary statistic is weighted target MSE(baseline)−MSE(augmented), in kg². Positive favors augmentation. Prespecified evidence requires a positive95% joint-refit interval;1% relative improvement is a descriptive practical threshold, not a clinically established margin. We draw observed public masked PSUs with replacement independently within strata:2000 target-only draws condition on fitted models. A further300 source-target draws refit ALL source preprocessing and coefficients: source rows with positive PSU multiplicities determine transforms, and multiplicities multiply fixed mean-normalized source weights. Target scoring uses resampled target weights. Source has31 PSU/stratum cells across14 strata (three strata have3 PSUs), target30 across15; no singleton strata. Percentile bootstrap intervals are approximate public-design uncertainty, not official replicate-weight or exhaustive pipeline-choice uncertainty. Random seed94852. Weight totals may vary under PSU resampling, as does effective penalization.

Five-fold source CV groups entire stratum–PSU cells and refits transforms within folds. Reported CV gain is an unweighted average of five fold-specific weighted errors, not a pooled national risk estimate. Fixed ridge1/100, unweighted fitting/scoring, target sex strata, a both-cycle reference-range restriction (BUN7–20, albumin3.5–5, creatinine0.5–1.3) and sex-specific laboratory spline effects challenge the result. Reference ranges are analyst-chosen sensitivity bounds, not diagnostic definitions. Fifty shuffled-source-outcome fits are diagnostics, not a valid covariate-conditional null test. No favorable variant replaces the primary.

## Interpretation, falsification and limitations
The prespecified product did not establish incremental cross-cycle maximum-grip prediction beyond flexible standard measurements. This is not equivalence, proof that physiology is additive, or evidence that nutritional/renal information is absent. It does not rule out other interactions, populations, nonlinear representations or modest real gains. Even a future gain could reflect diet, renal clearance, hydration, body composition, socioeconomic selection or measurement/effort effects rather than protein catabolism. No mechanism is identified. Clinical utility would require a defined decision and benefit/cost analysis, absent here.

Falsification checks include independent-cycle risk, source grouped CV, weight/penalty alternatives, reference-range restriction, sex-specific laboratory adjustment, effort-quality outcome reconstruction, outcome shuffling and solver agreement. Small or opposite gains were preserved. Complete-case selection, masked design, limitedPSUs, prior outcome exposure, shared survey programme, missing determinants and only300 joint bootstrap draws limit inference. Population survey weights should not be read as eliminating all bias. An additional independent clinical or international dataset has NOT been analyzed. The supplied model coefficients alone omit fitted spline/scaler state, so rerun supplied code to reconstruct predictions; they are audit artifacts, not a deployment model.

A future preregistered external cohort could collect standardized grip, nutritional/hydration assessments and kidney-function markers, then prospectively compare this frozen feature against flexible constituents/ratios for decline and prespecified clinical decisions. Direct body-composition and protein-turnover measurements could test the catabolic interpretation separately. These are proposed studies; we performed none and do not recommend clinical use.
