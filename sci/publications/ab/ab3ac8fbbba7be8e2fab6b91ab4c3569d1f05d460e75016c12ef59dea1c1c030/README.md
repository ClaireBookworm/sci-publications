# Does octave-spaced audiogram curvature improve perceived-hearing prediction? An exploratory cross-cohort test

## Abstract
Pure-tone averages discard audiogram configuration. We tested a specific nonlinear summary: root-mean-square second differences of bilateral octave-spaced thresholds. Source-only weighted logistic models in 3816 NHANES2011–2012 adults were frozen and evaluated in 4263 separately recruited2015–2016 adults. The comparator used standard ear-level PTA, high-frequency loss, notch summaries and demographic covariates. The concurrent outcome was self-reported hearing trouble. Octave-spaced bilateral audiogram curvature energy changed target weighted Brier score from 0.125382 to 0.125461: difference +0.000080, joint source/target PSU-bootstrap95% interval [-0.000212,+0.001000]. The registered Brier improvement criterion was not met. This is exploratory same-program cross-cohort evidence, not untouched confirmation, causal physiology or independent-program clinical validation.

## Feature and predicted relationship
Take octave-spaced thresholds T_e=(T0.5,T1,T2,T4,T8), dB HL, for each ear e. At interior octave positions f=1,2,4kHz, define D_e,f=T_e,f/2−2T_e,f+T_e,2f. Define **R=sqrt(mean over both ears and all three positions of D_e,f squared)**, in dB. Code names this feature `discord` only to retain scaffold compatibility; it always means R here, not ear discordance. Positive curvature energy may capture irregular spectral impairment not represented by PTA or one notch; it could alternatively reflect measurement noise. The added model's single linear R coefficient is unconstrained. No causal direction is imposed.

R is nonnegative, invariant to ear exchange and to adding any ear-specific affine function of log2frequency, and zero for an exactly log-linear audiogram in each ear. It scales with absolute amplitude. It measures nonlinear configuration, not slope, sharpness of a single clinical notch, or temporal physiology. Using equally log-spaced frequencies avoids applying equal-step second differences to3/6kHz irregular spacings. Squaring and averaging loses sign, location and ear-specific detail. Frequency-resolved benchmarks are therefore essential; R contains no new raw measurement beyond the audiogram.

## Limitations and future test
Curvature can amplify threshold quantization and unreliability. Squared second differences mix genuine pathology with noise; the1k repeat restriction does not establish multi-frequency reliability. Air-conduction thresholds cannot distinguish conductive and sensorineural mechanisms. Self-report reflects language, expectations, hearing-aid use, cognition and environment; residual confounding remains. No causal effect, hidden-hearing-loss detection, prospective prognosis or treatment recommendation is established. Complete-case weighting does not fully correct missingness. Same-program cross-cohort evaluation does not satisfy independent-program validation. Historical exposure to these cohorts and many prior local analyses makes optimism possible even with a newly registered question.

A future independent clinical study could preregister this exact descriptor, quantify repeat audiogram reliability across frequencies, record aided/unaided status and speech-in-noise performance, and evaluate the frozen models with a prespecified clinically meaningful improvement margin. That study was not conducted. An independent existing-data program with bilateral thresholds and comparable self-report is the next computational test; until then the scope remains these two cohorts and this model specification.
