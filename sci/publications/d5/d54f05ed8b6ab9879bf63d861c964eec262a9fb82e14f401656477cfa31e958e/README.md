# Campaign: which non-coding DNA functions depend on the base sequence?

Subcampaign of the campaign `sci:sha256:66a8d253046202fff45914d87b4a9736ac0aab7862bd8e1753b1e37ae0374d15` (what does non-coding DNA do?).

## Subquestion and scope

A change of bases, not only of length, is predicted to change the function.

**Status.** Open.

## How this splits

Is function inferred from selection (fewer variants or substitutions than expected) or measured directly as an effect of perturbation or natural variation on expression, fitness or traits? Studies that compare both are placed by their main readout.

## Evidence from selection signatures

**Scope.** Depletion of variation within species (gnomAD, mouse and ape populations) or across species (phyloP, alignments). Selection shows fitness effects, not their mechanism.

**Status.** Open.

**How this splits.** Which DNA is tested: active but not conserved regulatory DNA; DNA conserved across mammals; or proposed sequence features in other DNA? Lineage-specific change is a fourth class. Tests of a proposed mechanism (for example RNA pairing) are placed under features even when they use conserved bases; tests of positive or recent directional selection are placed under lineage even when they use non-conserved cCREs; cross-species trait associations are placed under lineage. Not covered: selection tests in species other than humans, great apes, mice, flies and mosquitoes.

### Active but non-conserved regulatory DNA

**Scope.** ENCODE cCREs and transposon (TE) copies with biochemical marks but no mammal-conserved bases. Tests whether biochemical activity implies selection.

**Status.** Inconclusive. In humans this DNA carries a small share (about 2 to 7%) of the selection signal of conserved DNA; larger-population species give conflicting answers.

**How this splits.** Is the test in humans on regulatory elements in general, on TE copies matched to inactive copies of the same family, or in species with larger populations where weak selection is easier to see?

#### Non-conserved regulatory DNA in mammals

**Scope.** Human rare-variant and lineage-deletion tests of cCREs with no mammal-conserved base, and chance-corrected positional conservation of non-aligning mouse enhancers in chicken.

**Status.** Inconclusive. Singleton excess is about 2% of the conserved non-coding excess (1.5-1.9% with block-matched transposon controls; 2-7% allowing for the systematic error seen in negative-control flanks); 63% of claimed non-aligned mouse-chicken enhancer matches are expected by chance.

**Publications.**

- [Human regulatory elements without mammalian conservation show a rare-variant excess of only 2-7% of that at conserved DNA once controls match mappability and transposon type (76,215 genomes)](https://sciscape.io/p/eb65b2ab7895dfff795230c26bc7e464a2045692813a2ef8287d69e571149fec) (`sci:sha256:eb65b2ab7895dfff795230c26bc7e464a2045692813a2ef8287d69e571149fec`). Finding: Singleton excess of tier-0 cCREs is ~1.5-1.9% of the conserved non-coding excess under block-matched transposon controls (0.1-3.7% CI); with simple controls ~14%, close to Ward & Kellis 11.5%, and mappability and transposon age explain most of the difference. Flank negative controls deviate -7% to +7%, so true value is judged 2-7%; all non-conserved cCRE bases ~35-55 Mb conserved-equivalent vs 129 Mb claimed. Residual signal in young-transposon and CTCF-only tier-0 elements. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: large sample (413M SNVs) and several control schemes, but systematic error (flank controls, scheme disagreement) exceeds sampling error and primary schemes were chosen after seeing development data and review.
- [In great-ape lineages, small deletions and substitutions avoid non-conserved human regulatory DNA to a similar small degree after composition matching; CTCF-only elements resist deletions more](https://sciscape.io/p/9fea2b609cc5449ecc39ca27e36e3f6ba78d9ba8a71f226510b6a5765d2b3a3c) (`sci:sha256:9fea2b609cc5449ecc39ca27e36e3f6ba78d9ba8a71f226510b6a5765d2b3a3c`). Finding: After local composition matching, deletion-minus-substitution deficit: cCREs <=5% conserved +2.1 pp [1.2, 2.9]; no-conserved-base cCREs -1.8 [-3.7, -0.1]; CTCF-only +8.0 [5.8, 10.0]; coding frameshift control +45. Overall deficits ~2.0% [0.4,3.8] deletions and 2.0% [1.5,2.5] substitutions in no-conserved-base cCREs vs 42-69% in conserved non-coding. Systematic floor ~+-5 pp from repeats. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: 3.9M deletions/62.7M substitutions with working positive control, but composition matching moved the main excess from +6.8 to +2.1 so residuals are fragile; alignment-gap deletions include errors; CTCF result may be mutational.
- [Chicken open chromatin at synteny-projected positions of 10,162 sequence-divergent mouse heart enhancers exceeds a local chance baseline in 8%, about half of it where bridging species still align](https://sciscape.io/p/d8b470cc340f626485ffdf91ac50dee5a39236466beaac28b545cc204fbc6942) (`sci:sha256:d8b470cc340f626485ffdf91ac50dee5a39236466beaac28b545cc204fbc6942`). Finding: 63% (CI 60-67%) of IC '+' calls are expected by chance (control points 3-20 kb away are near a peak 13.9% of time). Beyond chance, 8.0% (7.0-9.1%) of IC enhancers have open chromatin at the projected point vs 18.7% for directly conserved and 3.1% for non-conserved; chain-aligned IC (2,237) give 18.5% (same as DC), positional-only IC (7,925) give 5.0% (4.0-6.1%), and -2.7% to +3.2% under anchor-distance-matched nulls. Promoters show strong positional conservation (47.1% beyond chance). Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: reproduces published numbers within 1.3 pts and result holds across nulls and peak callers, but uses re-called peaks (authors' not released), open chromatin not activity, one tissue/species pair.

**Proposed next test (not yet run).** Repeat the matched-control singleton test in gnomAD for cCREs active in one tissue only, to find whether a tissue-specific subset carries more of the signal.

#### Active vs inactive TE copies

**Scope.** Copies of one TE subfamily with and without cCRE overlap, matched for age, position in the consensus, GC and local mutation rate.

**Status.** Inconclusive. Active copies have 0.097 points more rare alleles (6.7% of the missense effect); about half of it is shared with inactive copies in the same 100-kb block (regional). A separate test finds no excess over flanking DNA, and accessible copies are only 1-3% more conserved, near the artefact level.

**Publications.**

- [Active transposable-element copies carry slightly more rare human alleles than inactive copies of the same subfamily; about half of the excess is regional, little is specific to the cCRE segment](https://sciscape.io/p/04a7a4b121955cd1fd3b4b966e78a5a88f8d41b65af5f66f836e5ba9c100f213) (`sci:sha256:04a7a4b121955cd1fd3b4b966e78a5a88f8d41b65af5f66f836e5ba9c100f213`). Finding: Active TE bases not mammal-conserved had a higher fraction of AF<0.1% variants by 0.097 pp (95% CI 0.069-0.124; 27.3M variants), about 6.7% of the missense-vs-synonymous effect; random split of inactive copies gave -0.004. About half remains when compared within the same 100-kb (+0.043, CI 0.022-0.064) or 1-Mb block (+0.051, CI 0.018-0.085); active segment vs its own flank +0.031 (CI -0.005 to 0.067, n.s.). Excess in enhancer-like/TF-bound cCREs, not DNase-only (-0.001) or CTCF-only (+0.029, n.s.); only AluY significant alone. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): weak: tiny effect (about 3-7% of missense scale), half regional, segment-vs-flank not significant, near-identical young copies give 2.5x larger excess and are mapping-confounded; but large n, jackknife CIs, and de novo/mutation-rate checks.
- [In 38 transposable-element subfamilies, copies with ENCODE regulatory marks had no detectable extra purifying selection in 76,215 human genomes; their 6% variant deficit is regional](https://sciscape.io/p/af5831441480eac64dddb0615bab61e7ccc6dda23482ca7bbff6f75aa5ec4e31) (`sci:sha256:af5831441480eac64dddb0615bab61e7ccc6dda23482ca7bbff6f75aa5ec4e31`). Finding: Marked copies had 5.6% fewer variant sites than unmarked (RR 0.944, CI 0.940-0.948), but flanks 0-5 kb away had the same deficit (0.942-0.944), so relative to flanks there was no extra deficit (1.001, CI 0.996-1.005). Conserved TE bases (positive control) had 14% fewer variants (0.863); position-level difference-in-differences and motif positions also null (1.002; 1.000). Replication of Du et al.: TE-derived enhancer-like cCREs no deficit (1.006, CI 0.965-1.053), CTCF-only cCREs small deficit (0.938, CI 0.883-0.997 at mappable non-CpG). Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: large sample with matched-copy design and positive control, and bounds the effect (at most ~3% of analysable bases vs flanks); but gnomAD cannot see weak selection and young TE families are excluded.
- [In 361 human TE families, accessible copies were only slightly more conserved than inaccessible copies, most in old families, and their A/T-site excess fell as recombination rose](https://sciscape.io/p/6f21710dfe405df3e983d215607b36720ed3df30140c6a22e370bfe8b7724a26) (`sci:sha256:6f21710dfe405df3e983d215607b36720ed3df30140c6a22e370bfe8b7724a26`). Finding: Median excess +0.020 (primate) / +0.013 (mammal) phyloP per site, about 1-3% of a highly conserved site's score; grows with family age and is large in 12 ancient families (1.75% of copies). The authors state effects this size also occur as artefacts in their negative control; at A/T sites the excess turns negative in GC-rich, high-recombination regions, consistent with (not proven to be) GC-biased gene conversion. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: 361 units, 346k accessible copies, regional matching, but effect near artefact level, units non-independent, A/T deficit unexplained.

**Proposed next test (not yet run).** Use one shared matched design to resolve the two studies: compare active copies, inactive copies and 0-5 kb flanks in the same windows.

#### Weak selection in larger populations

**Scope.** Diversity deficit of human-defined non-conserved cCREs in great apes and wild mice, whose larger effective population sizes make weak selection more visible.

**Status.** Inconclusive. Apes show no slope with population size (at most 5% of bases at s=2.5e-5); wild mice show a several-fold larger deficit than humans, which rises with population size; mouse de novo mutations give no evidence for a lower mutation rate there, but one cannot be excluded, and population size is confounded with subspecies.

**Publications.**

- [In nine great-ape populations, human regulatory DNA without mammal conservation does not lose more diversity where populations are larger; at most 5% of its bases have selection near s = 2.5e-5](https://sciscape.io/p/69ea33c89abf1818cae6b26c494d761a61bf094d569b15c75ed6d8048b7ce02c) (`sci:sha256:69ea33c89abf1818cae6b26c494d761a61bf094d569b15c75ed6d8048b7ce02c`). Finding: Within genera the diversity deficit slope vs background diversity is +0.002 (-0.006 to +0.009) for the class and -0.002 (-0.010 to +0.006) for its transposon part, vs +0.048 predicted by the mouse weak-selection model; at most 5% of bases can carry s=2.5e-5 (5-20% over s 5e-6 to 2e-4). Positive controls (conserved non-coding +0.045, missense +0.041) show the rise. Exploratory +0.041 in GC-rich transposon-free elements (Holm p=0.13) is unresolved because context matching fails for GC-rich DNA. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: positive controls and explicit bounds, but only 9 small populations (some n=3-4), hg18 mapping, equilibrium and shared-activity assumptions, and post hoc subgroup signal.
- [Non-conserved regulatory DNA that aligns between human and mouse has a several-fold larger diversity deficit in wild house mice than in humans, which agrees with weak purifying selection](https://sciscape.io/p/fde7b1f489459b407abfc248dc02ba23336ff4cc83a9d471409a69695e796f8f) (`sci:sha256:fde7b1f489459b407abfc248dc02ba23336ff4cc83a9d471409a69695e796f8f`). Finding: Enhancer-like aligned non-conserved cCREs: D = 0.160-0.176 in mice vs 0.046-0.063 in humans (>=500 bp from conserved DNA: 0.118-0.145 vs 0.026-0.047). Within-mouse Ne trend modest (CAST minus GER 0.016, CI 0.000-0.032). A lower mouse mutation rate of 0.1-0.15 cannot be excluded. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): weak: species contrast confounded by different element catalogues, mutation-rate calibration (0.08-0.18 errors) and few mice; within-species Ne trend small.
- [Old regulatory DNA with no conserved base in mouse or human loses more diversity in wild mouse subspecies with larger population size; this fits weak selection but is confounded with subspecies](https://sciscape.io/p/f1c6bca37ef65adbec63541fa6795438d06fb5256fe8ddb8c577d8cb468b13f0) (`sci:sha256:f1c6bca37ef65adbec63541fa6795438d06fb5256fe8ddb8c577d8cb468b13f0`). Finding: In strictly non-conserved old cCREs, flank-relative diversity loss D rises AFG 0.039, IRA 0.070, CAS 0.108 (human 0.009, CI -0.042 to 0.061); CAS-IRA +0.038 (0.029-0.048). With GC-conservative SNVs the signal shrinks (IRA -0.004, CAS 0.045) and musculus is negative. 69% of mouse cCREs called non-conserved contain bases conserved in deeper human alignments. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): weak: three subspecies confounded with population size, two contrasts do not fit one nearly-neutral curve, callability/GC artefacts, population-specific effects the size of the signal.

**Proposed next test (not yet run).** Repeat the mouse test with a mutation-rate model from mouse de novo mutations, and with mouse-defined cCREs instead of lifted-over human ones.

### DNA conserved across mammals

**Scope.** Non-coding bases called constrained by phyloP or similar scores, and elements such as UCEs and CNEs.

**Status.** Supported within its stated scope. Conserved non-coding DNA is under selection, but the per-base signal is weaker than phyloP calls suggest.

**How this splits.** Does the study test whether the conservation signal is real (calibration), where constrained DNA lies relative to chromatin maps, whether removing it is selected against, or whether it follows its gene?

#### Is the conservation signal calibrated?

**Scope.** Nominal phyloP error rates, germline mutation rate at conserved bases, and primate-specific constraint calls against neutral ancestral-repeat DNA.

**Status.** Supported within its stated scope. phyloP calls exceed nominal rates in neutral DNA up to 40x; after mutation-rate calibration conserved non-coding bases keep only a 3-6% variant deficit vs 25-48% at coding bases; it is not hidden by recessivity (X-chromosome test).

**Publications.**

- [In 23.7 Mb of neutral human DNA the 241-mammal phyloP score exceeded its own nominal error rate by 2 to 40 times, and that error rate varied up to 25 times with sequence composition](https://sciscape.io/p/5f2b4de8759c58949609f14f333be09ee3ed56eed5def67df9f9e1c5e5074ed5) (`sci:sha256:5f2b4de8759c58949609f14f333be09ee3ed56eed5def67df9f9e1c5e5074ed5`). Finding: In 23.6M ancestral-repeat bases the realised conserved-call rate exceeds nominal by 1.9x at threshold 1, 5.9x at 2.27 and 39.9x at 4; the rate varies 11.4-fold (conserved) and 20-fold (accelerated) across 3-mer contexts. Accelerated-call rate rises 1.53-fold with recombination after context/GC standardisation (replicates on 447-way); conserved-DNA counts are robust to composition matching but not to the choice of neutral proxy. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): strong: millions of bases, replicated on a second score and under CpG masking; caveat that ancestral repeats are an imperfect neutral proxy and mechanisms are unattributed.
- [In 299,670 human de novo mutations, conserved non-coding bases mutate 3-10% less than low-score control bases, and their calibrated gnomAD deficit is 3-6%, against 25-48% at conserved coding bases](https://sciscape.io/p/a8e07f60105cbd67e4db5364720897b55c30e5b7ccb889f74f7654c8190428e8) (`sci:sha256:a8e07f60105cbd67e4db5364720897b55c30e5b7ccb889f74f7654c8190428e8`). Finding: Conserved non-coding bases mutate less than usual controls (mammal-conserved 0.898, CI 0.873-0.923 vs control A; 0.971, CI 0.942-0.999 vs control B). After mutation-rate calibration the left-over deficit is 3.3% (1.0-5.3) at primate-specific and 4.5% (2.2-6.6) at mammal-conserved bases vs 25-48% at conserved coding; mutation rate explains ~2/3 of the deficit vs control A. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: large DNM set, four call sets agree, but few DNMs per base, beta calibration unexplained, and density not frequency-spectrum based.
- [In human chromosomes 13-22, neutral repeat DNA with matched primate scores shows most of the gnomAD variant deficit in primate-specific constrained DHS cores, so it cannot confirm their function](https://sciscape.io/p/09e71f6a53f4cb183379d7ad20eceb2326e05c61cdb38df2244ece34ad94987b) (`sci:sha256:09e71f6a53f4cb183379d7ad20eceb2326e05c61cdb38df2244ece34ad94987b`). Finding: Score-matched neutral windows reproduce 72-92% of the singleton excess (intervals 39-141%) and 80-133% of the variant-site deficit of primate-only DHS cores; primate score selects low-mutation-rate DNA (neutral deficit rises from -2.4% to 10-13% with score). Per-base primate calls occur at 0.99x (0.98-1.00) the neutral rate in unannotated DNA and 1.15x (1.13-1.17) in cCREs. Positive control fails: same correction removes all signal from mammal-constrained M cores, so residual P-core signal is uninterpretable (sign design-dependent). Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: large data and multiple designs, but the method cannot measure constraint in either direction (acknowledged by failed positive control), so it shows non-confirmation, not absence; 10 autosomes only.
- [In 76,215 human genomes, the X chromosome adds ten times more variant deficit at conserved coding than at conserved non-coding sites, so strong recessive selection acts on few non-coding bases](https://sciscape.io/p/2979649e5dcb14145b8d371347404f186bf80034013c0d805e6c9da0c06c2b08) (`sci:sha256:2979649e5dcb14145b8d371347404f186bf80034013c0d805e6c9da0c06c2b08`). Finding: Extra X deficit is 0.192 (0.167-0.222) at conserved missense sites but 0.018 (0.011-0.027) at conserved non-coding sites (0.006, -0.005 to 0.015 with neutral reference near genes); pre-registered test found non-coding not more recessive than missense. Models fit h~0.2-0.3 or 1-3% fully recessive bases; fully recessive subclass with cost >=0.03 limited to 3-7.5% of conserved non-coding bases; non-conserved cCREs allow fully recessive selection (cost >=0.001) at no more than 6-7% of bases (15-21% on singletons alone). Model attributes conserved non-coding autosomal deficit to mutation-rate drop (0.05-0.06) plus selection (0.048). Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: large dataset and pre-registered contrast, but dominance is not identified (h shifts 0.1-1.0 with a +-2% X mutation offset) and the model does not fit strongly selected coding sites.

**Proposed next test (not yet run).** Recompute genome-wide 'fraction functional' estimates with calibrated phyloP thresholds and mutation-rate-matched controls.

#### Constrained DNA outside chromatin maps

**Scope.** Conserved or constrained non-coding DNA that no public open-chromatin map covers, in humans and Drosophila.

**Status.** Supported within its stated scope. In flies about half of constrained non-coding sites lie outside all maps; in humans such dark DNA is selected mainly within 1 kb of exons.

**Publications.**

- [Mammal-conserved human DNA far from mapped accessible chromatin is under strong selection in 76,215 genomes mainly within 1 kb of exons, and new chromatin catalogues add little of it](https://sciscape.io/p/e672def1d7b4fa33e7ac391314145eb88cb350bde1a428959faff21b1272b363) (`sci:sha256:e672def1d7b4fa33e7ac391314145eb88cb350bde1a428959faff21b1272b363`). Finding: Selection index 0.11 (0.07-0.15) for dark constrained bases overall, vs 0.25 in DHSs and 0.55 coding; within 1 kb of exons dark = 0.29 (0.19-0.39), equal to DHS, but >1 kb from exons only 0.08 (0.04-0.12) vs 0.22 in DHS. Added cCRE v4/CATlas DNA far from DHSs is only 2.0-2.5% constrained (genome ~3%); fetal neural tissue is the exception (7.4-12.9%). Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: large data and matched comparisons, but phyloP false positives (33-47% of dark bases) and index zero-point uncertainty weaken deep-dark estimate; functional mechanism (splicing) not tested.
- [In 197 Zambian fruit-fly genomes, half of the constrained non-coding DNA lies outside 1,345 open-chromatin maps, mostly as conserved sequence within 1 kb of the peaks](https://sciscape.io/p/9d0f6d98c9e2639f20539e346e720187661c3e48ede01a558af6b8e181f713d4) (`sci:sha256:9d0f6d98c9e2639f20539e346e720187661c3e48ede01a558af6b8e181f713d4`). Finding: Constraint C_D: never-open non-coding 0.53 (0.52-0.55; 29.1 Mb) vs open non-coding 0.66 and 4-fold synonymous 0.28; never-open conserved 0.82 (16.4 Mb) vs never-open non-conserved 0.16 (0.14-0.18); fully dark 0.48. About half of constrained non-coding sites are outside all maps (72% at ~79 experiments to 50% at 1,345, not saturated); mostly conserved DNA within 1 kb of peaks. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: large N, 1-Mb block bootstrap, two constraint estimators agree, but constraint is relative to short-intron reference (itself possibly not neutral), SFS misfit unresolved, DFE strength not identified, one population.

**Proposed next test (not yet run).** Test whether dark constrained DNA near exons predicts splicing changes in public splicing QTL data.

#### Selection against deleting conserved DNA

**Scope.** Natural deletions and copy-number variants in gnomAD that remove conserved non-coding DNA, including UCEs.

**Status.** Supported within its stated scope. Deleting 512-2,047 conserved bases is selected against about as strongly as deleting weakly constrained genes; UCEs are not more depleted than equally conserved DNA.

**Publications.**

- [In 63,046 human genomes, 1-10 kb deletions of 512-2,047 conserved non-coding bases are about as rare as coding deletions in weakly constrained genes; non-conserved regulatory DNA matters less](https://sciscape.io/p/8f0a2b205e77f1c63e76203862e5704112483e14f268e732bd41931e7ec70504) (`sci:sha256:8f0a2b205e77f1c63e76203862e5704112483e14f268e732bd41931e7ec70504`). Finding: In 1-10 kb deletions, removing 512-2,047 conserved non-coding bases raises singleton probability by +8.3 (1.8-14.7) / +4.1 (1.3-6.9) points, comparable to deleting coding sequence of weakly constrained genes (+6.9 / +4.0). Non-conserved cCRE DNA of equal amount gives +2.0 (0.9-3.1) / +0.7 (-0.5-2.0) points (ratio to conserved 0.02-0.42, model-dependent). Element-free spacer DNA between validated enhancer and gene: +0.0 (-2.9 to 3.0); lncRNA exon extra bases +0.2 (-1.2 to 1.6). Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: conserved dose-response is replicated on a larger callset, but the non-conserved effect size depends on model form/band and spacer/lncRNA nulls have wide CIs that do not exclude modest effects.
- [In 63,046 human genomes, ultraconserved elements lose and gain copies less often than neutral DNA but not detectably less often than equally conserved non-UCE DNA at the same loci](https://sciscape.io/p/234f62506ba53421d124d87c203b379a41b68b856f720700ee10166c8f0cfacf) (`sci:sha256:234f62506ba53421d124d87c203b379a41b68b856f720700ee10166c8f0cfacf`). Finding: UCEs are depleted vs neutral windows (deletions RR 0.60, 0.48-0.75; duplications 0.73, 0.56-0.95) but conservation-matched non-UCE windows are depleted similarly (0.64, 0.64); UCE vs non-UCE pooled 0.91 (0.61-1.36); all 8 pooled estimates 0.65-0.92 and non-significant. At 80% power only rate ratios <=0.56 are detectable, so a modest UCE-specific effect is not excluded. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: well matched and robust, but 362 UCEs and 144 conserved controls, wide CIs, SVs far larger than windows, short-read duplication calls.

**Proposed next test (not yet run).** Fit a dose-response of singleton probability against deleted conserved bases with coding deletions as the yardstick.

#### Do conserved elements follow their gene?

**Scope.** Conserved non-coding elements assigned to genes that were lost in some mammal lineages.

**Status.** Supported within its stated scope. CNEs of lost genes gain 20% more substitutions (OR 1.20, 1.12-1.29) but keep about 87-90% of their constraint, so most are not tied to that gene alone.

**Publications.**

- [After 777 gene losses in 18 mammal species, conserved non-coding DNA near the lost gene kept an estimated 87-90% of its constraint, while the gene's coding sequence decayed most of the way to neutral](https://sciscape.io/p/4b7673b9b5cdeb8a509fed422140f89a37d9948824a3f9a520855fb75a567a82) (`sci:sha256:4b7673b9b5cdeb8a509fed422140f89a37d9948824a3f9a520855fb75a567a82`). Finding: CNEs of lost genes had 20% more lineage substitutions (OR 1.20, 1.12-1.29; CNE-specific 1.16, 1.09-1.24) while the gene's coding sequence decayed phi=0.92 (0.81-1.02) vs CNE phi=0.09 (0.05-0.13); estimated share of CNE constraint lost f=0.098 (0.057-0.142; two-stage 0.038-0.160), ~87-90% retained. Neighbouring intact genes' CNEs show smaller excess (1.08); lost-vs-neighbour difference not significant (1.07, 0.98-1.15), so part may be regional. Excess 1.36 at disturbed-synteny loci vs 1.12 intact. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: 777 events with coding positive control and placebo, but only 53% of planned events (download failure), aligned bases only, f-calibration assumptions, and nearest-gene assignment is not specific.

**Proposed next test (not yet run).** Use 3D contact data to test whether retained CNEs regulate other neighbouring genes.

### Proposed sequence features in other DNA

**Scope.** Sequence features claimed to matter in transcribed or unannotated DNA: RNA structure, splice sites, small ORFs, motifs, G-quadruplexes, nucleosome signals.

**Status.** Inconclusive. No proposed feature showed selection of the strength seen at coding or conserved controls. Apparent selection on G-quadruplexes depended on how mutation context was matched, and apparent selection on nucleosome-positioning signals came from the mapped cells' own alleles. New moderate TF-motif matches show no selection, but losses of existing matches show about a quarter to a third of the signal at mammal-conserved sites.

**How this splits.** Does the feature act in RNA (structure, splicing, translation, transcription) or in DNA (motifs, G-quadruplexes, nucleosome positioning)?

#### RNA-level features

**Scope.** Base pairing in predicted conserved RNA structures, lncRNA splice sites, translated small ORFs, pseudogene 3'UTRs, and sense-strand words in introns.

**Status.** Ruled out within its stated scope. Constraint of positive elements rises with target s_het (Spearman 0.28, p=2e-11); DHS breadth explains more variance (R2 gain 0.029) than CRISPRi status (0.001); gnomAD-SV deletions 27% fewer at positives (p=0.02, uncorrected); Gnocchi shows no group difference. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: target-dosage-sensitivity link is consistent across screens/measures, but the headline function-vs-constraint contrast is marginal, negatives are a noisy null (K562 only, low power for 10% effects), and distance partly explains pair-level specificity.
- [Mouse papers reporting one enhancer deletion found a defect in 73% of deletions, papers reporting several in 41% (53% without 3 redundancy studies); conservation showed no detectable link](https://sciscape.io/p/1756317efc9cb8867e6155ed6462d9e23c7e951e839c914966a72586b3f39014) (`sci:sha256:1756317efc9cb8867e6155ed6462d9e23c7e951e839c914966a72586b3f39014`). Finding: 132/226 (58%; 49-68%) show a defect: 73% in single-deletion papers vs 41% in multi-enhancer papers (OR 3.8, 1.8-7.8; 73% vs 53%, OR 2.3, 1.2-4.4 without three redundancy-dissection papers). Conservation not detectably linked (OR 1.06, 0.74-1.51 per SD phastCons; 1.18, 0.75-1.84 phyloP; tertile rates 52/60/59%), with ORs up to ~1.7-1.9 not excluded. Loss of target expression strongly linked (71% vs 12%, OR 18, 5-56). Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): weak: agent-curated calls (no human double curation; 82% full text), publication bias, range-restricted conserved candidates, so the conservation null is underpowered; paper-type and expression-loss results are stronger.
- [Transcription-factor binding in mammal-conserved promoter DNA predicted gene response to factor knockdown in K562 cells; non-conserved promoter or distal binding had no detectable effect](https://sciscape.io/p/664c3bee776d0388a6d881ae48e3200b7e12a2818759d43252654f8fba1669b5) (`sci:sha256:664c3bee776d0388a6d881ae48e3200b7e12a2818759d43252654f8fba1669b5`). Finding: Per-peak effect on probability of response (mean 2.1%): promoter conserved +0.146 pp (0.065-0.221) vs non-conserved +0.029 (-0.060-0.142); difference -0.117 (-0.23 to +0.02; P=0.07) in the pre-specified main model; significant only in strongest-signal third and down-regulated subsets. Distal (10-100 kb) both classes null (upper limit ~0.07 pp). Placebo TF-swap gives 0.000. ENCODE bulk knockdown replication found no binding effect in any class with opposite point-estimate order. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): weak: main-model contrast n.s., effect tiny, carried by HOT promoters, disappears in top-expressed genes, independent dataset did not replicate, several analyses post hoc, one cell line.

**Proposed next test (not yet run).** Restrict to enhancers whose target is dosage-sensitive, where constraint rises with s_het.

#### Effects of non-conserved active DNA

**Scope.** eQTL and trait fine-mapping in cCREs without conserved bases, and reporter vs CRISPRi activity of TE-derived open chromatin.

**Status.** Supported within its stated scope. Non-conserved cCREs are 1.4-1.6x enriched for causal eQTLs, roughly 5-15 times their share of the selection signal. For 94 UK Biobank traits they are 1.66x enriched after adjusting for repeat type, but 1.04x without that adjustment, because most of their bases are transposon DNA, which holds few causal variants. TE-derived open chromatin is more active than other open chromatin in a reporter assay, but CRISPRi does not show that it controls nearby genes more often. Neighbouring active DNA raises an enhancer's chance of regulating its gene within tiled genes (weak evidence, mostly 21 genes).

**Publications.**

- [Human regulatory elements without mammal-conserved bases are 1.4-1.6 times enriched for causal eQTL variants in 440,983 credible sets, a third to a half of the fold for conserved elements](https://sciscape.io/p/8308b0567814e9a9982209a87e7ed9d192a1ca0950c50a6bf29fba1a2b0f9843) (`sci:sha256:8308b0567814e9a9982209a87e7ed9d192a1ca0950c50a6bf29fba1a2b0f9843`). Finding: A variant in tier-0 cCREs is 1.60 (GTEx; 1.46-1.77) / 1.54 / 1.37 times as likely to be the causal eQTL as other non-coding variants (tier-2: 4.41/3.13/2.47) and 1.30 (0.94-1.70) for disease; non-conserved share R of excess 0.17-0.25 (eQTL), 0.11 (-0.03-0.24) disease. This is ~5-15x the selection share (1.5-1.9%) from the companion study, i.e. expression effects with little fitness effect. Broadly open promoter-proximal tier-0 cCREs are as enriched as tier 2; narrow distal tier-0 (78%) show no GTEx excess (-0.1%, -1.1 to +0.8). Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: very large fine-mapping sample and consistent across eQTL sources, but estimator attenuates folds and overstates R by 15-40%, selection comparison is across different scales, and tier-0 is confounded with transposon content/alignment depth.
- [In 94 UK Biobank traits, regulatory elements without mammal conservation hold few extra causal variants, and their size relative to conserved DNA depends on which conserved DNA is the yardstick](https://sciscape.io/p/dda77b70249a8a4d24d4705c0d859a99f04c54465eb85a300b123fd359a35bc5) (`sci:sha256:dda77b70249a8a4d24d4705c0d859a99f04c54465eb85a300b123fd359a35bc5`). Finding: Tier-0 cCRE odds of being a causal trait variant OR 1.66 (1.19-2.14) vs non-conserved non-cCRE DNA (conserved classes 3-40x); 72% of tier-0 is TE and without repeat covariate OR was 1.04 (0.88-1.19, mean-PIP rule). Conserved-equivalent fraction for traits ranges 1.7% to 34% depending on yardstick (selection-based estimate 1.5-1.9%); eQTL OR for tier-0 2.03 (1.88-2.18). Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: large fine-mapped sets with block bootstrap, but results hinge on PIP rule, LD inside elements, repeat covariate and yardstick; yardstick dependence found by reviewers.
- [Transposon-derived open chromatin in human K562 cells is more active than other open chromatin in a reporter assay, but CRISPRi screens show no sign that it controls genes more often](https://sciscape.io/p/d2d00c41fb2b053bba0b7d1179b5fdf4a9e420e836c95e052bc1d075ef42ad44) (`sci:sha256:d2d00c41fb2b053bba0b7d1179b5fdf4a9e420e836c95e052bc1d075ef42ad44`). Finding: lentiMPRA: TE-derived tiles active 46.0% vs 38.4% (adjusted OR 1.54, 1.37-1.72; LTR 2.06). CRISPRi: TE-derived positive 3.7% vs 4.7%, adjusted OR 0.83 (0.60-1.17, p=0.29); 0.95 (0.68-1.33) with gene type; pooled with held-out cells 0.96 (0.70-1.31). The authors call the main CRISPRi estimate inconclusive. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): weak: wide CRISPRi CI (13 TE positive elements in held-out set), 89% of positives from K562, model error corrected post review, guides avoid repeats.
- [In K562 CRISPRi screens, enhancers with active neighbors are needed more often than other enhancers of the same gene, mainly at tiled loci, not across genes; a 5-kb neighbor feature adds no accuracy](https://sciscape.io/p/5176448a5def1b728e21f47d55e23dc1e2bfb3634b9f47001864b7690992cac1) (`sci:sha256:5176448a5def1b728e21f47d55e23dc1e2bfb3634b9f47001864b7690992cac1`). Finding: Within genes (3,071 pairs, 147 genes) active DNA 1-10 kb away OR 2.0 per IQR (1.1-3.6), separate DNase elements OR 1.5 (1.0-2.1), super-enhancer OR 2.2 (1.2-4.2), mostly from 21 tiled genes; across all pairs the same terms are 0.58 (0.44-0.75), 0.98, 0.98 because promoter-dense regions have fewer enhancers (OR 0.43 per IQR). Removing the published sumNearbyEnhancers feature changes held-out AUPRC by +0.003 (-0.005 to 0.012). Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): weak: within-gene effect rests on 21 tiled genes with no independent replication (held-out has 11 informative genes), single cell line, binary calls, and a Mundlak split gave opposite sign.

**Proposed next test (not yet run).** Test whether non-conserved cCRE eQTLs are older or younger than matched eQTLs, to separate tolerated effects from recent ones.

### lncRNA function claims

**Scope.** Published lncRNA claims: Cas13d essentiality calls, miRNA sponging, and knockout phenotypes that copy the neighbouring gene.

**Status.** Inconclusive. About 198 of 764 gene-level Cas13d essential calls (788 published calls) are supported; claimed growth direction does not predict screen drop-out; no sponge claim reaches the binding-site threshold; neighbour-like phenotypes follow naming ascertainment.

**Publications.**

- [In a published Cas13d screen of 5,496 human lncRNAs, single-cell-line essentiality calls largely match the rate of the screen's own negative controls; about 200 of 788 survive](https://sciscape.io/p/b83b722b4f77b763455bd31af80ec12ef773be05564e75c0687b9a4d9ffbc4d7) (`sci:sha256:b83b722b4f77b763455bd31af80ec12ef773be05564e75c0687b9a4d9ffbc4d7`). Finding: Single-line calls: 626 observed vs 521 expected from controls (false-discovery proportion 0.83, 0.74-0.93); calls in >=2 lines: 138 vs 42 expected (FDP 0.32). About 198 (126-271) of 764 gene-level calls supported; only 33 survive BH q<0.05. Single-line calls do not replicate in CasRx (OR 1.2, P=0.62); multi-line do (OR 4.8). Enrichment concentrates in small-RNA host loci (snoRNA/miRNA hosts); after removing them, conserved-base excess at q<0.05 set is n.s. Published age-conservation claim shrinks (OR 1.92 -> 1.29 -> 1.19 after expression and guide-count adjustment). Guide count per gene confounds call rate 160-fold. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: internal-null calibration with an independent-screen replication, but depends on negative-control modelling (122-198 supported), priors recorded after exploratory FDR calc, authors' tables only.
- [In 805 published claims that name the cell line, the long non-coding RNA said to sponge a microRNA supplies a median 0.003% of its binding sites, hundreds of times below thresholds measured in cells](https://sciscape.io/p/6011854fb759ee84ba35535942b2751ef7da7e47722017524496b4b218272107) (`sci:sha256:6011854fb759ee84ba35535942b2751ef7da7e47722017524496b4b218272107`). Finding: Median sponge fraction f is 3.0e-5 for 805 claims naming a cell line; 0 reach the 12% threshold, 5 (0.6%) reach 2.8%, 20 (2.5%) reach 0.9% (a deliberately generous construct). MALAT1, PVT1 and RMRP are under-counted by poly(A) RNA-seq, so many MALAT1 claims could approach thresholds. In 40% of claims the lncRNA is <1 TPM in every named line. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): moderate: large claim corpus (extraction error ~1-3%) and generous assumptions, but thresholds come from a few titration experiments in mouse ESCs and copy numbers are RNA-seq estimates; sponging not directly tested.
- [In curated mouse knockouts, 21 of 77 lncRNA genes had a phenotype like the nearest gene's knockout, 2.7 times the gene-pair rate, mostly lncRNAs named after that gene](https://sciscape.io/p/d465f5c3919d0bd72671564a4fe7b9f0e1281f4cb41242f9234ad846999d0484) (`sci:sha256:d465f5c3919d0bd72671564a4fe7b9f0e1281f4cb41242f9234ad846999d0484`). Finding: 21 of 77 non-imprinted, non-host lncRNAs (27.3%, CI 18.6-38.1%) copy the neighbour's phenotype (top 5%) vs 8.8% for protein-coding gene pairs (Mantel-Haenszel OR 3.4, CI 2.0-5.8, p=2e-6). Excess is concentrated in lncRNAs named after the neighbour (46.4% vs 16.3%, p=0.007); intragenic miRNAs copy their host at 26%, so copying does not prove cis action. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): weak: about 77 loci, ascertainment and cis effects not separable, distance/naming analyses unplanned.
- [Mouse lncRNA knockouts resemble genes their papers name on other chromosomes nearly as much as named nearby genes, so phenotype resemblance in curated data is not evidence of local action](https://sciscape.io/p/ae4e2cfec0033b641243b1e35b4d4dad342a11edb4613947b64d5466de9b3d50) (`sci:sha256:ae4e2cfec0033b641243b1e35b4d4dad342a11edb4613947b64d5466de9b3d50`). Finding: lncRNA KO resembled named genes on other chromosomes (0.803, CI 0.739-0.860, n=33) almost as much as named genes within 1 Mb (0.841, CI 0.758-0.913, n=29); paired difference -0.015 (-0.20 to +0.14, 9 loci). Regulatory-region KOs: 0.904 near vs 0.780 elsewhere. Unnamed lncRNA KOs resembled nearest gene above baseline (+0.086, CI +0.010 to +0.163) but not clearly above microRNA KOs. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): weak: small groups (9-39 loci), curation and publication bias; but suggests the resemblance test is not specific; it does not test lncRNA function itself.
- [lncRNA loci with a published growth claim drop out more than matched controls in three human screens, but the claimed direction predicts nothing detectable](https://sciscape.io/p/3bbf04a7940d133b8f3610d2a90b93fd8d34ba87e9d5154e464bb2571bab691f) (`sci:sha256:3bbf04a7940d133b8f3610d2a90b93fd8d34ba87e9d5154e464bb2571bab691f`). Finding: Claimed loci drop out more than expression-matched controls (Cas13d, 186 pairs: mean percentile 0.435, CI 0.389-0.482; 2.2x expected hits), but claimed direction does not predict the outcome (difference -0.012, CI -0.102 to +0.078). lncRNA claims are not weaker than protein-coding claims (-0.017, CI -0.086 to +0.053; with the abstract's cell line -0.134, CI -0.252 to -0.013, driven largely by MALAT1). Authors say the screens cannot be shown to test claim truth, and the data cannot exclude full directional validity. Evidence strength (orchestrator's reading): weak: modest claim-pair counts, wide CIs that include the full directional signal, claim extraction from abstracts, ascertainment confounded.

**Proposed next test (not yet run).** Re-test the about 200 supported Cas13d calls against CRISPRi data for the same lines.

## How to read the statuses

Statuses describe this swarm's tests within their stated assumptions. 'Ruled out' means the tested strong form of the claim was excluded with a positive control; weaker forms remain possible. 'Supported' means the node's stated result held under the swarm's controls; it does not mean that the DNA class is functional. 'Inconclusive' includes underpowered nulls and conflicting studies; a failed test of one measure does not rule out the approach. Next tests are proposals and have not been run.
