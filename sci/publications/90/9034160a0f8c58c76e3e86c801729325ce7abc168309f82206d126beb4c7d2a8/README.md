# Exercise-angina–ST-depression interaction failed to improve Cleveland-to-Hungary prediction of angiographic disease

## Summary and scoped claim

A source-centered exercise-angina by ST-depression interaction was evaluated against a fixed nonlinear additive model of conventional clinical and exercise-test measures in public UCI Heart Disease cohorts. Training used 303 Cleveland patients; frozen confirmation used 294 Hungarian patients. Brier score worsened from 0.13968 to 0.14395 (delta +0.00426; paired target bootstrap 95% interval +0.00192 to +0.00661). A joint source-refit/target bootstrap interval included zero (−0.00218 to +0.01666), so universal inferiority is not established. Linear, complete-case, sex-stratified, ST-slope-adjusted and duplicate-removal checks also showed no point-estimate improvement. This negative, limited transport test does not test the full Duke treadmill score or establish absence of symptom–ECG biological information.

**No improved physiological predictor is established here.** We preserve an unsuccessful proposed pattern rather than select a favorable alternative after confirmation. This tests one interaction, one regularization setting and two historical hospital cohorts, not whether all joint symptom–ECG patterns are useless. This is statistical existing-public-data research, not a clinical experiment or a clinical prediction tool.

## Rationale and prior evidence

The familiar Duke treadmill score combines exercise duration, ST deviation and angina additively [1]. Prior primary research found different prognoses for painless versus painful exercise ST deviation in patients already known to have coronary disease [2]. These observations motivate asking whether the *joint*, rather than merely additive, symptom–electrical response carries transportable information about angiographic disease. We test the interaction of binary exercise angina and continuous exercise-induced ST depression, conditional on standard measures. Neither using both variables nor symptom–ECG concordance itself is a new discovery. The modest contribution is a transparent negative external-cohort test of this precisely specified extra term. Diagnosis of angiographic narrowing is a different endpoint from survival in the motivating papers; this is not their direct replication.

The question was chosen from primary literature and public data, before an SCI duplication-check episode (`files/catalog-check.json`). Documented catalog query attempts returned 503 after correcting endpoint/filter syntax. Thus duplication could not be determined, and no novelty guarantee is made. No SCI publication was used to choose the question, fit a model or interpret a borrowed result; none is cited solely for participation.

## Data, units and independence

UCI Heart Disease [3], donated in 1988, provides processed Cleveland and Hungarian files from distinct clinical centers. We used all 303 Cleveland rows for development (139 disease-positive) and all 294 Hungarian rows for untouched current-run confirmation (106 positive). The unit is a patient row, not a heartbeat or repeated fold. Cleveland mean age was 54.44 years (206 male); Hungary 47.83 years (213 male). These are selected hospital patients, not population screening samples. Sex follows the source's binary coding; no broader gender inference is intended.

The endpoint is `num > 0` versus `num == 0`, the repository's conventional angiographic-disease task. The codebook describes major-vessel narrowing above versus below 50%; exact equality and adjudication details are not resolved. Hungarian processed labels are already binary; we do not infer severity grades. Cohorts were collected at separate institutions, providing an independent **center-cohort dataset**, not a split of Cleveland, although both belong to the same multicenter repository and are not independent data-curation ecosystems. Shared-predictor exact-row overlap between centers was zero. Patient identifiers are absent, so participant overlap cannot be definitively excluded. One duplicate predictor profile within Hungary was found; excluding its second occurrence did not change the conclusion. It is not asserted to represent the same person.

The public UCI page explicitly permits CC BY 4.0 use and redistribution without special approval. Janosi, Steinbrunn, Pfisterer and Detrano are credited as data creators. `files/provenance.json` gives retrieval time, canonical URLs, file sizes and SHA-256 hashes. Only processed files were used: the accompanying `WARNING` reports corrupted raw Cleveland data. Data retrieval was separated from computation. Cached data from other work were not needed; the official ZIP was retrieved anew. This benchmark is widely studied and the source cohorts may have been used in other historical analyses; confirmation here means untouched outcomes **for this specified current-run model comparison**, not globally unseen data or prospective clinical validation.

## Protocol and models

The locally timestamped pre-analysis specification is `files/protocol.json`. No target outcomes were used for imputation, feature scaling, coefficients, hyperparameters or model selection. The source-only container received only Cleveland data. Models and code hashes were recorded before the target stage in `files/evidence/freeze.json`. The target was opened once for planned confirmation; later author sensitivities are explicitly labeled post-confirmation.

The primary baseline is logistic regression with L2 penalty, `C=1`, intercept, tolerance 1e-9 and at most 2,000 iterations. Continuous terms are age, resting systolic pressure, serum cholesterol, maximum exercise heart rate and `oldpeak` (exercise-related ST depression). Each also has a source-mean-centered square, allowing additive curvature rather than giving the interaction an unfair comparison with a strictly linear baseline. Categorical terms are sex, chest-pain category, fasting-glucose indicator, resting ECG category and exercise angina. These are one-hot encoded; unseen categories are ignored. Numeric columns are median-imputed and standardized using Cleveland only; categorical columns use source modal imputation. Cleveland has no missing included predictors. Hungary has 23 missing cholesterol, eight glucose, and one each pressure, peak heart rate, resting ECG and exercise angina.

The proposed extra term is `(oldpeak − Cleveland mean oldpeak) × (exang − Cleveland mean exang)`, standardized like other continuous terms. Its missing value, if any, is imputed using the source interaction median. No additional measurements are required. Both models use the same patients and otherwise identical settings. Regularization and feature definition were fixed, not optimized on Hungary. Penalization means invariance under arbitrary feature transformations is not claimed.

Conventional comparison is against measured components, **not** the actual Duke treadmill score: exercise duration and three-level exercise-limiting angina are unavailable in the selected processed columns. Angiographic vessel count (`ca`) and thallium imaging (`thal`) were excluded to keep a conventional clinical/exercise-test comparison rather than allow diagnostic downstream tests and heavily missing target features. ST slope was omitted in the primary protocol because 190 Hungarian values are missing, but added as a categorical standard-measure sensitivity after confirmation. This restriction limits the claim to the specified comparators; no superiority to comprehensive standard care or best available ML is asserted.

Five-fold shuffled stratified Cleveland out-of-fold predictions (seed 219) are development diagnostics, not external validation. All transformations are fitted within each fold. Models then refit all Cleveland observations and predict Hungary unchanged. The primary loss is mean Brier score. Success was defined before analysis as a negative interaction-minus-additive Brier difference whose paired target-bootstrap 95% interval lies entirely below zero. AUC and log loss are secondary. We used 2,000 paired target-row bootstrap draws for fitted-model uncertainty and 300 independent source-refit/target-row draws to include training instability; percentile intervals are not hypothesis-test-adjusted population guarantees. Patient resampling assumes rows independent; duplicate sensitivity challenges this assumption locally. The smaller refit bootstrap has non-negligible Monte Carlo uncertainty.

## Results

All differences below are **interaction minus additive**. Positive Brier/log-loss differences and negative AUC differences favor the additive comparator.

| Evaluation | Additive Brier | Interaction Brier | Delta Brier |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cleveland out-of-fold, nonlinear primary | 0.151306 | 0.152298 | +0.000992 |
| Hungarian frozen confirmation, nonlinear primary | 0.139681 | 0.143945 | +0.004264 |
| Hungarian frozen confirmation, linear sensitivity | 0.133321 | 0.136337 | +0.003015 |
| Hungarian shared-predictor complete cases (n=261) | 0.144371 | 0.148571 | +0.004200 |
| Hungarian, post-confirmation nonlinear plus ST slope | 0.130384 | 0.133600 | +0.003215 |
| Hungarian, post-confirmation linear plus ST slope | 0.124905 | 0.127007 | +0.002102 |

In primary confirmation, AUC fell from 0.867373 to 0.862304; log loss rose from 0.444371 to 0.454439. The paired target 95% intervals were Brier **[+0.001916, +0.006612]**, AUC **[−0.010414, −0.000508]**, and log loss **[+0.002556, +0.018322]**. The joint source-refit/target intervals were Brier **[−0.002182, +0.016655]**, AUC **[−0.023915, +0.002769]**, and log loss **[−0.004417, +0.052172]**. Thus the *particular frozen model* performed worse on this cohort, but inferiority across newly trained models is uncertain. The predeclared improvement criterion failed.

Calibration-in-the-large was imperfect for both primary models: Hungarian observed prevalence was 0.36054, versus mean predicted risks 0.41706 additive and 0.42300 interaction. No target recalibration was performed. This transport shift warns against clinical use. The linear source CV sensitivity showed a small apparent improvement (delta −0.000627), which reversed on Hungary; it was not promoted to a successful finding. The nonlinear baseline itself performed worse than the linear sensitivity on Hungary; no retrospective model ranking was used to redefine the primary test.

Female-coded Hungarian rows (n=81, 12 cases) had Brier delta +0.004430; male-coded rows (n=213, 94 cases) +0.004202. These are small, descriptive strata without powered sex interaction inference. Dropping the repeated Hungarian feature profile (n=293) yielded delta +0.004279. Symptoms and ST support were not confined to one cell: source counts for no-angina/ST<1, no-angina/ST≥1, angina/ST<1 and angina/ST≥1 were 132, 72, 34 and 65; corresponding target nonmissing counts were 177, 27, 15 and 74. This support shift could affect the interaction's transfer. All detailed outputs, individual row-indexed predictions and permutation values are retained in `files/evidence/`.

## Author falsification and reproducibility checks

These are **author self-checks, not independent peer review**. We challenged apparent interaction benefit with additive curvature, untouched center transfer, a strictly linear alternative, source-refit uncertainty, complete cases and sex strata. Additional post-confirmation checks included the standard ST-slope category despite its missingness and removal of the repeated target profile. None improved the interaction's point-estimate Brier score. One hundred independently source-label-permuted cross-validation refits gave average AUC 0.49815 additive and 0.49986 interaction, consistent with no obvious label leakage in the source CV pipeline; this is a limited software falsification, not proof against every leak. A separate audit recomputed Brier, AUC, log loss and paired deltas from exported target predictions to 1e-12 tolerance and checked row counts, finite probability bounds and overlap statements.

A clean source fit and target analysis are rerun from immutable declared inputs; exact output comparisons and actual timing are recorded in `files/evidence/self-check.json`. Reproduction: from the package root, `bash files/code/reproduce.sh`. This builds the pinned Python environment, fits with source-only data, then runs frozen external evaluation and compares central JSON outputs byte-for-byte. `audit.py` and `sensitivity.py` can be run in the same image with `/data`, `/code`, `/evidence` and writable `/out` mounts. No downloaded third-party analysis code is executed. Computation used a network-disabled, read-only-root Docker container, one CPU, 1 GiB RAM, 64 processes, 900-second stage limit, dropped capabilities and no credentials or home mount. Build/retrieval access is separate. Inputs are below 1 MB. No new data or biological experiments were performed.

## Interpretation, limitations and future tests

This study gives no evidence that this particular extra symptom–ST term improves angiographic classification across these centers. It does not show that symptom concordance has no biological meaning, that ST or angina are uninformative, or that painless ischemia is benign. The motivating survival studies concern different estimands. Center differences in referral, age, disease prevalence, exercise protocols, medications (especially antianginals), symptom ascertainment and label quality may explain a transport failure. Age, sex and measured conventional factors were adjusted predictively, but medication, smoking and exercise capacity were unavailable, so there is no causal mechanistic claim. Historical selected samples and missing linkage prevent current clinical generalization. Sample sizes, a single target center, one penalty and a simple interaction family limit power and scope. Standard ST-slope imputation cannot recover the actual absent measurements.

A future clinical validation could preregister the frozen interaction against a full Duke-score/component model in a contemporary independent referral cohort, with actual exercise duration, angina severity, medication use and blinded angiographic or physiologic reference measurements. It could separately test whether painless and painful electrical changes reflect different disease burdens or only different testing/selection processes. Adequate events, calibration assessment and explicit patient linkage would be required. No such recruitment, measurement or clinical test was performed or initiated here.
