# MCHC–RDW interaction does not establish improved cross-cycle HbA1c prediction beyond fasting glucose and routine red-cell indices

## Central result and authorship
Adding standardized MCHC×RDW to a nonlinear routine-marker model did not establish improved HbA1c prediction from fasting glucose in separately sampled NHANES2009–2010 adults without diagnosed diabetes. Trained in2005–2006(n=1583), the model increased target(n=2196) fasting-weighted MSE by0.000216876 percentage-points squared (baseline-minus-augmented gain−0.000216876;95% joint source-refit/target PSU bootstrap interval−0.00525300 to0.00160656). These data do not establish equivalence, absent red-cell biology, or clinical utility; cross-cycle HbA1c distribution shifts and single fasting glucose limit interpretation.

## Motivation and question lock
[Hardikar et al., Diabetes Care2012, doi10.2337/dc11-1321](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3308281/) found that red-cell indices contributed information about HbA1c beyond glucose in young Indian participants. [A2022 assay-comparison study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35975502/) motivates caution about anemia and assay-related interpretation. These studies motivate testing a **joint MCHC–RDW pattern**, not a claim that red-cell associations are new. Can their product improve transported HbA1c prediction beyond nonlinear glucose and the individual red-cell indices? We test one interaction omitted by an additive-marker analysis, not something demonstrated to be universally unused in current medicine.

## Outcome, standard comparator and methods
Outcome: measured LBXGH HbA1c percentage points. Predictor LBXGLU is fasting glucose(mg/dL), not continuous mean glycemia. Thus this is **conditional HbA1c prediction**, not measured HbA1c–CGM discordance, intrinsic erythrocyte survival, or a causal glycation phenotype. The [CBC codebook](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2005/DataFiles/CBC_D.htm) defines MCHC(LBXMC,g/dL), RDW(LBXRDW,%), hemoglobin(LBXHGB,g/dL) and MCV(LBXMCVSI,fL). [Fasting-glucose documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2005/DataFiles/GLU_D.htm) specifies WTSAF2YR; these weights, normalized to mean1 separately per fitting/evaluation sample, are used here rather than whole-MEC weights.

Baseline: ridge(alpha1) with unpenalized intercept; additive cubic B-splines(3 uniform knots, include_bias=False, constant extrapolation) of age, BMI, fasting glucose, hemoglobin, MCV, MCHC and RDW, plus one-hot sex and race/ethnicity(drop first). Knots, encodings and weighted feature standardization are learned **only on source records**. Proposed feature: product of source-weight-standardized MCHC and RDW, then source-weight-standardized as a feature. Augmented ridge adds precisely this one feature; alpha is not target-tuned. All code is in files/analyze.py. The comparator explicitly includes known MCHC/RDW main effects and routine anemia/glucose measures; it is not a validated clinical score.

Primary metric: target fasting-weighted MSE(baseline)−MSE(augmented); positive means improvement. Locked success criterion: positive gain and95% interval excluding zero. Source5fold CV groups by design PSU; preprocessing is refit within folds. The source model then remains fixed for target evaluation. Uncertainty:400 seeded stratified Rao–Wu PSU replicates independently for source and target, refitting source models and evaluating target errors each time. Each stratum with m PSUs draws m−1 PSUs and scales counts by m/(m−1). Source strata all contain2 PSUs; retaining positive-weight records and refitting quantile-free uniform knots is equivalent to repeated selected records here. Weights are renormalized per replicate; target strata can contain3 PSUs. The percentile interval includes source fitting and target sampling uncertainty but is not adjusted for the broader program of prior hypotheses. It does not prove equivalence. Code uses seed20261118; bootstrap/permutation draws are supplied.

## Post-primary assay/support diagnostics
CDC's [GHB_F analytic warning](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2009/DataFiles/GHB_F.htm) reports a higher HbA1c distribution in2007–2010 than earlier cycles, with no cause established after evaluation; original values were re-released unchanged. The previously suggested crossover correction was withdrawn. We used original values and do not infer a biological temporal increase. This unresolved measurement/distribution shift limits cross-cycle interpretation.

The target weighted mean prediction-minus-observed residual was-0.167890 percentage points(baseline),-0.166241(augmented). Subtracting each model's target residual mean for **evaluation only** gave centered-error gain-0.00076792; it is not deployable target-blind recalibration. On2186 target records within source marginal ranges of every numeric predictor, frozen-model gain was-0.00026072. Marginal support is not full multivariate support. The top1% target weighted squared errors contributed24.67% of baseline error. These diagnostics do not convert a negative primary test into a positive one.

## Interpretation, limitations and future tests
This is a scoped **negative predictive test** of one product and baseline. It neither establishes an improved biomarker nor refutes known red-cell effects on HbA1c. Absence of a reliable gain is not equivalence. Uncertainty, sign reversals and model sensitivity preclude recommending a clinical correction. Untreated/undiagnosed diabetes, iron deficiency, kidney disease, hemoglobin variants, medications, inflammation, diet and unmeasured glucose dynamics can confound conditional HbA1c. We did not measure red-cell survival, CGM, reticulocytes, iron status or prospective disease outcomes. Complete-case restriction, tail observations, substantial assay/distribution concerns and common survey methods restrict scope. We cannot claim that biology absent from standard markers was discovered.

A future preregistered observational clinical study could measure repeated HbA1c, CGM mean glycemia, iron status/reticulocytes and standardized CBCs in a separate cohort, lock additive and interaction models, define a clinically meaningful improvement margin, and test held-out prediction/calibration across laboratories. That would separate single-fasting-glucose noise, assay variation and erythrocyte dynamics. It has not been performed here. This publication reports only existing-public-data computation.
