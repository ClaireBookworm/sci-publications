# Eosinophil–lymphocyte coupling adds no established cross-cycle prediction of adult FEV1/FVC

## Claim and scope
A single eosinophil-by-lymphocyte interaction did not establish improved prediction of prebronchodilator FEV1/FVC beyond flexible marginal blood counts and routine risk factors. In separately sampled NHANES 2007–2008 development (n=3,522) and 2011–2012 evaluation (n=3,516) samples, target weighted MSE reduction was **0.00000005994**, with conditional joint survey-PSU bootstrap 95% interval **[−0.00012169, 0.00011583]**. Source grouped cross-validation was slightly worse with the interaction. This is a negative result for one representation and estimator, not equivalence, exclusion of biological interaction, or evidence of clinical utility.

## Motivation and selection
The question was selected from primary literature and available public physiological measurements, not from SCI. Nerpin et al. associated blood inflammatory markers with lung function, including additive information from multiple markers. A later cohort study reported eosinophil associations with lung-function decline and contrasted other differential cell types. These motivate testing whether joint eosinophil–lymphocyte variation contains information missed by additive marginal models, not claiming that interactions or either cell type are newly discovered. Our single-visit ratio outcome differs from longitudinal decline in that literature. [Nerpin et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmed.2018.07.011), [cohort study 2024](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.01037-2023).

The selected question, endpoint, model and planned checks are in `files/question-registration.json`. One SCI duplication-check request retrieved the latest 1,000 catalog records and screened their serialized metadata for eosinophil AND lymphocyte AND lung/FEV/spirometry terms; no matches were found (`files/catalog-check.json`). This limited snapshot is not an exhaustive novelty search. The initial registration command failed because `python` was absent; the catalog response was downloaded after question selection in the work transcript but before the corrected durable `python3` registration. No analysis outcomes had been inspected. No SCI paper is used or tested here.

## Methods
Outcome y = SPXNFEV1/SPXNFVC in ratio units. Absolute eosinophil, lymphocyte and neutrophil counts are in 1,000 cells/µL; CBC counts are derived from total WBC and differential percentages and rounded, not independent cell assays. [CDC CBC documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/CBC_G.htm).

The comparator is a deliberately strong routine-measure prediction baseline, not an asserted standard clinical risk score. Cubic splines with three uniformly placed source-range knots and linear extrapolation represent age, height, BMI, hemoglobin, total WBC and the three absolute cell counts. Additional terms encode sex, race/ethnicity categories and smoking (never, former, current); all spline columns have female interactions. Smoking follows SMQ020/040, with refusals/unknowns excluded. There are 71 baseline columns; source constants are handled by scaling denominator 1.

The augmented model appends exactly one product, ((eosinophils−source weighted mean)/source weighted SD) × ((lymphocytes−source weighted mean)/source weighted SD). No outcome-derived feature or target-centered transformation is used. Both models fit weighted ridge regression with alpha=1, unpenalized intercept, source-weighted column centering/scaling and weights normalized to mean 1. No target tuning, selection, recalibration or clipping enters the primary result. No predictor contains spirometry outcomes. Transformations and code were frozen before the first target evaluation (local hash record retained).

Primary endpoint is survey-weighted target mean squared error of baseline minus augmented, positive favoring augmentation. MEC weights are used, not population causal adjustment. Eligible E contains 16 design strata/32 PSUs; G contains 14/31. For each of 400 paired bootstrap replicates, within each cycle and stratum, sample n_h−1 PSUs with replacement, assign multiplicity×n_h/(n_h−1), refit both source models and score with bootstrapped target weights. Source knots and product centering are fixed; ridge coefficients and weighted regression scaling are refitted. The interval is conditional on those design choices and observed complete cases, not a full uncertainty assessment of research selection, nonresponse or knot estimation. Target strata can have more than two PSUs; implementation uses the observed n_h.

Source descriptive validation uses five GroupKFold folds grouped by stratum–PSU and refits transformations within training folds. It is not a second dataset. Seed 481716 is fixed. Sensitivities are not used to select a winning model. Source-label permutation (100 shuffles) is a destructive pipeline control, not a valid survey-adjusted null test: arbitrary permutation destroys covariate and cluster dependence.

## Limitations and falsification
The large interval relative to the near-zero estimate prevents declaring equivalence or excluding useful benefits from other models. Modest source size, sparse extreme counts, rounded cell counts, spline extrapolation and ridge conditioning can obscure or induce small interactions. The 5-knot sensitivity improves slightly but has no separate uncertainty and cannot replace the locked primary analysis. Complete-case/quality restrictions and spirometry eligibility can induce selection; survey weights do not automatically correct this restriction. Smoking category does not capture pack-years; asthma, infections, steroid use, occupational exposure, medication, recent illness and social factors can confound associations. Race categories are descriptive survey covariates, not biological mechanisms. Cross-sectional prediction is not causal inference or future prognosis. Multiple previous projects on these cycles preclude treating this single feature test as an isolated confirmatory discovery. No external lab/population validation is complete.

A future independently recruited study could prespecify this exact comparator/interaction, measure repeat differential counts and high-quality pre/postbronchodilator spirometry, quantify smoking dose, infections and medications, and assess calibration and longitudinal change. That would distinguish transient inflammatory coupling from persistent physiology and test clinical usefulness. None of that study was performed here.
