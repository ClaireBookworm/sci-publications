# Perturbation-scale ratios do not demonstrate improved cross-cohort Parkinson voice discrimination

## Central claim
Adding unlogged RAP/local-jitter and APQ3/local-shimmer ratios to a fixed conventional acoustic classifier did **not demonstrate incremental discrimination** in an independently collected cohort. Oxford leave-one-subject-out AUC decreased from 0.7656 to 0.7500. Locked Istanbul AUC increased from 0.6200 to 0.6375 (difference 0.0175; paired participant bootstrap 95% interval −0.0575 to 0.1050), while probability errors worsened. This is an informative negative test of one representation and one model family, not evidence that voice lacks PD information or that the ratios are equivalent to zero benefit.

## Question and motivation
Local jitter/shimmer and windowed perturbation measures describe related aspects of phonatory instability. Little et al. [1] motivated testing voice measures while warning of confounding and measurement sensitivity. Sakar et al. [2] supplied a separately acquired speech cohort. We asked whether short-window/local ratios capture useful perturbation shape beyond magnitudes. Ratios are deterministic nonlinear functions of already available measurements: they add no new raw information. We do not claim that constituent measures, ratios in general, or this broad physiological idea are novel. The contribution is a frozen cross-cohort incremental-prediction test with subject-level uncertainty and transparent failure evidence. No literature-wide claim that current analyses never use ratios is made.

Only this one record per participant enters the primary test. The repository's separate PD-only test file is not used. Target labels were not used for fitting, scaling, tuning or model choice. This cohort was first acquired for this project, and the design was frozen before its effect estimates were computed. These are separately published/acquired cohorts, not splits of a common file. The original collection contexts differ; no participant overlap is reported in the documentation examined. Anonymized identifiers do not permit definitive person linkage. No exact shared nine-feature subject vector was found, which is only a duplicate-data check, not proof of unique people. Independent collection is not a guarantee of measurement compatibility.

## Methods
The outcome is supplied PD case/control status, not incident disease, disease severity or a clinical diagnosis generated by this study. The statistical unit is the subject identifier, never a phonation. Conventional baseline predictors are log local jitter, log local shimmer, log RAP, log PPQ, log APQ3, log APQ5, log APQ11, log mean fundamental frequency, and HNR (nine terms). Oxford `MDVP:PPQ`/`MDVP:APQ` map to the target's five-/eleven-point quotients; extraction implementations may differ. The augmented model adds RAP/jitter and APQ3/shimmer without logging them. Ratios are computed **after** subject-level averaging in Oxford. No fitted feature selection occurs.

Istanbul percentage perturbation exports are divided by 100 to match relative Oxford values; ratios are invariant to that conversion. A dimensional check gives median (absolute jitter × mean pitch)/(relative jitter) close to one; this supports jitter harmonization but does not validate all extraction equivalences. Target HTN is mapped to HNR. All input hashes, explicit column maps, source distributions and diagnostic checks are provided.

Both models use discovery-only StandardScaler, L2 logistic regression with C=1, balanced class weights, unpenalized intercept, and fixed solver tolerance. Discovery validation refits the entire pipeline leaving one subject out. The locked full-discovery fit predicts the separate cohort without target recalibration. Primary uncertainty is 2,000 paired stratified target-subject bootstrap draws with replacement, percentile intervals and fixed fitted models. Secondary uncertainty refits in 200 stratified discovery/target bootstrap draws. Seed 846103 and draw order are fixed in code. These intervals are approximate, small-sample, conditional on observed cohorts and do not encompass unknown participant or measurement errors. Positive success required both positive discovery gain and positive lower target confidence bound; neither held.

## Results
| Test | Baseline AUC | Augmented AUC | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Oxford subject-held-out discovery | 0.7656 | 0.7500 | −0.0156 |
| Istanbul locked primary | 0.6200 | 0.6375 | +0.0175 |
| C=0.1 sensitivity | 0.6125 | 0.6275 | +0.0150 |
| C=10 sensitivity | 0.6500 | 0.6700 | +0.0200 |
| Local-only conventional baseline | 0.6000 | 0.6250 | +0.0250 |
| Conventional terms plus their squares | 0.6175 | 0.6425 | +0.0250 |
| Median Oxford aggregation | 0.5950 | 0.6275 | +0.0325 |
| Three target vowels averaged | 0.5350 | 0.5875 | +0.0525 |

The training-and-target bootstrap interval for primary AUC difference was [−0.0552, 0.1005]. Deleting each target subject in turn gave gains from 0.0026 to 0.0289. A 1,000-permutation target-label challenge gave two-sided p=0.623 for the absolute paired AUC difference (a label-association challenge, **not** a conditional incremental-information test). Sensitivities are descriptive, not alternative routes to declaring success; their multiplicity is not corrected.

Target Brier score worsened from 0.3570 to 0.3849 and log loss from 1.4141 to 1.9468. All listed sensitivities also worsened these probability scores. Balanced case/control sampling, class weighting and cross-cohort feature shift mean these outputs are not population-risk estimates; no clinical risk calibration or utility is established. Full precision and coefficient vectors are in `files/outputs/main/results.json`.

## Falsification, self-check and rerun
Author checks independently reconstructed predictions with SciPy BFGS, checked hand pairwise AUC, subject mapping, constant within-subject labels, dimensions and target percentage conversion. Maximum differences from sklearn probabilities were 3.43×10⁻⁸ (baseline) and 1.06×10⁻⁷ (augmented). The complete central computation was rerun in a fresh read-only networkless container and an empty output directory; results JSON, subject tables and predictions were byte-identical. Logs include the unsuccessful initial count assertion. These are author self-checks, **not independent peer review**. Sensitivities, broad bootstrap uncertainty, failed discovery criterion and poorer probability scores challenge rather than support a positive claim.

## Limitations and proposed test
Small samples, only eight discovery controls, possible discrepancy between identifier and participant counts, case/control ascertainment, reused exploratory data, missing harmonized age/sex/medication covariates, unequal repetition counts and different acoustic pipelines limit inference. Fundamental frequency is an imperfect proxy for sex; confounding cannot be removed with the available shared columns. We establish neither mechanism nor age/sex-adjusted diagnostic specificity. Feature-distribution diagnostics document shift, not its cause. A negative incremental result is not an equivalence test and wide intervals leave clinically meaningful gains/losses possible. No confirmation on raw waveform re-extraction, prospective population, other diseases or calibrated clinical thresholds was performed.

A future clinical study could acquire protocol-matched vowels, harmonize feature extraction, match or adjust age/sex/medication, include non-PD dysphonia controls, repeat phonations and lock a model before prospective evaluation. Laryngeal or autonomic measurements could test whether these ratios track actual perturbation dynamics rather than processing artifacts. These are proposals; this study collected no new measurements or participants and performed no intervention or experiment.
