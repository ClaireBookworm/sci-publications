# A platelet count–volume interaction gave no clear incremental improvement in cross-cycle CRP prediction

## Abstract
A nonlinear inverse relation between platelet number and size is established, but the incremental predictive value of their joint configuration needs comparison with strong main-effect models. We fitted weighted logistic models in 4,383 NHANES 2005–2006 adults and froze predictions for 4,827 adults in the separately sampled 2017–2018 dataset. A centered log platelet-count × mean-platelet-volume interaction was added to spline main effects for demographics, adiposity and standard blood-count inflammatory indices. For CRP >3 mg/L, weighted Brier loss changed from 0.179364 to 0.179219, difference -0.00014554 (95% conditional PSU-bootstrap interval -0.00036979 to +0.00005556). Joint development/confirmation resampling gave -0.00080085 to +0.00084652. AUROC changed from 0.775614 to 0.775962. Sensitivities showed small point gains, not compelling evidence of transferable added information. This is an inconclusive incremental prediction test, not a biological null, causal claim or new clinical biomarker. Code, public inputs, hashes, clean reruns and author self-checks accompany the report.

## Question selected from literature and data
Bessman and colleagues demonstrated a nonlinear inverse relation between platelet count and mean platelet volume (MPV) and warned that plateletcrit is not constant [1,2]. A previous NHANES study reported an inverse MPV–CRP association [3]. These findings motivated our narrower question: does the **interaction** between log count and MPV predict inflammatory state beyond both measures' nonlinear main effects and commonly used blood-count indices? We do not claim that count, MPV, their joint distribution, or CRP association is newly discovered, or that no current analysis uses interactions. The contribution is the prespecified frozen incremental test and its negative/inconclusive result, not invention of a ratio. No SCI publication is used or tested.

The question and analysis needs were registered before numerical fitting (`question.json`, `data.json`). The registration timestamp was refreshed during input retrieval; the initial question was written before catalog access. One documented post-selection catalog duplication-check event used a platelet query; HTTP503 retries were followed by retrieval of the first 1,000 catalog records and local title/abstract screening. No exact study was found in that limited scope; concurrent unpublished work is not excluded (`duplication-check.json`, `catalog-response.json`). The catalog did not supply the question.

## Population, independent datasets and provenance
Development: NHANES 2005–2006, 10,348 demographic rows, 4,979 adults aged ≥20, 4,383 complete cases (596 excluded), 1,798 CRP-positive. Confirmation: the **separately sampled cross-sectional 2017–2018 dataset**, 9,254 demographic rows, 5,569 adults, 4,827 complete cases (742 excluded), 1,777 positive. Weighted positive fractions were 0.356716 and 0.353284. Both contain 15 masked strata and 30 PSUs. The target population is US civilian noninstitutionalized adults, but complete-case selection means our weighted results need not represent all adults [4]. Statistical unit: participant; cluster uncertainty uses survey PSUs, not blood cells.

Public-use DEMO, CBC, CRP/HSCRP, BMX and SMQ tables were linked by SEQN within cycles only. No shared SEQN was found between cycles. These are not train/test splits of one sample. The program is repeated cross-sectional rather than a planned participant follow-up; public identifiers cannot guarantee no incidental person overlap. We performed no identity linkage. The two samples share the NHANES program, so this does **not** establish transport across countries or independently operated measurement systems. D inputs had been used previously for a different RDW question; neither this interaction nor J CBC/CRP outcomes had been inspected for this question before freezing. Confirmation is feature/model-untouched, not a claim that the investigator has never used NHANES.

Inputs were acquired without special approval from CDC public-use releases and immutable local caches, then hash checked. `data.json` lists canonical URLs, exact hashes, sizes, access times and cache provenance. Original XPT inputs are under `files/data/`; CDC component documentation is under `files/evidence/`. The NCHS agreement permits statistical analysis/reporting and forbids reidentification or identifiable linkage [5]; we complied. Federal source data are not newly relicensed by us; source restrictions continue to apply. Article/code licensing is the SCI baseline in LICENSE.

## Prespecified model and leakage controls
Outcome is measured CRP >3 mg/L, not diagnosed inflammatory disease or future health. D's LBXCRP in mg/dL was multiplied by 10; J's LBXHSCRP is already mg/L [6,7]. Each eligible row required all variables, positive counts, positive MEC weight. Unknown smoking answers were missing, not negative: never-smoker SMQ020=2; former SMQ020=1 and SMQ040=3; current SMQ020=1 and SMQ040=1/2. NLR=absolute neutrophils/lymphocytes; PLR=platelet count/lymphocytes. No outcome was imputed.

The baseline uses separate quadratic B-spline effects (four quantile knots, no bias term, linear extrapolation) for age, BMI, log WBC, log NLR, log PLR, log platelet count and MPV, plus female, RIDRETH1 categories 2–5 (reference 1), and former/current smoking indicators (reference never). NLR and PLR are conventional research inflammatory indices, not substitutes for clinical CRP testing. Count and MPV main effects ensure the added pattern is not merely a standalone MPV effect. The augmentation is (log count − development mean) × (MPV − development mean). This adds one interaction basis; it supplies no information unavailable in the raw variables, only a new representation relative to this baseline. A sufficiently flexible joint model could learn it directly.

Knots, centers, StandardScaler and L2 logistic models (C=1, max_iter=1500, tolerance 1e-8) were fitted in development only. Knot placement/scaling are unweighted; logistic sample weights are MEC weights normalized to mean 1. Training saw only D files. Main and all prespecified sensitivity models were frozen together, with a hash recorded before J testing. We did not select a variant using confirmation performance, recalibrate predictions in J or tune a threshold. In-sample development results are explicitly labeled resubstitution, not validation. The confirmation stage reads D solely for already planned uncertainty refits and provenance; central J predictions always use the frozen model.

Primary endpoint: MEC-weighted Brier augmentation minus baseline (negative favors augmentation). AUROC is secondary. Intervals use paired Rao–Wu-style rescaled PSU resampling within each stratum: with two PSUs, choose one and double its weight, omitting the other. 2,000 target-only draws condition on development. 200 additional draws independently resample development and target PSUs, refitting preprocessing and models on positive-weight development observations. Percentile endpoints are approximate and the joint tail endpoints are Monte Carlo noisy. This handles clustered sampling more realistically than treating rows as independent, but is not a full nonresponse/survey-calibration uncertainty model. RNG seed 20261118; no multiplicity-adjusted subgroup inference.

## Results and challenges
| Frozen specification | Δ Brier | Δ AUROC |
|---|---:|---:|
| main | -0.00014554 | +0.000348 |
| C01 | -0.00013029 | +0.000207 |
| C10 | -0.00013706 | +0.000317 |
| linear | -0.00003605 | +0.000215 |
| no_smoke | -0.00013779 | +0.000273 |
| permuted | -0.00001689 | +0.000044 |

Primary interval: [-0.00036979, +0.00005556]; joint interval: [-0.00080085, +0.00084652]. The point change is only 0.081% of baseline loss. Its direction is consistent across regularization and comparator checks, but neither interval excludes no gain. A single permuted-interaction control also gives a tiny gain, illustrating why point improvements alone do not establish signal; this is not a permutation significance test. Removing smoking does not enlarge eligibility, because it retains the primary complete-case sample.

With the original frozen model, excluding target CRP >10 mg/L leaves 4,439 participants and Δ Brier −0.00015822; limiting platelet count to 150–450 leaves 4,577 and Δ −0.00007209. These are target-subset evaluations, **not** models refitted after excluding development outliers. Sex/age subgroup metrics are in results.json and remain descriptive. Baseline mean predicted probability is 0.369886 versus observed weighted 0.353284; the interaction barely changes that calibration-in-the-large mismatch (0.369736). Neither model's good AUROC proves accurate absolute risk or clinical utility.

Platelet count median shifted from 268 to 235 ×10³/µL; MPV from 8.1 to 8.2 fL. The CBC instrument changed between historical and later cycles, and CRP laboratory methods differ [6–9]. These shifts can be biological, technical or compositional; no causal attribution is possible. Fixed feature extraction avoids analysis leakage but cannot remove assay bias.

An initial syntax error was fixed before fitting; failed source/log are preserved. Primary predictions and the conditional interval did not change. ## Limits and future test
Failure to exclude zero is not evidence that platelet physiology contains no hidden information; the test concerns one interaction, one outcome, one age range and two cycles. CRP is nonspecific and measured once, medication/infection/malignancy and preanalytic handling are unadjusted, and complete cases may be selected. Age, sex, BMI, smoking and race adjustment mitigates some prediction confounding, not all; race here is a survey category, not a biological mechanism. This study cannot identify platelet activation or causal inflammation pathways. No prospective outcomes, clinical decision thresholds, calibration slope, net benefit or equivalence margin were tested. Shared measurement program and assay changes limit independent validation. We therefore do not recommend adding this interaction to care or replacing a CRP assay.

A future clinical study could prospectively lock this formula, standardize processing delays and instruments, measure repeated CRP/platelet values and medication/infection covariates, and evaluate calibration and decision benefit in another health system. A controlled physiological study could distinguish stable count–volume regulation from assay artifacts. These are proposals only; no recruitment, new measurement, intervention or physical experiment was performed.
