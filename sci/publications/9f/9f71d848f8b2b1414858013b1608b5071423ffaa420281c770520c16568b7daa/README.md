# ST-depression–chronotropic interactions did not establish incremental diagnostic value across Cleveland and Hungarian cohorts

## Abstract
We tested four interactions between exercise ST depression and age-normalized peak heart rate against conventional noninvasive predictors with flexible additive terms. Logistic models trained on303 Cleveland patients were frozen and evaluated on293 Hungarian patients from a separately named clinical cohort. Target Brier error decreased from0.130995 to0.126343, but the joint source-refit/target-bootstrap95% interval for the difference was[-0.034404,0.023021]. A simpler linear baseline performed better than either primary model, and complete-case performance reversed the apparent advantage. Incremental diagnostic improvement was not established. These summary-field interactions do not implement the established ST/HR index, and historical selected angiography cohorts cannot establish clinical utility.

## Scientific question and scope
Heart-rate adjustment is an established exercise-ECG idea, not our invention. Kligfield, Ameisen and Okin studied rate-adjusted ST depression in selected low-likelihood and angiographic disease groups [1]. We ask a narrower computational question: does a joint pattern in two routinely released summary fields improve transport of diagnostic probabilities beyond their separate flexible effects? This is motivated by physiology but is not evidence that existing clinical analyses generally ignore this information. We make no priority claim: heart-disease machine-learning models may already learn such interactions.

The processed UCI files lack resting heart rate and the exercise/recovery time series. Therefore `oldpeak/(change in HR)`, true ST/HR slopes and recovery loops cannot be reconstructed here. Our ratio is **peak heart rate / (220 − age)**, not heart-rate reserve, an individually validated maximum, or the clinical ST/HR index. We do not test or refute the original clinical studies. The independent-cohort analysis did not establish the registered improvement criterion. The contribution is a reproducible, scoped negative transport test rather than a new biomarker.

## Data, independence and rights
The UCI Heart Disease archive [2,3], DOI10.24432/C52P4X, provides separately named Cleveland and Hungarian databases. We use only `processed.cleveland.data` and `processed.hungarian.data`, not pooled random train/test splits. The archive describes an angiographic disease diagnosis; we binarize `num>0`. Its documentation associates the positive label with major-vessel narrowing >50%; original boundary wording and clinical verification details cannot be resolved from processed files. All rows, not healthy volunteers or a population sample, are historical clinical referrals. No new measurements, recruitment, interventions or physical experiments were performed.

Cleveland has303 rows,139 positive diagnoses. Hungarian has294 rows; one lacks required peak HR/ST fields and was excluded, leaving293 rows and106 positives. No restrictions on sex or age beyond available finite measurements were added. Observed Cleveland ages29–77 and Hungarian ages28–66. Rows are the statistical unit, presumed patient observations, with one exact duplicate14-field row in Hungarian. No exact13-predictor rows are shared across the two retained cohorts; no personal IDs are provided. Separate geographic cohorts support genuine site transfer, unlike two splits of one dataset, but cannot prove identity-level nonoverlap. Original enrollment dates, repeat visits and linkage are unavailable. A target de-duplication sensitivity retains292 rows, delta Brier-0.004669, paired target-only95% interval[-0.02104812015801347, 0.011931375251734346]. Duplicate rows are not automatically proven repeat people; the primary row bootstrap does not account for unrecognized repeated participants.

## Registration and model specification
`files/evidence/registration.json` records the choice before any project outcome fit, its literature origin, cohorts, endpoint, primary comparison, uncertainty and resource budget. A single post-choice catalog check searched1000 returned records for Cleveland, Hungarian, ST-depression and chronotropic terms. Its one incidental Cleveland-site match concerned SEEG and was not the same study; none matched this question. This bounded search is not exhaustive or a guarantee against concurrent/unindexed work. It did not select the question; no SCI publications were used as scientific inputs and none are gratuitously cited.

Cleveland is the sole model-development cohort. Hungary is the frozen external evaluation cohort, with no target-driven preprocessing, penalty selection or threshold fitting. This familiar public benchmark is not a pristine prospective confirmation dataset; historical exposure by AI training or other investigators cannot be ruled out. We distinguish external evaluation from strong untouched confirmation. After primary evaluation, missingness and influence challenges are explicitly exploratory. We report all planned penalties rather than selecting the best target score.

Both models use penalized logistic regression (scikit-learn1.8.0, `lbfgs`, max_iter2000, C=1; unpenalized intercept). Continuous age, admission resting blood pressure and cholesterol are source-median imputed and source-standardized; explicit missingness indicators are included. Categorical sex, chest-pain class, fasting-glucose flag, resting ECG class, exercise angina and peak ST slope use fixed dictionary indicators (first category reference) plus a missing indicator. Unknown categorical values are not anticipated in this archive. Slope here means the categorical peak ST morphology, **not** the ST/HR slope. Fluoroscopic vessel count `ca` and thallium-test `thal` are excluded to keep the comparator noninvasive and avoid diagnostic-workup leakage; endpoint num is never a predictor. Medication and protocol fields are absent from these processed inputs and cannot be controlled.

The flexible additive baseline adds separate cubic B-splines (`SplineTransformer`,3 source-fitted uniform knots, degree3, include_bias=False, default constant extrapolation) for oldpeak and peakHR/(220−age); spline outputs are source-standardized. Primary augmentation adds four products: form source z-scores of each variable, create powers1 and2, center and scale each power by its source standard deviation (floor0.1), multiply each oldpeak power by each ratio power, then source-standardize the four products before logistic fitting. These are only four prespecified interactions, not a search over features. No target normalization is used; the complete pipeline is refit on each source resample or CV training fold. Because age is already included, this tests a joint pattern, not a causal adjustment or isolated chronotropic physiology.

The simpler conventional sensitivity replaces the two spline bases with their linear standardized components and retains all other covariates. Added interactions are evaluated against that comparator as well. Additional registered checks use C0.1/10, source5-fold stratified CV (seed41125), sex strata, complete predictors, calibration, label shuffles and row overlap. Diagnostic calibration fits on target labels are reported only and do not change predictions. Missing-category and continuous missing-indicator coefficients are effectively zero if not seen during source training; this limits transport to missing target predictors.

## Statistical analysis
Primary loss is mean Brier error, equal weighting per row; augmented minus baseline below zero favors augmentation. AUROC and log loss are secondary, with no optimized diagnostic threshold. With RNG seed41125,1000 paired target row bootstraps quantify uncertainty conditional on the fitted models.300 paired source-refit plus target row bootstraps quantify training-sample and target-sample uncertainty. All transforms and models are refit for the latter. Intervals are percentile2.5/97.5%, not multiplicity-adjusted, with finite bootstrap Monte Carlo error. The prespecified success rule is the joint Brier interval entirely below zero. We did not stop on statistical significance, select among target penalties or claim equivalence from failure to reject. Small selected samples and unknown clustering limit precision.

## Limitations, interpretation and future test
This test does not establish hidden clinical information beyond standard use, a universal absence of interaction, a new mechanism, or clinical utility. Historical referral/angiography selection, small sample size, case prevalence and age shifts, severe differential missingness, unmeasured medication effects (including beta blockade), stress protocols, and unknown repeated visits limit transport.220−age is a crude proxy; no current threshold or diagnostic-care recommendation follows. There are no confidence claims about individual patient diagnosis. Full published clinical ST/HR measures cannot be compared directly because these processed summaries omit required information. No modern cohort, ECG waveform or prospective clinical validation was analyzed.

A future separately approved clinical validation could prespecify the frozen model and conventional linear/additive comparators, collect or use independently recorded full exercise/rest/recovery ECG with protocol, medication and angiography verification, directly compare ST/HR index/slope and test calibration, discrimination and decision benefit in a representative diagnostic pathway. That would test whether the summary-field interaction captures physiology or documentation artifacts. We have not performed or initiated such a study.
