# Three-trial handgrip sequence shape and stair difficulty: an exploratory cross-cohort prediction test

## Abstract
We tested whether curvature and interhand change disagreement in six existing grip trials add to conventional strength and demographics for concurrent stair difficulty. NHANES2011–2012 provided 1160 adults aged60+ for training; independently recruited2013–2014 provided 1235 for frozen evaluation. Adding grip-sequence curvature and interhand slope disagreement changed cross-cohort Brier score from 0.116410 to 0.116226 (difference -0.000184; joint PSU-bootstrap95% interval -0.001592 to +0.003815). The registered improvement criterion was not met; this does not establish equivalence or absence of physiological information. Both cohorts were previously inspected for other questions, not untouched confirmation. Independent-study-program validation remains incomplete.

## Question, provenance and motivation
Peak strength discards trial order. This analysis asks whether a middle-trial dip/rebound or disagreement of change between hands carries additional information about self-reported lower-limb function. Kapan et al. used ten repeated contractions to study fatigue/recovery and falls ([primary study](https://doi.org/10.1177/02692155251355881)); Bautmans et al. studied handgrip fatigue resistance and physical functioning ([primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17331228/)). Those protocols do not validate three alternating NHANES trials as a fatigue test. We therefore call these **sequence-shape descriptors**, not physiological fatigability or biomarkers. We collected no data and performed no intervention.

The question and checks were registered in files/evidence/registration.json before the single bounded SCI duplication query. See duplication.json and duplication-interpretation.txt; no exact duplicate was found among returned matches, but the query is not exhaustive. The question was not selected from SCI papers. Generic local modeling code from earlier work was adapted; no published SCI result is used or tested here.

## Models and locked comparison
For hand h and trial t, let x[h,t] denote kg and S=sum_h max_t x[h,t]. Combined mean M=2*mean(all six trials); asymmetry=abs(max1-max2)/S; decline=sum_h(x[h,1]-x[h,3])/S. Baseline continuous predictors are age, BMI, height, S, M, asymmetry and decline. Each has a cubic spline with four source quantile knots, five columns, no basis bias, constant extrapolation; all columns also interact with female indicator. Sex and four RIDRETH1 indicators (codes2–5, reference1) enter additively. The baseline is intentionally stronger than peak strength alone and includes mean and decline, but is a research comparator, not an established stair-difficulty clinical score.

Added linear terms are K=sum_h(x[h,1]-2*x[h,2]+x[h,3])/S and D=abs((x[1,1]-x[1,3])-(x[2,1]-x[2,3]))/S. These ratios are dimensionless. Both models use source-only StandardScaler and L2 logistic regression C=1, tolerance1e-9, max5000 iterations. Examination weights normalize to mean1. Nothing is fitted on target outcomes; target preprocessing uses source transforms. No target-driven feature or hyperparameter choice was made for the primary analysis. Bootstrap source fits remove zero-weight rows before knot/scaler fitting; bootstrap penalty normalization uses the original source sample mean of replicate weights.

Primary estimand is target examination-weighted Brier difference (added minus base; negative better). AUC and logloss are secondary. The registered criterion is a negative upper endpoint of the joint95% interval; it is not a clinically meaningful improvement threshold. Source five-fold stratified OOF estimates are exploratory, not a second dataset. Stratified rescaled PSU bootstrap draws n_h-1 PSUs with replacement per stratum and multiplier n_h/(n_h-1), retaining singleton strata;1000 target-only draws and200 independent source-refit/target draws produce percentile intervals. These finite-replicate intervals do not include data-selection or model-selection uncertainty, imputation uncertainty or between-program heterogeneity.

## Limitations and future test
Concurrent self-report cannot establish direction, mechanism, causality, diagnosis or future disability. Body size/age/sex are controlled flexibly; hand pain and effort are checked by restriction but residual socioeconomic, neurological, musculoskeletal and reporting confounding remain. Selection on six successful trials and direct stair responses can bias associations and reduces applicability to frailer people. Three trials separated by rest may reflect familiarization, motivation, pain or measurement noise rather than endurance. The result concerns these two descriptors and models, not all information in the recording. Large baseline spline designs may be unstable in small subgroup samples. Cohort reuse and multiple historical projects increase selection risk; no broad discovery claim is made.

A future independent cohort could preregister these exact formulas and frozen comparison, collect standardized handgrip repeats and observed stair performance, measure effort/pain and test repeat-day reliability and longitudinal outcomes. That clinical study has **not** been performed here.
