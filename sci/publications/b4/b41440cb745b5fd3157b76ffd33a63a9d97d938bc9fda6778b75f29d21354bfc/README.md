# Platelet size–count coupling fails to improve cross-cycle prediction of combined grip strength

## Abstract
A registered interaction of log platelet count and log mean platelet volume was added to flexible conventional predictors including CBC indices and the systemic immune-inflammation index. Training used 4129 NHANES2011–12 adults; frozen evaluation used 4590 separately recruited NHANES2013–14 adults. Weighted MSE increased from 138.791709 to 138.793278 kg². The joint source–target survey-PSU bootstrap95% interval for baseline-minus-augmented MSE was [-0.564704,0.093126]. This representation provides no demonstrated incremental prediction. Independent-survey validation was not completed; temporal transfer within one survey cannot establish clinical utility or mechanism.

## Question, rationale and scope
Platelet volume and number have an established nonlinear inverse relationship in normal subjects [1]. A large NHANES analysis already studies MPV in health and disease [2]. An existing NHANES study relates systemic immune-inflammation index (SII: platelet count × neutrophils/lymphocytes) to relative grip strength [3]. These motivate a narrower test: does the **nonadditive size–count pattern** contain useful information about concurrent muscle strength beyond their individual marginal values and established inflammatory indices? Coordinated large and numerous platelets could reflect a systemic state distinct from isolated changes in either. This is a hypothesis, not evidence for platelet activation or a causal pathway. Neither MPV, platelet count nor their joint interpretation is newly discovered or generally unused. We test a representation omitted from the specified additive baseline, not universal literature novelty. The product of standardized logs is an interaction, **not plateletcrit**; log plateletcrit is already additive in the two logarithms.

## Frozen methods and predictions
Before outcome analysis, fixed ridge regression alpha10 and one added interaction. Baseline: additive cubic B-splines (three unweighted source quantile knots, degree3, no bias basis, linear extrapolation) for age, BMI, height, hemoglobin, RDW, log WBC, log neutrophils, log lymphocytes, log platelet count, log MPV, log SII. Categorical sex, race/ethnicity (RIDRETH1), smoking (never/former/current); additional age×female and height×female linear terms address major known strength differences. The augmented model adds the product of source-standardized log platelet count and log MPV. All log inputs must be positive. SII uses absolute counts. These are relevant conventional covariates and established blood measures, not a comprehensive clinical gold standard. No hand measurements or outcome-derived predictors enter the baseline.

Preprocessing is source-fit only: knots, category encoding and column scaling; no target labels or target moments are used in training. Interaction centers/scales use source empirical moments, reweighted by bootstrap multiplicities during refits; design scaling also uses source multiplicities. Fits use normalized MEC weights times resampling multiplicities. Original model hyperparameters were not tuned on either cohort. GroupKFold5 groups source participants by survey stratum–PSU; its result is exploratory source performance, not independent validation. Source folds do not use target data. Raw numeric XPT near-zero sentinel magnitudes are normalized to0 before clinical-value filters. One-to-one merge assertions prevent row multiplication.

Primary prediction: weighted target MSE gain (baseline−augmented) positive with joint source–target95% interval entirely above0; descriptive1% improvement benchmark. Secondary MAE. Survey uncertainty: independently resample PSUs within source and target strata, drawing m−1 PSUs from m with replacement, applying multiplicity scale m/(m−1). 300 full refits re-estimate the entire source pipeline; 2000 target-only paired replicates condition on fitted models. There are no singleton retained-domain strata. Seed181234. Percentile intervals approximate design uncertainty, have Monte Carlo error, and do not adjust for program-wide selection.

## Limits and future test
This negative result does not show platelet biology is irrelevant to muscle strength, disprove all nonlinear features, or establish equivalence within a clinical margin. Complete-case selection, grip completion requirements, age restriction, assay/preanalytical MPV variability, sampling design approximation and covariate/model choice limit generalization. Medications, activity, hand disorders, nutrition, chronic inflammatory disease and smoking dose remain residual confounders. Weighting and prediction do not establish causation; no platelet activation assay, longitudinal decline or clinical intervention was studied. The conditional versus joint intervals differ because re-estimating a fitted interaction can be unstable. Broader representation search could require new untouched data. No reliable previously unused health information was discovered by this specific test.

A future independently recruited cohort could preregister this frozen pipeline, standardize blood draw-to-analysis timing and dynamometer protocol, record medications, activity and hand morbidity, and test paired errors against the same baseline with prespecified precision and an external population. Repeated assays and longitudinal strength could distinguish transient blood variation from stable physiology. Such a clinical or experimental study would require separate approvals; none was performed or initiated here.
