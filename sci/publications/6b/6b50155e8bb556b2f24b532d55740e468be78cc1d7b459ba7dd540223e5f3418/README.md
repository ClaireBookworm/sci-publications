# Red-cell size dispersion × hemoglobin concentration adds no clear transferred information about elevated CRP

## Abstract and scoped claim
In NHANES 2003–2004 to 2007–2008 temporal-sample transfer, adding a centered RDW × MCHC interaction to flexible CBC main effects changed weighted Brier loss by an improvement of 0.00001320 (95% joint PSU-bootstrap interval -0.00107146 to 0.00147047), providing no clear evidence of incremental prediction of CRP >3 mg/L. Source fitting used 4,366 adults and target evaluation used 5,225 adults in a separate cross-sectional probability sample. Target AUC was 0.773227 without versus 0.773274 with the interaction. This negative result concerns one joint representation and model family, not absence of physiological information in red cells, equivalence of models, or clinical utility. The uncertainty interval permits both harm and modest improvement.

## Scientific motivation and question selection
A primary [NHANES study of obesity, anemia and iron deficiency](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10965637/) reported higher elevated-CRP incidence and reduced MCV with higher body-fat percentage; associations differed by sex. This supports considering erythrocyte physiology jointly with inflammation and controlling adiposity rather than treating CBC measurements as isolated markers. A [primary analysis of CBC inflammatory markers and obesity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7785136/) also considered erythrocyte and leukocyte measurements. The latter was available via search metadata; browser access was challenged and two full-text XML attempts returned HTTP500, so no detailed findings from that paper are relied upon.

We hypothesized that the association between red-cell size heterogeneity (RDW) and elevated CRP depends on mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (MCHC). A source-centered product is a low-dimensional candidate representation of coexisting anisocytosis and altered cellular hemoglobin concentration, possibly reflecting iron handling or inflammatory erythropoiesis. Neither mechanism nor iron deficiency is identified here. RDW, MCHC and their physiological associations are established; the contribution is this explicit incremental prediction and temporal transfer test, not a claim that these markers or interactions have never been used. This literature/data-first question was recorded in files/protocol.json before the analysis. A single documented SCI duplication check searched 2,000 newest catalog records for MCHC, hypochromia, hemoglobin-concentration and RDW-interaction terms, finding no matches. It cannot exclude older, differently worded, or concurrent work. No SCI scientific result was used or tested, so no SCI citation is added merely for participation.

## Prediction methods and leakage controls
Outcome: LBXCRP >0.3 mg/dL, exactly >3 mg/L, not a diagnosis or future event. Baseline: ridge logistic regression, C=1, intercept unpenalized, max_iter=1500, tolerance 1e-7. Continuous age, BMI, log WBC, log neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio, RDW, MCHC, Hb, MCV and log platelet count each receive additive cubic spline effects with four uniformly spaced knots, no spline bias, linear boundary extrapolation. Sex, race/ethnicity (RIDRETH1 categories 2–5 relative to 1), and smoking (former/current relative to never) are indicator covariates. Both models already contain flexible RDW and MCHC main effects. The augmented model adds only (RDW − source mean RDW) × (MCHC − source mean MCHC). MCHC is LBXMC (g/dL), not MCV, which is LBXMCVSI (fL).

Spline knots, interaction means, unweighted standardization means/scales and fitted coefficients are source-only; sample weights in model fitting are MEC weights divided by their source mean. Primary target metrics use MEC weights. Models are serialized before target data are loaded; all target predictions precede target scoring. No target recalibration, threshold selection or hyperparameter search is performed. The baselines are relevant routine CBC measures represented flexibly, not a claim to match every published clinical risk score. Since CRP itself is measurable, the intended test is latent CBC information rather than replacing the laboratory assay.

Primary metric: weighted baseline Brier loss minus augmented Brier loss (positive means improvement). AUC improvement is secondary. Uncertainty: 400 independent joint source/target stratified Rao–Wu rescaled PSU bootstrap refits, random seed 16237, drawing m−1 PSUs with replacement per stratum and assigning m/(m−1) times the draw multiplicity. Every retained stratum in both surveys has at least two PSUs. Source model coefficients refit with bootstrap weights, while knot/centering/scaling choices condition on the original source covariates; target scores use target bootstrap weights. Percentile 95% intervals are descriptive, conditional on the fixed model specification and preprocessing, and do not account for prior studies on this cache. Zero-weight observations retain their array rows; no participant-level bootstrap is substituted for clustering.

Six prespecified challenges retained source-trained predictions: excluding target CRP >10 mg/L, scoring men/women separately, unweighted scoring, excluding low-Hb observations (<13 g/dL men/<12 women), and adding a flexible hematocrit main effect to both models. The low-Hb restriction is operational, not a pregnancy-adjusted clinical anemia diagnosis. These sensitivity scores have no separate uncertainty intervals and are exploratory, not six additional confirmations. None shows a compelling incremental AUC or Brier effect. In 200 source-label permutations, apparent source incremental Brier gains had a 95% null interval [-0.00000180, 0.00030172]. This is an overfitting diagnostic, **not** a valid conditional test of the interaction or external significance: global permutation destroys main-effect associations and design structure. Original source in-sample scores are included for audit only, not validation.

## Limitations and proposed next test
This negative result does not establish no information, equivalence, causality, or utility at a clinical decision threshold. MCHC, Hb and hematocrit are algebraically related; including hematocrit can induce collinearity, partly controlled by ridge, not remove all redundancy. Iron status, menstruation/pregnancy, disease, medication, diet, socioeconomic position and other confounding remain incompletely measured. CRP is nonspecific and contemporaneous; future inflammation, treatment response, disease diagnosis and iron status were not tested. Residual model misspecification, laboratory rounding/shift, complete-case selection, finite masked PSU counts and repeated target reuse limit generalization. No net-benefit analysis or clinical cost claim is made.

A future prespecified observational clinical study could test the frozen representation against flexible CBC baselines in an independent laboratory/population, measure ferritin, transferrin saturation, medication and menstrual status, and assess repeated CRP. It could distinguish iron restriction from inflammatory signaling and assess transport/calibration. Such a study has not been performed or authorized here.
