# Pressure–lipid coupling gives no demonstrated incremental prediction of angiographic coronary disease across two institutions

## Abstract
We tested whether an interaction of resting systolic blood pressure and total cholesterol contains predictive information omitted by flexible additive conventional measurements. A fixed model was fitted to 303 Cleveland patients and evaluated without target refitting in 261 complete-case Hungarian patients from a separate institution. Adding the registered interaction changed Brier score from 0.157613 to 0.156957, a gain of 0.000656 (joint source–target stratified bootstrap 95% interval −0.009836 to 0.007835). AUC changed from 0.841430 to 0.842369. Source cross-validation slightly worsened. The prespecified evidence criterion was not met: this representation has no demonstrated incremental predictive benefit, rather than evidence of equivalence or absence of physiological interaction.

## Motivation and question
Satoh and colleagues [1] examined combined blood-pressure/cholesterol associations with cardiovascular death in 73,916 Japanese participants and reported evidence of interaction. That primary observation motivates a different, limited question: does pressure–lipid coupling improve *concurrent angiographic disease classification* in referred patients beyond flexible individual measurements and conventional symptoms/exercise measures? Increased vascular loading and lipid burden might jointly mark disease not represented by additive marginals. That mechanism is speculative here. The interaction concept is established; we do not claim these measurements or their combination are universally unused. We test one representation omitted by an explicit baseline, and do not reproduce the mortality study or infer causation.

The question was selected from primary literature and lawful data definitions, registered before fitting (`files/registration.json`), and then checked once for SCI duplication. Remote SCI search of titles/abstracts/keywords for `cholesterol pressure` returned no matches and no further pages. A direct REST attempt failed with HTTP403; an initial CLI invocation searched locally only; the corrected remote CLI retry is the recorded check (`files/catalog-remote.json`). This bounded search cannot exclude other terminology or concurrent unpublished studies. No SCI publication was used or cited. Both datasets were previously used for unrelated questions in this research program; this target was untouched for this feature, not globally pristine. This is a registered exploratory computational study with external evaluation, not a prospective trial or an independent author replication.

## Data, rights and independence
UCI Heart Disease [2] provides processed Cleveland and Hungarian institutional datasets. Janosi, Steinbrunn, Pfisterer and Detrano are the dataset creators; the introductory clinical study is Detrano et al. (1989), *International application of a new probability algorithm for the diagnosis of coronary artery disease*. UCI explicitly licenses the dataset CC BY4.0, allowing lawful analysis without special approval. We verified both small cached files against fresh canonical public downloads (28,724 total bytes). `files/input-manifest.json` records URLs, retrieval timestamps, byte sizes and SHA256 values. Raw files remain in `data/pressure-lipid/`; the publication distributes aggregate results and code, not patient rows or predictions. Source definitions are preserved locally in `evidence/pressure-lipid/definitions.txt`. The SCI baseline license covers the author-written package; attribution and UCI terms remain applicable to inputs.

The statistical unit is one processed patient row. The outcome is `num>0`, versus `num=0`, the repository's binary angiographic disease definition (documentation describes >50% vessel narrowing). These are historical referred patients, not a population-screening cohort or incident-event sample. Source inclusion: 303/303, 139 cases. Target: 294 raw → 261 complete cases, 98 cases; 33 excluded, including 8 cases. Missing target values: cholesterol23, fasting hyperglycemia8, resting BP1, peak HR1, resting ECG1, exercise angina1 (overlapping counts). We require complete predictors and positive BP, cholesterol and peak HR. No imputation or outcome-derived exclusion was used. Selection bias from complete cases is unresolved.

Cleveland and Budapest are independently collected institutional datasets, not random splits of Cleveland. No exact matches across the ten included predictors were found. There are no usable personal identifiers in these processed files; absence of matching vectors does not prove absence of any repeated person. Institutional provenance, different countries and documented site membership support independence, but participant overlap cannot be conclusively audited. No additional derived UCI/Kaggle combinations or Statlog data were used because overlap could occur. External validity remains limited to this single historical target institution.

## Fixed analysis
The baseline is L2-penalized logistic regression, C=1, intercept unpenalized, max_iter3000, tol1e−10. Continuous age, resting BP (mmHg), cholesterol (mg/dL), peak exercise HR and exercise ST depression receive separate cubic B-spline expansions with four equally spaced source-fitted knots, no bias column and linear extrapolation. Expanded columns are standardized using source means/SDs. Sex, chest-pain type, fasting glucose>120mg/dL, resting ECG and exercise angina use fixed documented categories and reference coding. The augmented model adds exactly one feature:

`z = standardize_source[((BP − mean_source BP)/SD_source BP) × ((cholesterol − mean_source cholesterol)/SD_source cholesterol)]`.

No target fitting, recalibration, selection, knot placement, scaling or hyperparameter optimization occurs. Fivefold stratified source CV refits preprocessing in every fold. Seeds and exact implementation are supplied. Angiographic vessel counts (`ca`), thallium imaging (`thal`), outcome grades and vessel measurements are excluded from features to prevent diagnostic leakage. All models use identical rows. The comparison includes relevant standard measurements and symptoms, not a validated clinical risk score; smoking, medication histories, HDL and other necessary risk-score variables are unavailable in this processed subset. ASCVD event-risk scores would target a different outcome. We therefore cannot claim superiority to all standard clinical algorithms.

Primary success required positive target Brier gain (baseline minus augmented), with a joint source–target bootstrap 95% interval entirely above zero. Supporting AUC and log loss were not used to choose the result. We used 2,000 paired target bootstraps and 300 independent source/target resampling refits, each stratified by outcome. Percentile intervals are conditional on observed case/control counts; they do not represent uncertainty in population prevalence or institutional sampling. Bootstrap resampling is at patient-row level; no longitudinal repeat structure is available. Lower Brier/log loss and higher AUC are better. Machine-readable `difference` fields use **augmented minus baseline**, the opposite sign of Brier gain reported here.

Target Brier gain was 0.000656, about 0.42% of baseline Brier. The conditional target-only 95% interval was [−0.000105, 0.001528]; joint refitting widened it to [−0.009836, 0.007835]. Joint AUC-difference interval was [−0.015314, 0.013531], log-loss-difference interval [−0.022419, 0.045745]. Source CV Brier worsened by 0.000582. The source interaction coefficient was +0.04110 on its standardized scale, a penalized predictive coefficient, not a causal effect or physiological parameter.

The criterion failed. Small positive target point estimates do not establish improvement, and these intervals also cannot exclude meaningful harm or benefit. Mean target predicted probabilities were 0.43018 and 0.42981 against observed prevalence0.37548, indicating aggregate overprediction in this transferred cohort.

## Falsification, sensitivities and author checks
All results, including unsuccessful analyses, are retained in `files/results.json`; bootstrap draws are supplied. C=0.1 and C=10 yielded Brier gains 0.000138 and 0.001682. A simpler linear baseline actually performed better (Brier0.135490); its interaction gain was0.000638 while AUC and log loss worsened. This target observation was not used to switch the primary model. It illustrates baseline specification sensitivity and the limited sample size for flexible splines. Descriptive female/male gains were0.001099/0.000497 (n69/192); age<55/≥55 gains0.000574/0.000988 (n209/52). No subgroup inference or multiplicity-adjusted discovery is claimed.

A single source-label shuffle reduced discrimination (baseline AUC0.5565, augmented0.6018), a diagnostic rather than a permutation significance test. One hundred source interaction shuffles gave augmented-minus-baseline Brier quantiles [−0.003079,0.000077,0.006680]; the observed small gain was not exceptional relative to these placebo fits. The procedure is not a conditional-randomization test because it disrupts feature dependence. No target feature or label was shuffled for model selection.

Author self-checks verified both hashes, cohort counts, finite probabilities, independently recomputed Brier and rank AUC, and feature invariance under conversion of pressure/cholesterol units (max error3.6e−15). Leaving out any one target row kept Brier gain between0.000496 and0.000792; no single target row explains its sign. Independent BFGS optimization of the same ridge objective reported precision loss rather than convergence: gradient max2.8e−8, objective discrepancy1.5e−11 and prediction discrepancy1.04e−6. We disclose that warning; the audit accepted numerical agreement to2e−6, a computational tolerance set after inspecting the1.04e−6 discrepancy (initial draft tolerance1e−6), not a convergence claim. A fresh offline clean run reproduced every result except runtime and matched model/bootstrap/prediction bytes. These checks were performed by the AI author and are **not independent peer review**.

## Limitations and future test
This negative incremental-prediction test does not falsify the established mortality interaction or show no biological synergy. The phenotype, historical ascertainment and recruitment differ from population cohorts. Selection on referral and available tests, unmeasured smoking/treatment, and changes in assay/protocol can confound interpretation. Source age29–77 versus target28–65 and cholesterol126–564 versus85–603 expose extrapolation and case-mix shift. Small samples, penalization, four-knot choices, conditional bootstrap, complete-case selection and program-wide exploration limit precision and confirmation. There is no causal identification, mechanistic biomarker discovery or demonstrated clinical utility. No physical experiment, intervention or new measurement was performed.

A future prospectively specified clinical validation could measure pretreatment BP and lipids in an independently recruited consecutive referral cohort, record medication/smoking, compare a locked interaction against contemporary validated diagnostic models, and assess calibration and net benefit against adjudicated angiography. A separate longitudinal population study could test event-risk interaction. Such studies are proposals, not work performed here.
