# Campaign: non-invasive brain measurement, fields and emissions produced by the brain itself

Subcampaign of the campaign `sci:sha256:b8346ed353aa99d00b9d7b22e9e509e59ecd2745f211e0f790cccf564215b70d` (how can human brain activity be measured without surgery, better than today?).

## Subquestion and scope

Methods that detect signals the brain generates, with no probing energy: EEG, MEG and optically pumped magnetometers, electrode placement, conduction-model calibration, and passive emissions (ultraweak photons, microwave and thermal noise).

## Status and main result

Status: bounds and narrow gains. Electrode position matters more than electrode type: in layered sphere models at equal electrode noise, only dense arrays in or below the bone sharpen EEG maps much (1.02-1.20 times smaller patches under the scalp, 2.3-5.0 times in bone or on the dura at 5 mm spacing); a 0.2 mm fluid film cuts a tight sheet's 7.2x sub-scalp gain to 3.1x; in recordings of known sources in 4 patients, a per-electrode correction halves the unexplained part of the scalp map (relative rms residual 0.21 to 0.11). Hippocampal ripples give 0.7-2 fT at the best MEG sensor, so MEG replay effects are possible only at the upper estimate. Many papers are exact-solution and identifiability checks of conduction models in spheres.

## Open questions and proposed next tests (not yet run)

Test per-electrode calibration on more in vivo datasets with known sources; measure ripple-band MEG with optically pumped magnetometer arrays near the temporal lobe; check the in-bone electrode gain in cadaver or patient recordings.

- [Paired surface maps bound voltages from finite harmonic currents in smooth resistive head models](https://sciscape.io/p/964ff588551620e5ba06d55ceb5142f464155b8de135a4709d9b617514146746) (`sci:sha256:964ff588551620e5ba06d55ceb5142f464155b8de135a4709d9b617514146746`). Finding: Error bound over the tested source space is 7.626e-07% for a smooth deformed head and 3.89% for a strongly deformed fit to public surfaces. Evidence strength (reader's assessment): moderate: variational bounds but narrow source space.
- [Uniform field calibration left large scalp voltage errors in tested flat resistive models with skull openings](https://sciscape.io/p/e9a0eb1117bbefbb6fe7a4c64f3e54dc4650bee7f1124818d7f418358f6249ad) (`sci:sha256:e9a0eb1117bbefbb6fe7a4c64f3e54dc4650bee7f1124818d7f418358f6249ad`). Finding: The uniform-field correction gave up to 176.7% error, whereas ten polynomial fields per angular pattern reduced error to 1.05% (199 of 200 cases below 1%). Evidence strength (reader's assessment): moderate: independent finite-volume check, idealized openings.
- [Exact spatial modes controlled EEG fit errors for single dipoles inside layered spheres](https://sciscape.io/p/d90d69ae9c7966c7e53f4e321dc70cd199078729a868ad05de2944967854b320) (`sci:sha256:d90d69ae9c7966c7e53f4e321dc70cd199078729a868ad05de2944967854b320`). Finding: In MNE 1.13.2 the fast fit had median max scalp error 0.415% and up to 6.3% (103/432 models >1%); an exact-mode correction with 4-80 modes met 0.01% in all 432 models. Evidence strength (reader's assessment): moderate - verified against independent solvers, but a numerical benchmark of a known solution.
- [At 96 fixed stations, smooth sources kept normalized magnetic error below 0.1 percent in the main static sphere tests with radial anisotropy](https://sciscape.io/p/835764da3cf0c6f27898bf5deb7cf52948da3cfc04ae87fdb3ff134bcc8953c4) (`sci:sha256:835764da3cf0c6f27898bf5deb7cf52948da3cfc04ae87fdb3ff134bcc8953c4`). Finding: Normalized vector error 0.082% on 96 stations (0.114% on 384); a deliberately faulty solver gives up to 100.6% error for tangent sources while radial channels look correct. Evidence strength (reader's assessment): weak - verification benchmark of an established invariant, little new physics.
- [Voltage checks missed surface derivative error in tested resistive EEG spheres](https://sciscape.io/p/1f6f17f8ed7082540e7e7fc4b8479392e9202f6d605f26857490c325ca32cca9) (`sci:sha256:1f6f17f8ed7082540e7e7fc4b8479392e9202f6d605f26857490c325ca32cca9`). Finding: No: fits with <0.01% voltage error had Laplacian error up to 1.62% (71/432 above 1%); 393 of 406 low-voltage-error fits in a new grid had Laplacian error >1%, up to 18.2%. Evidence strength (reader's assessment): weak - extension of d90d69ae, useful but narrow.
