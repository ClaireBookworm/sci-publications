# Signed grip-trial drift does not improve cross-cycle HbA1c prediction beyond strength summaries

## Summary and scoped claim
Among complete-case adults in NHANES 2011–2012 (source, n=4,400) and separately sampled 2013–2014 (confirmation, n=4,890), a signed bilateral trial-order grip feature did not convincingly improve prediction of concurrent glycated hemoglobin beyond conventional strength, anthropometry, demographics and order-invariant grip summaries. Target weighted mean squared error (MSE, HbA1c percentage-points squared) increased from **0.74402652 to 0.74414969**: change **+0.00012317 (+0.0166%)**. A 2,000-replicate conditional target-PSU bootstrap 95% percentile interval was **[−0.00060627,+0.00090536]**; 200 source-plus-target PSU refits gave **[−0.00074832,+0.00158109]**. Negative changes would favor the feature. These intervals do not demonstrate equivalence or absence of all useful trial information. This is a preserved negative result for one feature and specified models, not a new biomarker claim.

## Question chosen from literature and data
A small primary physiological study compared repeated sustained submaximal handgrip contractions in people with type 2 diabetes and controls and found reduced endurance on the second contraction in diabetes ([primary study, 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16192898/)). This motivates asking whether the direction of repeated grip performance contains glycemic-state information beyond peak strength. It does **not** establish that short NHANES maximal efforts measure fatigue, autonomic damage or microcirculation. NHANES alternated hands, used three measurements per hand with approximately 60 seconds between same-hand trials, and provided conventional combined maximum strength ([G documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/MGX_G.htm), [H documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2013/DataFiles/MGX_H.htm)). Learning, effort and recovery may dominate trial direction.

## Feature, baseline and locked analysis
Let Hjt be kg grip for hand j and trial t and M=max(H11,H12,H13)+max(H21,H22,H23), verified against `MGDCGSZ`. Define **D=[(H13−H11)+(H23−H21)]/M**. Positive D indicates net improvement, not decline. D is unchanged by hand exchange and negated by reversing both trial sequences. Its unweighted source mean was 0.044023 and SD 0.080842; source standardization alone was applied to target D.

The **primary strong baseline** contains age, BMI, height, M, sum of hand means, absolute difference between hand maxima divided by M, and sum of within-hand population SDs divided by M; categorical sex, race/ethnicity and education. These summaries deliberately control strength and order-free dispersion, leaving D to test direction rather than merely weak or variable performance. The secondary conventional baseline uses age, BMI, height, M and normalized maximal-hand asymmetry with the same categories. This second comparator was registered before fitting, not chosen for significance. Standard measures are predictors here; the trial pattern is not asserted to be wholly unused across all existing literature.

Continuous variables receive source-fitted cubic B-spline bases (four quantile knots, degree three, no bias basis, linear extrapolation) followed by standard scaling. Categories are source-fitted one-hot indicators, unknown levels ignored. Ridge regression with alpha=10, unpenalized intercept, sample weights, Cholesky solver is fit in source only. Augmentation adds one standardized **linear** D column (48→49 primary columns; conventional 38→39). No hyperparameter is tuned on the target. Preprocessing and D normalization are refit inside each source five-fold GroupKFold with survey-PSU grouping, and each source bootstrap. Random seed 20261125. The hypothesized fatigability direction was greater HbA1c with more negative D; the fitted primary coefficient was instead +0.014493 HbA1c percentage points per source SD, a descriptive noncausal coefficient without an inferential interval.

Primary endpoint: target weighted MSE(augmented) minus MSE(baseline). MAE and source grouped-CV error are supporting metrics. Stratified rescaled bootstrap draws n−1 observed PSUs with replacement within each stratum with n PSUs, multiplying weights by draw counts×n/(n−1). There are 2,000 target-only replicates conditional on the original source fit, plus 200 independent source and target draws with full source refits. These are approximate percentile uncertainty intervals, not exact finite-survey design coverage or independent cohorts. Few strata and the 200-refit Monte Carlo size limit precision. No multiplicity-adjusted subgroup claims or post-target model selection.

## Limitations and future test
This negative result does not reject sustained endurance abnormalities in diabetes. Brief rested maximum efforts are a different construct. A linear added feature may miss nonlinear, hand-specific or temporal interactions. The additive baseline can retain residual confounding by activity, neuropathy, medications, illness, motivation, smoking, socioeconomic conditions and HbA1c-altering hematologic states. Pain and effort checks reduce only particular alternative explanations. Cross-sectional associations cannot show direction of causation. Complete-case losses and excluding people unable to complete six trials can remove the most impaired individuals. HbA1c lab-location shift and secular changes affect transfer. No clinical utility, diagnosis, causal mechanism or equivalence is established.

A future independently enrolled clinical study could prespecify the same six-trial feature and baseline while measuring neuropathy, medication, effort and recent activity, then compare brief-trial drift with sustained-endurance tasks and repeat-session reliability alongside laboratory glycemia. Randomized rest duration could distinguish recovery from learning or effort effects. Those are proposals; no recruitment, measurement, intervention or physical experiment was performed here.
