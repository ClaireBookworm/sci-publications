# Serum sodium–potassium coupling failed to improve cross-cycle cognitive-score prediction

## Abstract
In NHANES2011–12 training (n=1172) and2013–14 testing (n=1391) among adults60+, adding a standardized serum sodium×potassium term to a flexible electrolyte/demographic baseline changed weighted DSST MSE from 163.791368 to 164.663023. Baseline-minus-augmented MSE was -0.871655 (paired target-PSU95%CI -1.584145 to -0.040405); incremental benefit was not established. RMSE changed from 12.798100 to 12.832109 DSST points. A coefficient-refit interval spanned benefit and harm. Alternative penalties changed the sign, so neither robust predictive superiority nor universal harm is established. This preserves an unsuccessful candidate rather than claiming discovery of unused clinically useful information.

## Scientific question and motivation
Published studies already associate serum sodium with cognition [1,2], and joint serum sodium/potassium patterns with mortality [3]. The two electrolytes reflect different aspects of homeostasis, but serum concentrations are not intracellular ion gradients or a direct neuronal measurement. We asked whether their joint term contains **incremental, transportable cognitive prediction beyond flexible separate electrolyte measures**, not whether electrolytes are associated with cognition at all. Prior association and mortality interactions do not answer this predictive question. We do not claim to discover electrolyte coupling, the serum ratio, or a new physiological mechanism, nor that all current analyses omit joint terms. The serum ratio is included as an additional comparator, not described as an established clinical cognition score. Serum and dietary sodium/potassium ratios are not interchangeable.

The question was selected from primary literature and available public data, then locked in files/question-registration.json before main modeling. One SCI catalog duplication check with three ordinary retries returned HTTP403 (files/documentation/catalog-check.json). Duplication remains unverified; the catalog was not an idea source. No SCI publication is used or tested, so none is cited. The preceding completed package, ready request, receipt and evidence were preserved in a named archive, and its old ready request removed before this project.

## Population, statistical unit and independent datasets
NHANES2011–12(G) supplies training/discovery; NHANES2013–14(H) supplies the separate-cycle test. These are independent cross-sectional survey samples, **not random splits of one dataset**, although they share country, survey frame, instruments and assay protocols. We do not claim geographically or clinically external validation. DEMO, BMX, BIOPRO and CFQ files were joined one-to-one on public SEQN identifiers. Statistical unit: one participant. Within-cycle identifiers were unique and between-cycle SEQN intersection was zero. Public records cannot establish lifelong person-level re-enrollment; no attempt to reconstruct identity was made. No known participant overlap was found; unidentified overlap cannot be conclusively excluded.

Age eligibility was >=60 years. There were 9756 source/10175 target public records, 1791/1841 age-eligible adults, and 1172/1391 complete cases. Source/target male counts were 581/670. Cases required observed outcome and every covariate, positive sodium, potassium and MEC weight, and education codes1–5. Missing values were excluded, not imputed. This can select healthier, test-capable participants. No nonresponse correction of complete-case survey weights was fitted. Results are not claimed as nationally representative effect estimates.

The continuous outcome CFDDS is the Digit Symbol Substitution Test score, a performance measure, not dementia diagnosis or longitudinal decline [4]. Age is public-release top-coded. Sodium(LBXSNASI), potassium(LBXSKSI) and bicarbonate(LBXSC3SI) use mmol/L; glucose(LBXSGL) and creatinine(LBXSCR) mg/dL; albumin(LBXSAL) g/dL; BMI kg/m² [5]. Single laboratory values need not reflect stable homeostasis. Code aliases la and lt refer to **untransformed sodium and potassium**, not logarithms; there is no log transform in this analysis.

These cycles and cognitive outcomes were used for other questions earlier in this workspace and in the motivating literature. H was untouched by model/term selection **within this question**, but is not a globally pristine holdout. This is model-frozen cross-cycle testing, not strong pristine external confirmation; an entirely untouched cohort is still needed. Source cross-validation is exploratory. No target-based choice of primary penalty, covariate, term or cutoff was made.

## Baseline, proposed feature and analysis
The baseline is weighted ridge regression with cubic B-splines for age, income/poverty ratio, BMI, albumin, creatinine, serum glucose, bicarbonate, sodium, potassium and raw serum sodium/potassium ratio; sex, race/ethnicity and education are one-hot indicators. Four source-fitted uniformly spaced knots per continuous predictor, cubic degree, no spline bias, and linear extrapolation were fixed. Expanded columns are source-standardized. Primary ridge alpha=1, with unpenalized intercept. This is a flexible conventional laboratory/demographic comparator, not a validated clinical cognition model or exhaustive state-of-the-art comparison. Individual serum electrolytes are standard clinical measurements; the comparator additionally protects against mistaking their nonlinear marginal associations or ratio for new joint information.

The augmented model adds one product of source-standardized sodium and potassium, then standardizes its full design using source data. A deterministic interaction is a new representation, not a new measurement; its possible advantage depends on the restricted hypothesis class of the baseline. Both models fit WTMEC2YR weights, normalized to mean1 within source; target weighted losses similarly normalize target weights. All knots, scaling, categories and coefficients are learned on source alone. No target preprocessing or outcomes enter fitting. Fivefold shuffled source CV (seed71) refits transformations within folds and does not select models. Predictions are not clipped.

The primary estimand is baseline MSE minus augmented MSE on H, in DSST-points squared. Positive favors the interaction. Registered success required a95% uncertainty interval entirely above0. The paired bootstrap draws public PSUs with replacement within each public stratum;2000 replicates hold source predictions fixed. Source/target contain 31/30 PSUs. This is an approximate survey-design bootstrap with few PSUs, not a calibrated official NHANES variance method. A300-replicate sensitivity resamples both source and target PSUs and refits **regression coefficients only**, holding the original source transforms fixed. It is not a full-pipeline refit. Intervals are percentile intervals, seed71847. They do not account for prior question selection, unmeasured confounding or missingness.

## Actual results
Primary target MSE 163.791368→164.663023, gain -0.871655, target-PSU95%CI[-1.584145, -0.040405]. The conditional target interval favors a slight worsening for these fixed fitted models. The coefficient-refit95%CI[-2.533728, 0.753750] spans both directions, so we do not infer that coupling always worsens prediction. The absolute RMSE change is small and no clinical utility threshold was established. Interaction coefficient 2.422890 is in standardized-design units and is not a mechanistic effect.

Primary alpha1 remains primary. Alpha0.1 gives an apparent benefit but worse absolute error for both models; alpha10 gives lower absolute errors and still no interaction gain. These target comparisons are sensitivities, not a license to select a new successful primary. Strong regularization sensitivity precludes a representation-independent conclusion. Unweighted gain changes sign. Normal-electrolyte restriction means sodium135–145 and potassium3.5–5.0: the target-only restriction includes1267 participants; the both-cycles row refits coefficients on restricted source but retains original source transforms. These checks suggest abnormal electrolyte values alone do not explain the primary loss. Sex results are descriptive; no subgroup inference is made.

Four of five exploratory source folds had negative gains; all fold losses are retained in results.json, not pooled as an independent cohort. One hundred source-outcome shuffles provide a gross negative control; quantiles are retained. They destroy demographic associations too and are not a formal covariate-conditional null or valid interaction p-value. No unsuccessful fit or sensitivity was discarded.

## Scope, confounding and future test
No hidden physiological information outperforming standard measures was demonstrated. The negative result applies only to this product, comparator, DSST outcome, age group and cross-cycle evaluation. It does not refute published sodium–cognition or electrolyte–mortality associations, rule out other joint representations, demonstrate equivalence, or show absence of a biological role. Cross-sectional prediction is not causation. Kidney disease, drugs (especially diuretics), hydration, diet, cardiovascular and neurological disease, sampling/laboratory conditions, education and socioeconomic status can confound the measurements and cognition. Covariate adjustment is incomplete; serum glucose/creatinine/bicarbonate/albumin reduce some obvious ambiguity but do not remove it. Selection, missing tests, public age top-coding, common survey-frame bias and prior reuse of outcomes limit transfer claims. No new data collection, recruitment, intervention or physical experiment occurred.

A future independent prospective, multisite cohort could preregister the exact feature/baseline, measure repeated electrolytes and cognition, document medications and renal/hydration status, and assess calibration and clinically meaningful incremental prediction from a frozen model. Repeated measurements could test whether stable coupling differs from transient shifts. These are proposed tests, not work performed, and no sodium or potassium treatment recommendation follows.
