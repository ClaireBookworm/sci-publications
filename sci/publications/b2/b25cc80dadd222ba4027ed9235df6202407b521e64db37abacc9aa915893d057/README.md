# Signed pulse-pressure curvature: negligible Brier gain but a small AUC gain for reported heart failure across NHANES cycles

## Abstract
Adding signed pulse-pressure middle-reading deviation to a frozen clinical-covariate/mean-BP model gave target Brier gain 0.00000677 (400 PSU-refit percentile95% interval [-0.00021029,0.00013388]); the prespecified improvement criterion was not met. Secondary target AUC gain was 0.004365 [0.000739,0.007751]. This is cross-cycle, same-program validation, not clinical utility or independent-system validation.
The source contained 3,265 complete-case adults aged at least 40 (142 reported cases); the separately sampled target contained 3,509 (135 cases). A stronger BP-variability baseline changed Brier gain to −0.00000479. This scoped negative primary result does not establish absence of physiological information. The secondary ranking signal warrants untouched independent-system testing, not clinical deployment.

## Question and rationale
Repeated blood-pressure measurements are normally summarized by their mean. Primary research has investigated within-visit SD and mortality, with differing conclusions [1,2]; sequential reading changes can alter BP classification [3]. We ask whether signed curvature of pulse pressure (PP), rather than its mean or unordered dispersion, adds predictive information about prevalent reported congestive heart failure (CHF). This question was selected from that literature and temporal-order algebra, before a single SCI lexical duplication check. That check returned one unrelated photoacoustic paper; it cannot guarantee uniqueness. No SCI study supplied the hypothesis, method or evidence, and no SCI citation is necessary.

The candidate is q = PP₂ − (PP₁ + PP₃)/2, with PPᵢ = SBPᵢ − DBPᵢ, in mmHg. It distinguishes permutations with identical mean and SD; it is **not** time-irreversibility (it is unchanged by reversing all three readings). It may capture adjustment during the examination, noise, or operator behavior rather than cardiovascular regulation. The broad idea of within-visit patterns is established; we claim only this specific test and reusable negative evidence, not first discovery of BP variability.

## Public data, population and independent-sample scope
NHANES 2007–2008 is the source dataset and 2009–2010 the target dataset [4]. Public-use BPX, DEMO, BMX, MCQ, SMQ, DIQ and BPQ files were reused from local immutable caches and hashed afresh. Registry entries provide exact CDC URLs, bytes and SHA-256. No approval-only data, new collection, recruitment, interventions or experiments were used. Inputs total 40,443,680 bytes. Raw participant data are not redistributed. CDC public-use and restricted-access datasets are distinct [5].

The datasets are separate repeated cross-sectional survey samples, **not splits of one cycle**. No overlapping public SEQN was found. SEQN disjointness is not independent proof of person-level non-overlap; household/person linkage across cycles is unavailable. Both samples share the NHANES sampling frame, measurement protocol and institutional system. This is a real independent-sample check within one program, but validation in an independent study/system remains uncompleted. Claims are narrowed accordingly.

The statistical unit is one adult aged ≥40. Files are merged one-to-one by SEQN. CHF is MCQ160B=1 versus 2 (doctor ever said CHF); refused/unknown/missing are excluded. Three finite readings with SBP>DBP>0, all model covariates and positive examination weights are required. Ages are released top-coded; no attempt is made to reverse disclosure protection. Of adults remaining after table merges, source 3,863 and target 4,026 were eligible by age, with 598 and 517 excluded respectively. These are not counts from a missingness-corrected national cohort. Follow-up and incident outcomes are unavailable.

## Frozen methods and estimands
The primary estimand is change in mean participant-level Brier loss, baseline minus augmented, in the complete-case target dataset. This is an **unweighted prediction estimand**, not a national prevalence or causal estimate. The models are ridge logistic regressions with C=1; StandardScaler is fitted only to the source (or source training fold). Covariates: age, age², sex, race/ethnicity indicators with category 1 reference, BMI, ever smoking 100 cigarettes, diagnosed diabetes, antihypertensive use, mean of three SBP, mean of three DBP, resting pulse. Sex and released race/ethnicity categories are adjustment variables, not causal biological interpretations. Diabetes borderline responses are grouped with no; medication skip logic encodes no only for BPQ050A=2, BPQ020=2 or BPQ040A=2, yes for BPQ050A=1, otherwise missing. File codebooks and exact encodings are supplied.

The augmented model adds only q. No target fitting, threshold choice, recalibration or hyperparameter tuning occurred. Source evaluation uses five stratified folds (seed 581), not PSU- or household-held-out folds (so source cross-validation may share sampling-cluster context), with each scaler fitted inside each fold. Target results use all source data for fitting. The preanalysis criterion required positive source cross-validated Brier gain plus positive target gain with its 95% interval above zero. AUC is secondary. The plan was recorded before model execution; the encoding addendum was made after codebook inspection and before modeling. These public files had been used for other local projects; this was not a sequestered data repository or prospective test. No model pretraining is used.

Uncertainty uses 400 bootstrap replicates, separately sampling public masked PSUs with replacement inside each variance stratum in each cycle, refitting source preprocessing/models and evaluating paired loss differences on the resampled target. Percentile intervals are conditional on the modeling procedure and complete-case selection; they are not multiplicity-adjusted. They do not fully represent design-based finite population inference. The bootstrap table retains every replicate. Survey examination-weighted target scoring is a sensitivity, not the primary estimand. No power-based equivalence margin was specified.

## Falsification and sensitivity
All prespecified challenges were run, including C=0.1 and 10, older target adults, weighted scoring, SD/slope-adjusted baseline, sign reversal, permutation, leakage and algebra checks. Target Brier gain was 0.00001333 at C=0.1, 0.00000525 at C=10, −0.00003239 in target adults ≥60, and 0.00006047 with examination-weighted scoring. Adding SBP/DBP SDs and first-to-third slopes to the baseline gave gain −0.00000479, while its AUC gain remained 0.004676. Sign-reversing q worsened augmented target Brier to 0.03627618. A single target-q permutation produced 0.03612590, better than either original model: this is a challenge to strong Brier claims, not a permutation p-value. Mathematical controls verified invariance to constant PP offset and full reading reversal, a manual calculation of the first 20 target q values, row-reordering consistency and target-label independence of predictions.

A post-result quadratic baseline challenge (all baseline squares/two-way interactions, C=0.1, q added afterward) is reported separately in files/challenge.json. It is exploratory and cannot rescue a failed primary criterion. All planned sensitivities are in results.json. None was used to redefine the central model or choose a new primary outcome. No independent peer review was performed.

## Limitations and interpretation
Only 277 complete-case reported CHF cases were available across the two datasets. Reporting, healthcare access and survival may bias labels. Medication type/dose/timing, arrhythmia, unmeasured cardiac disease, cuff/operator effects, time between readings and examination anxiety can confound q. Cross-sectional prediction cannot establish whether CHF causes the pattern or the pattern precedes CHF. Complete-case exclusions can induce selection bias; the masked-PSU bootstrap does not correct that. The conventional BP means are relevant standard measures, but our multivariable model is not an externally validated CHF risk score. Additional nonlinear models could yield different results. The AUC result is secondary, and metric disagreement plus sensitivity to baseline specification precludes a clinical-utility claim. There is no independent-program cohort here, no mortality validation and no demonstrated measurement repeatability.

A future clinical study could preregister q and these frozen models in a different institution, record repeat visits and cuff timing, adjudicate CHF with clinical examination/echocardiography, and test repeatability and incremental calibrated prediction versus standard measures. That study was **not performed**. No diagnostic or treatment recommendation follows.

## Limitations and future test
This is cross-sectional self-reported historical diagnosis, vulnerable to reverse causation, recall, healthcare access and treatment effects. Medication class, dose and timing, autonomic function, contemporaneous ventricular function and operator effects are unmeasured. SD/slopes are noisy from three readings. Complete-case and positive DBP restrictions may induce selection; no missing-at-random correction was attempted. Modest case counts, low event prevalence, age top-coding and shared survey methods limit sensitivity and transfer. The baseline is a conventional risk-factor model, not an established CHF diagnostic score; it lacks echocardiography, natriuretic peptides and symptoms. We do not show improvement over standard clinical CHF diagnostic workup. No causal mechanism or clinical utility is established. AUC and Brier are different estimands; the AUC interval is secondary, with no multiple-testing correction. No threshold, decision-curve or equivalence claim is made.

A future independent study could preregister q, measure its test–retest reliability from standardized repeated BP, and evaluate frozen risk models against adjudicated CHF, echocardiography and biomarkers, while recording treatment timing and measurement operator. A prospective cohort could test incident outcomes separately from prevalent CHF. These are proposals only; no such clinical or experimental study was performed.
