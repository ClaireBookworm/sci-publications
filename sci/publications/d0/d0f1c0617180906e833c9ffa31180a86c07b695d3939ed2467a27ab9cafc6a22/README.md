# Thyroid–pituitary coupling does not consistently improve independent-cycle radial-pulse prediction

## Abstract and scoped claim
A source-standardized log-TSH × free-T4 product was tested for incremental information about radial pulse. A fixed model trained in 4,951 NHANES 2007–2008 adults was transferred without tuning to 1,847 adults from 2009–2010. Survey-weighted target MSE was 128.1945 bpm² with flexible individual hormone and conventional covariate effects, versus 128.2524 with the interaction (difference +0.0578; +0.0451%, worse). Target-only and two-sample refit bootstrap intervals included zero. Source PSU-held-out prediction also worsened. These results do not demonstrate consistent incremental prediction from this particular representation. They do not establish equivalence, absence of thyroid physiology, or invalidity of published sensitivity-index associations.

## Question, motivation, and novelty boundary
[Roef et al., PMID23339744](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23339744/) reported associations of thyroid hormones with heart rate in an adult population. [Ye et al., PMID39986552](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39986552/) studied thyroid-sensitivity indices and low heart rate in 550 participants. Neither finding itself establishes that a combined hormone representation improves prediction over flexible component effects in a separate sample. We ask that narrower question for one fixed logTSH-by-free-T4 product. This is not a replication of either paper's exact endpoint or population, nor a claim that combined hormone indices are new or unused worldwide. A deterministic product adds no new measurement: any potential benefit is representational nonadditivity.

The question and primary plan were registered locally before outcome modeling (`files/question-registration.json`). This is not a public preregistration; the broader worker program had previous unrelated NHANES analyses. A single post-choice SCI catalog duplication check on 2026-11-18, query “thyroid pulse interaction,” returned HTTP503 on all three same-query attempts (`files/evidence/catalog-check.json`). Duplication is unknown, not ruled out. No SCI publication supplied the question or was used/tested, so none is cited.

## Public inputs, population, and independence
Public-use CDC NHANES DEMO, BMX, BPX, THYROD, SMQ, and RXQ_RX XPT files for 2007–2008 (E) and 2009–2010 (F) were used. Acquisition URLs, byte sizes, retrieval/cache provenance and SHA-256 for every input appear in `files/input-manifest.json`. Raw files are not redistributed. Use follows the [CDC data-user agreement](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/policy/data-user-agreement.html); no approval-only files, new collection, recruitment, experiments, or reidentification were involved.

The statistical unit is a participant aged ≥20 with complete BMI, individual thyroid measures, pulse, blood pressure, smoking and survey-design variables, and positive laboratory values/weights. E contained 5,935 adults, 5,439 with pulse and 4,951 eligible complete cases; F contained 6,218 adults, 5,796 with pulse and 1,847 complete cases. The large F reduction partly reflects its one-third thyroid subsample, not simply nonresponse. Source and target are separate cross-sectional sampling cycles, not splits of one dataset. Eligible SEQN intersection was zero. SEQN separation is not a direct identity audit: public files lack direct identifiers to prove absence of repeat persons. Shared recruitment design, instruments and country limit geographic/platform generalization.

The outcome is `BPXPLS`, a 30-second radial-pulse count multiplied by two, in bpm—not a continuous ECG, arrhythmia diagnosis, or 24-hour rate. See [BPX documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2009/DataFiles/BPX_F.htm). TSH=`LBXTSH1` µIU/mL; freeT4=`LBXT4F` ng/dL; freeT3=`LBXT3F` pg/mL. [E thyroid notes](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2007/DataFiles/THYROD_E.htm) specify exam weights; [F notes](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2009/DataFiles/THYROD_F.htm) provide A-subsample weights. Accordingly E uses WTMEC2YR, F WTSA2YR. TT4 lab crossover notes do not concern the freeT4 endpoint used here.

## Fixed prediction methods
The baseline is weighted ridge regression (alpha10, unpenalized intercept, training weights normalized to mean1) with additive cubic splines for age, BMI, mean of available first-three SBP and DBP readings, logTSH, freeT4, and freeT3. Each variable uses four uniform knots, degree3, no spline bias, linear extrapolation, followed by source-fitted standardization. Additional terms are sex, race/ethnicity indicators (RIDRETH1 reference1, indicators2–5), current smoking, thyroid-medication and beta-blocker flags; age and BMI spline effects interact with sex. Race is an adjustment descriptor, not a biological mechanism. The augmented model adds exactly one product: z(logTSH)·z(freeT4), then source-standardizes that product. All transformations, knots and parameters are fitted using source participants only. Target labels never select hyperparameters or the primary representation.

Smoking uses SMQ020/SMQ040 skip logic. Drug-name regexes flag listed thyroid agents and beta blockers in public prescription records; any matched drug sets the participant flag. Absent/unreported drug names count as no matched drug, not verified nonuse. Source/target thyroid flags numbered338/134 and beta flags661/266. See executable code for exact lists and matching. These coarse flags cannot remove indication, dose, adherence or all rate-modifying drug confounding.

Primary assessment is survey-weighted paired target MSE; negative augmented-minus-baseline differences favor the product. RMSE, MAE, R² and bias are secondary. The directional prediction required lower MSE with a95% interval excluding zero; a1% relative gain was a pragmatic, not clinically validated, benchmark. Source discovery assessment uses five GroupKFold splits by survey PSU; it does not constitute independent-dataset validation. Uncertainty uses1,000 target-only rescaled stratified PSU bootstrap replicates conditional on source fit and200 independently resampled source-and-target refit replicates. Within each stratum with n PSUs, sample n−1 PSUs with replacement and multiply weights by n/(n−1); preserve duplicated blocks. The [rescaled survey bootstrap](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/71-526-x/2017001/chapt7-eng.htm) accounts for clustering and stratification approximately, not every NHANES design or nonresponse feature. E had16 strata/32 PSUs; F15/31. Percentile intervals are approximate, especially the small200-replicate refit tail; they are not multiplicity-adjusted.

## Limitations and future test
This is a fixed negative incremental prediction test, not evidence that thyroid information is absent or that no nonlinear representation could help. Few survey PSUs, a smaller target subsample, complete-case selection, coarse medication flags, residual confounding (illness, fitness, activity, autonomic state, timing and additional drugs), pulse rounding, high-leverage values and source extrapolation constrain inference. Blood pressure covariates can capture shared/downstream physiology: the conditional prediction question is not a total causal thyroid effect. Both datasets are NHANES cycles, independently recruited but not independently instrumented cohorts. No clinical outcomes, prospective prediction, causal pathways, tissue sensitivity measurements or clinical utility were demonstrated.

A future independent clinical study could prespecify this exact predictor and additive/index comparators, assess simultaneous ECG and thyroid tests with verified drugs and assay-specific intervals, and measure repeat/resting and24-hour rates. It could test whether a stable small gain remains after activity/medication control and improves a meaningful decision. That study was not performed here.
