# Summary early-expiratory volume ratio did not establish added wheeze prediction across separate NHANES samples

## Abstract and central claim
Adding summary FEV0.5/FEV1 to a source-trained model containing standard volumes and midflow did not establish reliable target wheeze prediction gain: Brier difference -0.00009733, paired PSU95% interval [-0.00023149,0.00003852], NHANES2007-08 n3670 to independently sampled2011-12 n3650. The target Brier score changed from 0.10421330 to 0.10411598; the registered success criterion was not met. This does not demonstrate equivalence or absence of biological information. It limits a specific linear summary-ratio addition to fixed additive logistic models. A post-registration effort-adjusted sensitivity reversed the point gain.

## Question and rationale
Standard adult spirometry emphasizes FEV1, FVC, and FEV1/FVC; the ATS/ERS statement lists FEV0.5 as optional [1]. Extended reference-equation work motivates evaluating other curve summaries [2]. Mid-expiratory flow already has documented physiology associations [3], so it is a strong comparator, not a novel feature here. We asked whether summary FEV0.5/FEV1 contains useful wheeze-prediction information beyond these measures. Ratios and early volumes are not claimed to be novel or unused everywhere. No new physiological recordings, recruitment, interventions or experiments occurred.

The question was selected from literature and public data, not SCI. Its title-based SCI duplication check, performed after registration, returned zero matches (files/catalog-check.json); alternate terminology and concurrent unpublished work may be missed. An earlier BP-curvature candidate was abandoned when its duplication check found an existing study; the failed attempt is retained in the worker archive, not counted as supporting evidence or cited. The urine-electrolyte alternative was rejected during feasibility because access is restricted. No SCI publication was used or tested in this analysis.

## Population, independent datasets and outcomes
Source: public NHANES2007–2008; confirmation: public NHANES2011–2012. Each is a separately sampled cross-sectional survey, not a train/test partition of one release. Noninstitutionalized adults20–79 were included, excluding known pregnancy (RIDEXPRG=1), requiring FEV1/FVC quality A or B, positive FEV0.5, FEV1, FVC and FEF25–75, complete covariates, positive MEC weights and valid RDQ070. A/B meet or exceed the survey's ATS collection standards [4]. Pregnancy status missing is not treated as pregnant. Missing smoking status is excluded. The code supplies the complete selection logic.

Of5439 age/pregnancy-eligible source participants,3670 entered (513 wheeze events); of5140 target participants,3650 entered (475 events). Source mean age47.31, female50.44%, wheeze13.98%; target45.45,49.86%,13.01%. Excluded participants were older on average (51.44 and50.68). This is selected complete-case prediction, not national prevalence estimation.

The outcome is reported wheezing/whistling in the chest during the past12months (RDQ070=1 vs2) [6]. It is not clinician-diagnosed asthma, COPD or prospective exacerbation. Measurement and reported symptom refer to overlapping periods, so this is cross-sectional symptom prediction, not future risk. Participant is the statistical unit. Merges are one-to-one by SEQN; IDs are unique and disjoint across releases. Public data cannot establish absence of re-enrolment across cycles, and the common survey, equipment and protocols limit independence beyond sampling. No longitudinal repeated participant data were intentionally used.

## Locked models, prediction and uncertainty
The routine baseline uses L2-penalized logistic regression with additive cubic splines (4 source quantile knots, degree3, no spline bias, linear extrapolation) for age, BMI, height, FEV1, FVC and FEV1/FVC. Sex, race/ethnicity (RIDRETH1 fixed categories) and never/current/former smoking are categorical predictors. The strong baseline additionally includes a spline for FEF25–75. Augmentation adds one standardized continuous term H=FEV0.5/FEV1. All spline knots and scaling are fitted only on source participants. No percent-predicted reference population, pretrained model or target normalization was used.

C=1, L-BFGS, tolerance1e-9 and max3000iterations were fixed rather than tuned. A constant column is included, fit_intercept=False; all coefficients including constant receive the L2 penalty. Nonconstant columns are standardized on source. Five-fold source CV refits preprocessing inside each fold (seed831); it is exploratory, participant-wise rather than PSU-wise, and not independent validation. Source fits and CV are saved in locked-source-models.json before the program loads target analysis outcomes. No target-based feature/model selection was performed. Retrieval stored both releases before fitting, but the analysis ordering is explicit; confirmation is analytically untouched, not a blinded external team.

Primary success required negative target Brier difference (augmented minus strong baseline) with the paired95% interval upper bound below zero. We report2000 stratified rescaled n-1 PSU bootstrap replicates for fixed target predictions (seed181121), multiplying counts by n/(n-1) within each stratum, with n the number of observed PSUs. Source has16strata/32PSUs; target14strata/31PSUs. These are approximate design-cluster intervals in the complete-case sample. We also report300 source-and-target PSU refits to reflect training variability; spline preprocessing remains frozen at source-fit values in this sensitivity. Primary fitting/scoring is unweighted for the observed complete-case distribution; MEC-weighted source fits and target scoring are a sensitivity, with source weights normalized to mean1. Small design-cluster counts and selection limit inference.

## Falsification and sensitivity checks
Target A-only quality, men, women and never-smoker paired intervals all included zero; these are secondary subsets, not independent datasets and not multiplicity-corrected claims. Their exact sizes, metrics and intervals are in results.json. Source-shuffling the early ratio gave target difference0.00000315; this is one negative control, not a permutation significance test.

After the primary analysis, we added source-fitted splines of peak expiratory flow, back-extrapolated volume and forced expiratory time to the strong comparator (complete ancillary measures; target n3648;1000 target bootstrap replicates). The added ratio now worsened Brier by0.00004891 (fixed-model95% interval[0.00000806,0.00008541]). This warns against interpreting the tiny unadjusted gain as physiology independent of start/effort. It does not establish that effort causes the association, and this interval excludes training uncertainty. The sensitivity was not prespecified as a primary test and no new candidate replaced the failed primary.

Author self-check: an independently written SciPy BFGS solver reproduced all four primary probability vectors within4.4e-6; one fit returned a precision-loss status despite max gradient2.8e-6. This is disclosed rather than called perfect optimizer convergence. The primary BFGS fits converged. Ratio arithmetic, probability bounds, unique identifiers, disjoint releases and valid early-ratio range were checked. A clean offline rerun repeats all fits, bootstrap calculations and target predictions; exact-file comparisons are recorded in the self-check artifact. These are author checks, **not independent peer review**.

## Limitations and interpretation
FEV0.5/FEV1 is a ratio of released best-value summaries; CDC notes that best values for different parameters may come from different acceptable maneuvers [4]. It must not be presented as a verified same-maneuver emptying fraction or an anatomical small-airway measure. We use no raw-curve alignment. The range is plausible but that does not remove this limitation. Spirometric effort, symptoms influencing effort, reporting error, medications and unmeasured respiratory disease remain possible confounders. Covariate adjustment is not causal identification; no clinical diagnosis or treatment recommendation follows.

The primary test is negative, not an equivalence test or rejection of early-expiratory physiology. There is no independent health-system or equipment validation, no longitudinal outcome, no correction for complete-case selection, no nonlinear early-ratio alternative tested, and no proof this is the optimal model family. Same-family temporal transport is narrower than universal generalization. Publication does not establish clinical utility.

A future clinical study could prespecify same-maneuver early-volume extraction and repeated effort-controlled spirometry, enroll an independent clinical population, document medications and compare prospective symptom/exacerbation prediction against established lung-function measures. This is a proposed test only; none of these data were collected here.
