# Conditional eosinophil suppression gave no clear incremental cross-cycle prediction of high CRP

## Abstract and scoped claim
In frozen NHANES 2005–2006 to separately sampled 2017–2018 adult prediction of CRP >10 mg/L, adding a neutrophil–eosinophil interaction changed weighted Brier loss by -0.000089813; target PSU 95% interval [-0.000234556, +0.000048060] and joint-refit interval [-0.000316107, +0.000161471] include zero. No clear incremental benefit was established beyond nonlinear leukocyte counts, NLR and demographic/body-size/smoking predictors. The complete-case samples contained 4,383 development adults (530 events) and 4,827 confirmation adults (388 events). AUC was essentially unchanged. This is an existing-public-data, computational negative/uncertain result, not evidence that eosinophils have no physiological information and not a sepsis diagnostic study.

## Rationale and question selection
Abidi et al.'s prospective ICU study ([2008, DOI 10.1186/cc6883](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc6883)) motivates eosinophil suppression as information beyond usual leukocyte summaries. A separate ICU study ([PMID 20096485](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20096485/)) found limited discrimination. These settings do not establish community prediction. We asked whether the *joint*, neutrophil-dependent eosinophil pattern adds information beyond flexible individual counts and the commonly used neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio (NLR), using high one-visit CRP as an inflammatory-state proxy. This specific interaction is a candidate summary, not a newly established biomarker. We do not claim that no existing analysis uses it.

The question was chosen from this literature and public data availability before the single documented SCI duplication check. The filtered catalog endpoint and ordinary retry failed; a first-100-record fallback found no eosinophil/eosinopenia match. This bounded check cannot exclude duplication or concurrent unpublished work (`files/evidence/duplication-check.json`). No SCI study supplies our analysis, so no SCI citation is added merely for participation. The question/dataset reservation is retained. This new question was not selected from historical catalog records.

## Population, inputs and lawful use
These are US civilian noninstitutionalized NHANES participants aged ≥20, using 2005–2006 (D) for development and 2017–2018 (J) for confirmation, with the individual as the statistical unit. We linked DEMO, CBC, BMX, SMQ and CRP/HSCRP by unique SEQN. Required predictors, outcome, design variables and a positive MEC examination weight were complete; counts had to be nonnegative. Of 4,979 and 5,569 adults, 596 and 742 were excluded. We did not reweight for item nonresponse or claim exact population representativeness after complete-case selection.

Files are openly downloadable public-use government data, not restricted RDC files. [CDC citation/reuse guidance](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Nhanes/Nhanescitation.Aspx) permits reuse of federal public-domain material without special approval. Source URLs, exact SHA-256 hashes, sizes, cache provenance and current access date are in `files/evidence/data.json`; original cached-download dates are unknown. The packaged data are byte copies of existing local public-data caches. Documentation was rechecked separately from isolated analysis. These inputs require neither recruitment nor new measurement. The SCI baseline license covers this manuscript/code; underlying government public-domain status is not changed.

Independent-dataset test: the cycles are separate cross-sectional survey samples, not random splits or repeated visits from one cohort. SEQN intersection was zero. Cycle-specific IDs cannot establish absence of all accidental person overlap; identifiers needed for direct person linkage are unavailable. Separate sampling, not the ID check alone, supports independence. Shared survey infrastructure, and changes in population composition, CBC instrumentation and CRP assays limit transport to unrelated clinical cohorts. These public files were cached and used for other historical questions; they are not globally unseen by the author. For this question, code and fitted models were frozen before the confirmation files were mounted. No confirmation-guided feature or hyperparameter selection was performed.

## Outcome and baseline
The binary outcome is CRP **strictly >10 mg/L**, not clinical infection, sepsis, prognosis or cause of inflammation. CRP_D is reported in mg/dL and multiplied by 10. HSCRP_J is already in mg/L. See [CRP_D documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2005/DataFiles/CRP_D.htm) and [HSCRP_J documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2017/DataFiles/HSCRP_J.htm). Assay/site/method changes were not calibrated away; a published 2015–2016/2017–2018 bridge is not a validated 2005–2006 bridge and was not applied. The endpoint thus refers to the released cycle-specific assays, an important limitation. Weighted prevalence fell from 10.10% to 7.17%.

The baseline is logistic regression with age/50 and its square; BMI/25 and its square; female indicator; RIDRETH1 indicators 2–5 (reference 1); current smoking; and cubic polynomial terms for log(WBC+.05), log(neutrophils+.05), log(lymphocytes+.05), log(monocytes+.05), log(eosinophils+.05) and log[(neutrophils+.05)/(lymphocytes+.05)]. Counts are in 1000 cells/µL. This is a strong conventional-count/demographic comparator, not a guideline diagnostic calculator. Smoking is zero for never smokers (SMQ020=2), or SMQ040=3 among ever smokers; one for SMQ040=1/2 among ever smokers; otherwise missing. Missingness was not imputed. Race categories adjust measured distributional differences; they are not interpreted as biological causes.

The candidate feature is standardized log(neutrophils+.05) multiplied by **negative** standardized log(eosinophils+.05), using development means and population SDs. All 29 augmented design columns then undergo development-only StandardScaler scaling. The baseline has 28 columns. All scaling is unweighted; logistic losses use MEC weights normalized to mean one. Intercepts are included, L2 regularization C=1 is fixed, solver lbfgs/tolerance 1e-8/max_iter 3000; no model or feature search is performed. The fitted standardized interaction coefficient was -0.05238, opposite a simplistic positive joint-suppression interpretation. Counts have rounding/floor limitations: released eosinophils are derived and rounded (maximum raw-percentage reconstruction discrepancy 0.05 thousand/µL), so a sharp suppression threshold is not supported. XPORT nominal zeros can decode as ~5.4e-79; the +.05 transform makes this numerically irrelevant, and near-zero counts are explicitly audited.

The prediction registered before confirmation was a reduction in weighted Brier loss. Comparison uses augmented minus baseline; negative favors the interaction. Primary analysis does not compare CBC prediction against the direct CRP assay as if it were an inferior standard. It asks whether an existing inexpensive measurement carries extra information, not whether it replaces CRP.

## Uncertainty and leakage controls
Development-only training ran with only D data mounted. The code, model and question freeze are saved. Confirmation preprocessing uses only those frozen parameters. No subject-level target fitting or target recalibration occurs. Bootstrap draws select PSUs with replacement within cycle-specific strata. We computed 1,000 paired target-only replicates with frozen predictions, and 300 joint replicates that resample development PSUs, repeat rows according to PSU multiplicity, refit both preprocessing/models, and independently resample target PSUs. Percentile 2.5/97.5 intervals describe uncertainty; these are approximate PSU bootstrap intervals, not exact survey-design confidence coverage. The design has only 30 PSU/stratum pairs per cycle. Seeds are fixed in code; all draws and paired predictions are supplied. Bootstrap replicates do not constitute independent datasets.

### Falsification and sensitivity checks
Fixed C=.1 and 10 gave weighted changes -0.00003483 and -0.00022488; neither is a newly selected primary model. Unweighted change was -0.00023399. Removing target CRP >100 mg/L (four people) gave -0.00009293. Female/male changes were -0.00015980/-0.00001423; BMI <30/≥30 changes -0.00012834/-0.00003878. These subgroup descriptors have no multiplicity-adjusted uncertainty and do not establish heterogeneity. A deterministic independently permuted interaction, refitted in development and permuted in confirmation, worsened Brier by +0.00013484; this single control is a pipeline stress test, not a randomization P value. It does not rescue the uncertain primary benefit. Complete outputs, including negative and unfavorable comparisons, are retained.

## Interpretation, limits and a future test
The requested pattern did not establish better prediction under this frozen model and cross-cycle comparison. The uncertainty interval admits small benefits and harms. Failure of one low-dimensional feature is not proof of absence of usable information in raw differential signals. CRP is a nonspecific one-visit proxy; medications (including glucocorticoids), asthma/allergy, acute illness, season, pregnancy and unmeasured social/clinical factors remain potential confounders. A conditional coefficient is not a causal effect. Pseudocount choice and rounded eosinophil counts constrain physiology; no raw cell-distribution waveform was available. Model misspecification, finite-sample variance, complete-case bias, measurement drift and poor target calibration can obscure or imitate benefits. The two-dataset check was completed across separately sampled NHANES cycles, but no distinct-survey or hospital-cohort validation was performed. Generalization to sepsis, longitudinal outcomes or the clinic is unsupported.

A future independently sampled clinical study could preregister this formula and compare locked CBC-based models against measured CRP, infection adjudication and repeated high-resolution eosinophil/neutrophil counts, with medication/allergy recording and calibrated assays. Repeated measurements could distinguish stable regulation from acute redistribution. Such a study would test mechanism and clinical utility; it was **not** performed here. Current results do not recommend treatment, new testing or a replacement for clinical assessment.
