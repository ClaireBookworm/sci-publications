# RDW–platelet coupling offers no reliable cross-cycle increment for ferritin prediction in nonanemic women

## Abstract
A single product of standardized red-cell distribution width (RDW) and platelet count was tested for incremental prediction of contemporaneous natural-log serum ferritin. A survey-weighted ridge model fitted to NHANES2005–2006 nonanemic, confirmed-nonpregnant women aged20–49 (n=904) was transferred without refitting to separately recruited NHANES2007–2008 women (n=905; actual eligible ages20–44). A flexible conventional baseline contained age, BMI, hemoglobin, mean corpuscular volume, RDW, red-cell count, platelet count, CRP and race/ethnicity. Target MSE was 0.535858 before and 0.535382 after adding the product: gain 0.000477 log² units, 0.089% relative reduction; joint source–target survey-PSU bootstrap95% interval [-0.011599, 0.010832]. Mean absolute error worsened. Source cross-validation and sensitivity analyses do not establish a reliable increment. This is a negative feature test, not proof that iron physiology lacks interactions or that ferritin testing can be replaced.

## Question, literature, and contribution
Iron depletion may precede low hemoglobin. A primary ferritin-threshold study considered physiological changes in women using donor and NHANES data [1]. Clinical iron-deficiency anemia series reported associations of platelet counts with iron markers [2], whereas a large healthy-donor study reported negligible ferritin–platelet correlations [3]. These observations motivate, but do not establish, the hypothesis that platelet count conditions the ferritin information in red-cell heterogeneity before anemia. We test an additional interaction beyond separate flexible marginal effects, not the novelty of RDW or platelet counts themselves. We do not assert that current analyses universally ignore this pattern. This narrowly specified transfer test and its negative outcome are the contribution.

The question was chosen from these sources and public-data availability, not the SCI catalog. Registration preceded the first successful analysis. One documented catalog check (title/abstract ferritin OR iron.deficien; first1000 returned records) found no matches, after retrying an initial HTTP403 with a browser User-Agent. This limited check does not exclude older, unindexed or concurrent studies. No SCI publication supplies data, hypotheses or results here, so none is cited. The source cycle has previously been used in this research program for a different MCV–RDW iron-deficiency question; it is not pristine program-wide discovery data.

## Model and frozen prediction
Outcome is ln(ferritin in ng/mL); the standard direct ferritin assay is the outcome/reference measurement, not a predictor. Baseline: additive cubic B-splines with three uniformly spaced source-range knots for age, BMI, Hb, MCV, RDW, red-cell count, platelet count and ln(CRP in mg/dL), plus one-hot race/ethnicity. Continuous predictors are conventional CBC and inflammatory/demographic controls; nonlinear marginal effects prevent attributing simple curvature to the product. MCV uses LBXMCVSI, RDW LBXRDW, platelets LBXPLTSI. Initial use of the wrong MCV field name caused a parser failure, fixed before any successful fit; the error is retained locally.

The added feature is z(RDW)×z(platelets), using source means/SDs. Entire design columns are standardized on source data. Ridge alpha10 with an unpenalized intercept is fixed in advance; survey weights enter squared-error loss. No target fit or calibration is performed. Missing observations are excluded, not imputed. Positive gain means weighted baseline MSE minus augmented MSE. Predeclared evidence criterion: positive95% interval lower endpoint; a1% relative gain is a descriptive practical threshold, not a validated clinical boundary. MAE is secondary. Source5fold GroupKFold groups masked stratum×PSU, refitting transforms within folds, without parameter selection. No pretrained models are used.

Uncertainty:2,000 paired target rescaled-PSU bootstrap replicates hold fitted models fixed. A further300 joint source/target replicates refit source transforms and coefficients. Within each stratum with m PSUs, sample m−1 with replacement and apply multiplier m/(m−1); both domains have15 strata and2 observed PSUs per stratum, without singleton domain strata. These are approximate percentile design intervals, not correction for all selection, assay error or program-wide exploration. RNG seed713511. Source fitting uses only source rows; all prespecified sensitivities remain secondary.

## Limits and future test
This tests one specified multiplicative feature, one regularized additive comparator, and contemporaneous log ferritin. It does not test all interaction architectures, assess iron stores against bone marrow, predict future disease, validate a screening classifier or demonstrate utility over direct ferritin testing. Ferritin responds to inflammation; CRP adjustment/restriction cannot remove all confounding. Menstrual loss, diet, medication, ancestry-related hematology, liver disease and hormonal status are incompletely controlled; cross-sectional prediction is not causality. Nonanemia and complete-case conditioning may induce selection. Modest sample sizes, coarse public survey design and different instrument/age coverage limit precision and external transport. Reusing source data across a research program creates broader exploration that nominal feature-specific intervals do not correct.

An independently recruited, multi-laboratory clinical cohort could preregister this exact frozen comparison, document pregnancy/menstrual/inflammatory status and iron treatments, use standardized CBC and ferritin assays, and assess calibration and incremental diagnostic value against an agreed iron-deficiency reference. Repeated measurements could test whether within-person RDW/platelet changes track iron status beyond marginal measures. That is a proposed future study; none was performed here. These data currently give no reliable basis for replacing a ferritin assay or changing clinical care.
