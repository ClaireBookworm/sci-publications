# Bilateral discordance of repeated grip change does not establish added digit-symbol prediction across NHANES samples

## Summary
In older adults who completed both grip testing and digit-symbol substitution, the difference between the two hands' first-to-third grip changes offered only a small, uncertain predictive gain beyond standard grip summaries and demographic covariates. A fixed model trained in NHANES 2011–2012 (1,245 adults) was evaluated without refitting in the separate 2013–2014 sample (1,397 adults). Mean squared error (MSE) decreased by 0.3107 score², with a conditional stratified-PSU bootstrap 95% interval of −0.2627 to 0.9357. The prespecified benefit criterion failed. Survey-weighted fitting/scoring and a pain-free standing-only analysis reversed the gain. This is a negative incremental-prediction result, not evidence of absence of all neuromuscular information in repeated grip trials.

## Scientific question and rationale
Standard grip summaries discard trial order. Experimental repetitive-grip work documents changes with repetitions, but its short-rest, 30-trial protocol is not the NHANES three-trial protocol [1]. Grip strength has been associated with cognition in these NHANES years [2], while another primary investigation challenges the specificity of adjusted grip–cognition associations [3]. We therefore asked whether *between-hand discordance of short-session grip change*, rather than the known peak-strength association, carries additional information about digit-symbol performance. No claim of first discovery of grip variability or grip–cognition associations is made. This particular feature and fixed cross-release prediction test are the contribution.

The literature/data-based question and comparison were written before acquisition of cognitive files and main fitting (`files/question.md`). A single subsequent SCI duplication check, with an endpoint retry, found no matching feature/outcome analysis (`files/catalog-note.md`, saved JSON). SCI publications were not used or tested in this analysis.

CDC/NCHS NHANES public-use XPT files MGX, CFQ, DEMO and BMX for 2011–2012 (G) and 2013–2014 (H) were used. They are openly downloadable without special approval; no restricted data, participant contact or reidentification was involved. CDC government data and source attribution are retained. Input provenance, eight exact SHA-256 hashes, URLs, file sizes and access timestamps are in `files/datasets.json`. New cognitive files were downloaded on 18 November 2026; grip/demographic/body-measure files reused existing local caches, verified against their declared hashes. No raw source files are redistributed; the downloader reproduces the declared inputs.

The population is examined, noninstitutionalized US adults aged ≥60 with six positive grip readings, completed bilateral test status, observed DSST score, age, sex, race/ethnicity, education and BMI, and positive examination weight. Education refusal/unknown codes were excluded. No outcome or predictor imputation was used. Of 1,791 and 1,841 age-eligible adults, 1,245 and 1,397 respectively met all criteria; excluded counts are 546 and 444. Thus the primary estimand concerns complete tested participants, not every older American. Missing/failed cognitive pretests are not scored as zero. DSST is a performance score, not a dementia diagnosis.

Each row is one participant, linked one-to-one across components by SEQN. Analytic IDs are unique and disjoint (G 62174–71915; H 73557–83724). NHANES is repeated cross-sectional rather than a longitudinal cohort. These are separate survey-release samples, not a random split of one dataset. They share instrument, survey infrastructure and US source population; there is no independent institution/country validation. No public cross-cycle person linkage is available, so disjoint IDs cannot categorically exclude unidentified repeat enrollment. Earlier operator projects used these grip caches for mobility analyses; cognition was newly acquired here. Confirmation was outcome-untouched for this project, not wholly data-naive. Unknown cross-project selection effects remain possible.

## Prespecified models and analysis
NHANES measures three squeezes per hand, alternating hands with 60-second rest for a given hand [4]. Hand 1/2 is measurement order, not assumed right/left. Let x(h,t) be grip kg and s be the mean of each hand's maximum. The proposed scalar is

`D = abs([x(1,3)-x(1,1)] - [x(2,3)-x(2,1)]) / s`.

It is invariant to exchanging hands and to reversing trial order; it measures discordance, **not the direction of fatigue**, recovery, or a validated physiological mechanism. Pain, variable effort, learning and noise can generate it. No extra measurements are needed.

The baseline includes age, age², BMI, BMI², sum of bilateral maxima, its square, sex-by-strength interaction, absolute peak asymmetry divided by s, mean signed first-to-third change divided by s, and the average within-hand sample SD/mean (CV). Sex, RIDRETH3 and five education categories are full one-hot columns. Race/ethnicity adjusts social/demographic differences; it is not interpreted as biological mechanism. This deliberately stronger baseline tests information beyond peak strength, asymmetry and ordinary repeatability. Predictors are standardized using training means/SDs only; ridge alpha=10 is fixed, intercept unpenalized. No feature, alpha, cutoff or model was selected by target performance. Predictions are continuous and unclipped. Full frozen coefficients/scales are exported.

Primary evaluation is unweighted target MSE reduction, baseline minus augmented model. Positive values favor D; success required the target conditional 95% interval to exclude zero in that direction. Primary unweighted fitting is a prediction study for the observed complete-case sample, not a design-consistent national association estimate. Weighted fitting and scoring use WTMEC2YR as a sensitivity analysis; fitting weights are normalized to training mean one. MSE, MAE and R² are reported on the same people for each paired comparison. Discovery fivefold OOF assessment holds out whole masked PSUs (`GroupKFold`), and all scaling is fitted within folds. It is internal assessment, not the second-dataset validation.

Uncertainty uses resampling of PSUs within each masked stratum, retaining all rows in selected units and allowing duplicate selections. The conditional bootstrap resamples target units only (500 replicates); joint uncertainty resamples training and target units and refits both models (300). Percentile intervals describe design-cluster sampling uncertainty approximately, not a guarantee of clinical transport. They do not account for all prior-project exploration or complete-case selection. The random seed is fixed at 20112013. Target data are never used for fitting or preprocessing estimates. Source has 31 analytic PSUs/14 strata; target 30/15.

## Results
DSST mean (SD) was 45.89 (17.42) in discovery and 46.88 (16.90) in confirmation. Median D was 0.0611 and 0.0628; its 90th percentile was 0.1747 and 0.1762.

| Evaluation | Baseline MSE | With D MSE | MSE reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Discovery PSU-held-out OOF | 148.6169 | 148.5134 | 0.1035 |
| Separate target sample, primary | 154.1557 | 153.8450 | 0.3107 |
| Examination-weighted fit and score | 153.8414 | 153.9441 | −0.1027 |
| Pain-free, standing-only | 150.7856 | 151.0758 | −0.2902 |
| Discovery-threshold upper 1% D trimming | 153.2270 | 153.0507 | 0.1762 |
| Baseline also includes all six raw readings | 155.4137 | 155.0803 | 0.3334 |

Primary target R² changed from 0.46011 to 0.46120, and MAE from 9.87003 to 9.86304 points. The MSE gain is approximately 0.20% of baseline MSE. Conditional 95% interval: **[−0.2627, 0.9357] score²**. Joint training/target interval: **[−0.3124, 1.0934]**. Neither establishes benefit, and no equivalence margin was tested.

Pain-free standing sensitivity required no pain in either hand (MGQ070=MGQ100=2) and standing (MGDSEAT=1), leaving 840 source/882 target adults; both models were refitted in the source subset. Trimming used only the source 99th percentile of D (0.34159), retaining 1,232/1,381. Adding raw readings to the already strong baseline is a model-family challenge, not proof of superiority: this larger baseline itself had worse MSE.

## Falsification and author checks
Shuffling D in training while leaving target D intact produced MSE gain −0.6389, not an automatic benefit from adding a column. Permuting training outcomes 100 times gave gain 95% range [−1.4375, 0.0801]; no replicate reached the observed gain. Because target outcomes stayed fixed and the null disrupts all covariate relationships, this is a diagnostic control, **not an inferential permutation p-value**. Sensitivities were specified before the main analysis; they do not replace the failed primary criterion.

Direct rowwise reconstruction of D exactly matched the vectorized formula, and hand-swap invariance passed in both samples. Calculated sums of hand-specific maxima exactly reproduced CDC's MGDCGSZ. IDs were unique; merging enforced one-to-one cardinality. An independently written *author* normal-equations solver reconstructed frozen ridge predictions within 1.5×10⁻¹³ points; direct score reconstruction matched reported MSE. This is an implementation cross-check, not independent review. A clean, offline rerun into a separate output directory produced byte-identical results, models, feature/prediction CSVs and bootstrap arrays. Saved SHA-256 comparison and logs document this.

## Scope, limitations and next test
No reliable incremental prediction was established for this fixed feature/model/outcome. The small point estimate should not motivate clinical screening, and null intervals do not establish absence of information. Three widely spaced contractions are not a validated fatigability protocol; D ignores middle-trial direction and may reflect measurement variability. DSST depends on visual and manual processing as well as cognition, so a shared motor component is a plausible alternative explanation. Pain/standing sensitivity was adverse. Vision, neurological disease, arthritis, occupation, language, examiner effects, socioeconomic circumstances and selection could confound residual patterns. Education and race categories do not eliminate those effects. Cross-sectional predictions do not establish future decline, causality, central coordination, or treatment response. Population weights reverse the nominal gain; generalization beyond this tested sample is especially uncertain. The modest dataset and limited model family leave nonlinear or task-specific alternatives open.

A future independently recruited clinical study could prespecify the feature, standardize force/rest and effort, measure vision, dexterity and neurological status, and compare incremental prediction of prospective cognitive change against peak grip and baseline cognition. Simultaneous dynamometry could distinguish repeatability from central coordination. These are proposals only; no clinical or physical experiments were performed.
